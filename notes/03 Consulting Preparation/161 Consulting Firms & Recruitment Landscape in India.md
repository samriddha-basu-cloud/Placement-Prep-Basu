## 1. The Consulting Landscape: Firm Types
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Strategy, Big 4, operations/turnaround, specialist and boutique firms

### Definition
Management consulting sells advice and implementation support on strategy, operations, organisation, technology, and transactions. Firms cluster into types that differ in work, client level, pay and recruitment:

| Type | Examples | Typical work | Client level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategy houses (MBB)** | McKinsey, BCG, Bain | Corporate and business strategy, transformation, operations improvement, M&A, increasingly digital and AI | CEO and board, large corporates and PE |
| **Big 4 advisory** | Deloitte (including Monitor Deloitte), EY (EY-Parthenon), PwC (Strategy&), KPMG | Strategy, risk, deals, technology, operations, and implementation at scale | CXOs; broad mid-to-large market |
| **Large integrators** | Accenture (including its strategy practice) | Strategy plus large technology and operations transformation | CXOs, large programmes |
| **Operations and performance specialists** | Kearney, Alvarez & Marsal | Supply chain, procurement, manufacturing, cost, turnaround and restructuring | COO, CPO, CFO, PE portfolio |
| **Sector or function specialists** | ZS (pharma and healthcare, commercial analytics), Oliver Wyman (financial services, risk), L.E.K. (strategy, M&A, life sciences), Arthur D. Little (technology, innovation, telecom, industry) | Deep-vertical advice | Function heads, CXOs |
| **Boutiques and in-house** | Regional strategy boutiques, corporate strategy cells, PE value-creation teams | Narrow topics or internal projects | Varies |

Context from public sources (indicative): McKinsey about 38,000 employees (2025), BCG about 33,500 (2026) and revenue of about $14.4 billion (2025), Bain about 22,000 (2025) with an India office since 2006, Kearney 5,300+, Oliver Wyman about 7,000 (2025), L.E.K. about 2,100 (2023), Arthur D. Little about 1,500, ZS over 13,000 (all Wikipedia, which may lag). Founding years: ADL 1886, Kearney 1926, ZS 1983, L.E.K. 1983, Alvarez & Marsal 1983, Oliver Wyman 1984.

### Example
A client's problem decides the firm type: a conglomerate board asking "should we split our consumer and industrial businesses?" goes to a strategy house; a promoter asking "our plant network is costing 8% more than competitors, fix it" may go to an operations specialist; an ERP and supply-planning rollout across 14 plants goes to a Big 4 firm or an integrator. See [[024 Consulting Frameworks]] for the frameworks used across them.

### In the news
See news box. IIM Calcutta's list of 2026 recruiters (BCG, Bain, McKinsey, EY-Parthenon, KPMG, Kearney, Alvarez & Marsal, Monitor Deloitte, Accenture Strategy) is a sample of every type in the table at one campus.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why do you want to join us rather than a competitor?" or "What kind of consulting work do you want to do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Knows the firm's positioning, practices and a recent piece of work or publication
> - Distinguishes strategy, operations and implementation consulting
> - Links own background (for example an operations internship) to the practice
> - Avoids generic "learning curve" answers; see [[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]

## 2. Strategy Houses: McKinsey, BCG and Bain
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ CEO agenda; hypothesis-driven; highest bar; what each house is known for

### Definition
The three firms advise on corporate strategy and large transformations, with practices in operations, digital, analytics, sustainability and PE. Common features: small teams of 3–6, engagements of weeks to months, intense hypothesis-driven problem solving, a stringent up-or-out culture (McKinsey formalised "up or out" in 1951 according to Wikipedia), and recruiting from top MBA programmes.

Differences in interview culture, according to public descriptions:
- **McKinsey:** cases tend to be **interviewer-led** with a fixed script; the **Personal Experience Interview (PEI)** forms a large part of each round; Solve (gamified assessment) before interviews; the official page lists PEI, a problem-solving case and, for some roles, an expertise interview.
- **BCG:** historically **candidate-led** cases that resemble real client work; fit interview is open in format; an online case with a chatbot is used by some offices.
- **Bain:** cases historically candidate-led, now reported as interviewer-led with a scripted behavioural interview; online tests before interviews.

These are third-party descriptions and change by year and office; confirm with your placement committee and the firm's own pages. Operations-relevant practices: McKinsey Operations, BCG Operations / Supply chain, Bain Performance Improvement (a pattern: procurement, manufacturing, supply-chain and cost work is a large share of engagements). Prepare using [[025 Case Interview — Profitability]], [[026 Case Interview — Operations Cases]], [[027 Case Interview — Market Entry]] and [[159 Case Interview - M&A & Due Diligence]].

### Example
The same prompt "profits are down" is handled differently: in an interviewer-led case you answer the question asked ("what is the revenue trend by segment?") and keep the structure in your head; in a candidate-led case you propose the structure, choose the next branch and drive to a recommendation. Practise both.

### In the news
See news box. McKinsey's official page confirms the Solve, PEI and problem-solving structure; IIM Ahmedabad's 2026 report names BCG, McKinsey and Bain as the leading consulting recruiters (in that order).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Walk me through a time you led a team through a difficult situation" (PEI-style) or a business case, often in the same 45–60 minute round.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Structured, hypothesis-led case work with quick, accurate mental maths
> - A specific, personal PEI story: your actions, the impact and what you learned
> - Adaptability to interviewer-led and candidate-led styles
> - Genuine interest in the firm's work, shown through specific knowledge

## 3. Big 4 Advisory and Accenture
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Strategy and deals plus technology and operations implementation; scale; many entry points

### Definition
The Big Four (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG) grew from audit and tax into broad advisory. Wikipedia notes that they entered strategy consulting through acquisitions since about 2010; the brands you will see on Indian campuses include **Monitor Deloitte** (strategy), **EY-Parthenon** (strategy and transactions), **PwC Strategy&** and KPMG's strategy and operations practices. Accenture is a large integrator with strategy and operations consulting alongside technology and outsourcing. Features:

- **Breadth and scale:** strategy, risk, finance, supply chain, technology, human capital, deals; large programmes (ERP, PMO, transformation).
- **Implementation depth:** advice plus delivery; a heavier ratio of project execution to pure strategy.
- **Entry points:** MBA campuses, laterals, and bigger graduate pools; roles differ by service line.
- **Culture:** more varied, with different cultures by service line and office; career paths run on grade ladders with promotions on a more regular schedule than up-or-out.
- **What they value:** client-ready analysis, structured problem solving, Excel and PowerPoint speed, and sector or technology knowledge (for example SAP, procurement or supply-chain domain).

For supply-chain candidates the Big 4 and Accenture are the largest employers of operations consultants: network design, S&OP and planning transformation, procurement transformation, and ERP programmes ([[174 Supply Chain Technology Landscape - Planning, Execution & Procure Tech]], [[198 SAP IBP, APO & Demand-Driven Planning]]).

### Example
A typical "operations strategy" project at a Big 4 firm: diagnose a manufacturer's order-to-cash and plan-to-produce processes, size ₹120 crore of working-capital and cost opportunity, design a target operating model, and run a transformation office for 18 months. The interview version is a case on cost reduction or supply-chain redesign ([[160 Case Interview - Cost Reduction, Turnaround & Pricing]]).

### In the news
See news box. Statista's report of more than 1.5 million Big Four employees worldwide in 2025 (Deloitte 473,100) shows the scale of the segment, and EY-Parthenon, KPMG and Monitor Deloitte all appear among the 2026 recruiters at IIM Calcutta.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why this firm and not an MBB firm?" or "Tell us about a time you delivered a project with tight deadlines."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Specific interest in the service line and the type of work (implementation, technology, supply chain)
> - Evidence of structure, delivery and stakeholder management
> - Realistic expectations of the work and the career ladder
> - Avoids implying the firm is a "second choice"

## 4. Operations, Turnaround and Specialist Firms
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Kearney, Alvarez & Marsal, ZS, Oliver Wyman, L.E.K., Arthur D. Little

### Definition
- **Kearney (founded 1926):** operations-led strategy; supply chain, procurement and manufacturing are signature practices; Wikipedia notes acquisitions in supply-chain planning (Optano 2022, IMTEK 2025) and a Bangalore office through the Cervello acquisition (2019).
- **Alvarez & Marsal (1983):** performance improvement, turnaround management, restructuring and transaction advisory; "hands-on" and often embedded in the client during crises ([[160 Case Interview - Cost Reduction, Turnaround & Pricing]]).
- **ZS Associates (1983):** sales and marketing effectiveness and analytics, strongly in pharma, healthcare and life sciences; recruits analytics-oriented candidates.
- **Oliver Wyman (1984, part of Marsh McLennan):** financial services, risk, plus industrial and transport practices.
- **L.E.K. Consulting (1983):** corporate strategy, M&A, and commercial due diligence for PE; life sciences and consumer.
- **Arthur D. Little (1886):** technology, innovation, telecom, energy and industry strategy; sometimes called the oldest consulting firm.

What they test: operations-led firms emphasise **numbers, structure and practicality** (cost, capacity, supply-chain mechanics); ZS emphasises **analytics, healthcare commercial cases and data interpretation** ([[029 Data Interpretation & Charts]]); A&M emphasises **cash, turnaround and hands-on judgment**; strategy specialists emphasise **market and due-diligence cases** ([[159 Case Interview - M&A & Due Diligence]]).

### Example
Mapping a supply-chain background to firms: Kearney (procurement, supply-chain strategy), A&M (supply-chain restructuring, working-capital release in turnarounds), ZS (pharma supply and commercial operations), ADL (innovation and industrial operations), MBB operations practices (manufacturing and procurement transformation). In your tailored answer name two practices and one type of project for each target firm.

### In the news
See news box. Kearney, Alvarez & Marsal and others appear in the 2026 IIM Calcutta recruiter list: operations and performance-improvement firms are part of the consulting hiring mix at top campuses, not only MBB.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why Kearney (or A&M, or ZS) rather than a strategy firm?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Specific knowledge of the firm's signature practice and projects
> - Matches own skills (operations, analytics, finance) to the practice
> - Shows interest in hands-on, implementation-oriented problem solving
> - Prepared for the firm's typical case types

## 5. Typical Interview Formats and Rounds
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Resume shortlist → assessment → case rounds → fit/PEI → partner round

### Definition
A generic pipeline (details vary by firm, office, year and whether it is a campus, summer or lateral process):

1. **Resume shortlist:** academics, work experience, extracurriculars and leadership; some campuses use a firm-specific shortlisting committee or a "case-based" shortlisting.
2. **Pre-interview assessment:** online tests such as McKinsey's Solve, Bain's online tests or BCG's online case (a chatbot-based case tool was reported by CaseCoach); some firms use aptitude, written or group exercises.
3. **First-round interviews:** usually two interviews with managers or senior consultants, each combining a **case** (25–40 minutes) and **fit/behavioural** (10–20 minutes).
4. **Final round:** with partners; a mix of cases, fit and sometimes a "hot-seat" presentation; decisions are often announced the same day on campus.
5. **Offer and PPI/PPO:** summer-internship performance can convert into a pre-placement offer (PPO).

Case flavours: **profitability, market entry, M&A, operations, growth, pricing, public sector**, plus **market-sizing** ([[028 Guesstimates & Market Sizing]]), **data interpretation** ([[029 Data Interpretation & Charts]]) and **written cases**. At Big 4 and operations firms, group discussion (GD), written analysis and a "case presentation" are more common than at MBB.

### Example
A candidate with five shortlists in a campus week might see: two first-round interviews (one case, one fit), then a final partner round of 45 minutes with one 30-minute case and 15 minutes of fit. Prepare for all three in the same week: the case, the story bank ([[049 STAR Stories — Leadership & Conflict]]) and the "why this firm" answer.

### In the news
See news box. McKinsey's official description (Solve, PEI, problem-solving interview, optional expertise interview) matches the generic pipeline; reports of Bain's move to interviewer-led cases and a 45-minute scripted behavioural interview show formats changing between years.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you prepare for a consulting interview process in three weeks?" (a common placement-committee or mentor question)

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Describes the full pipeline, not only the case round
> - Allocates time to cases, maths, fit stories and firm research
> - Prepares for both candidate-led and interviewer-led cases
> - Plans mock interviews with feedback and tracks error patterns

## 6. What Each Firm Tests: Competency Map
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Structuring, quant, synthesis, communication, fit, leadership, domain

### Definition
All consulting interviews test the same core skills, weighted differently:

| Competency | What the interviewer looks for | How to show it |
|---|---|---|
| **Structuring** | MECE, hypothesis-led structure ([[024 Consulting Frameworks]]) | A tailored issue tree in 60 seconds, not a memorised framework |
| **Quantitative reasoning** | Accuracy, speed, sense-checks | Mental maths, rounding, order-of-magnitude, chart reading ([[029 Data Interpretation & Charts]]) |
| **Business judgment** | Insight and prioritisation | Hypotheses with a "so what"; focuses on the biggest driver |
| **Synthesis and communication** | Answer first, structured, concise | Pyramid Principle delivery ([[162 Structured Communication - SCQA, Storylines & Case Delivery]]) |
| **Creativity** | Ideas beyond the obvious | Lists that are MECE and also include non-standard options |
| **Personal impact / fit** | Leadership, drive, teamwork, humility | Specific stories with your actions and outcomes ([[049 STAR Stories — Leadership & Conflict]], [[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]) |
| **Coachability** | Takes feedback and adjusts | Incorporates the interviewer's hints without defensiveness |
| **Domain and tech** (specialist and Big 4) | Knowledge of the function or sector | Pharma commercial, SAP and supply-chain, finance basics |

Emphasis by firm type (typical, not absolute): MBB (structuring, insight, PEI/fit and communication weighted heavily); Big 4 and integrators (structure plus practicality and delivery); operations specialists (numbers and practical operations judgment); ZS (analytics and data interpretation); A&M (cash, finance and turnaround logic).

### Example
Scoring yourself after a mock: structuring 4/5, maths 3/5 (two arithmetic slips), synthesis 3/5 (too long), fit 4/5. The plan: two weeks of daily maths drills and a 30-second summary at the end of every case, not another 10 cases of the same type.

### In the news
See news box. The shift of McKinsey's PEI to a four-dimension framework (Connection, Drive, Leadership, Growth, as reported by CaseStar) is a reminder that "fit" has named dimensions to prepare for, not just a general conversation.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What do you think makes a good consultant?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Structured thinking, analytical rigour, communication, and teamwork
> - One example for each from your own experience
> - Awareness of client impact (the work must be implementable)
> - Honest self-assessment of one area to improve

## 7. Career Paths: Analyst to Partner
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Ladder, up-or-out vs grade bands, MBA entry level, partner track, exits

### Definition
Common ladder at strategy firms (names vary): **Business Analyst / Analyst → Associate / Consultant (MBA entry level) → Engagement Manager / Project Leader / Case Team Leader → Associate Partner / Principal → Partner / Director**. MBA hires typically join at the Associate/Consultant level; undergraduate hires join as analysts. Promotion steps are typically 2–3 years apart in early years and slow down above manager. Many firms operate an **up-or-out** system: McKinsey's, established in 1951 according to Wikipedia, had roughly one-fifth of consultants leaving annually by 1997 (a historical statistic). Big 4 firms usually use **grade bands** (for example Consultant → Senior Consultant → Manager → Senior Manager → Director/Partner), with a more regular promotion cycle, though partner admission is competitive. Operations and turnaround specialists such as A&M use their own ladders (check the firm's careers page), typically with a strong emphasis on client delivery and revenue at senior levels.

Typical work pattern: a team of 3–6 per engagement, travel to client sites, weekly steering committees, long hours, and a leverage model where one partner oversees several managers and teams. Pay structures are fixed salary plus performance bonus, varying by firm; on campuses the packages quoted publicly are the best indicator (the IIM Calcutta 2026 consulting average of about ₹40 lakh is from Careers360).

### Example
Illustrative 7-year path at a strategy firm: MBA hire (Associate) → Engagement Manager after 2–3 years → Associate Partner after 3–5 more, or an exit to industry strategy, private equity, a start-up or a business-unit role at the 2–4 year mark.

### In the news
See news box. IIM Calcutta's consulting average (₹40 lakh), median (₹41 lakh) and highest (₹110 lakh) package figures (approximate, Careers360) give a campus-level anchor for entry-level pay; do not quote them as firm-specific.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Where do you see yourself in five years?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Shows commitment to growing at the firm (not an exit-first story)
> - Realistic about the ladder and the learning curve
> - Links to a specific practice or skill (for example operations transformation leader)
> - Mentions the value of consulting experience without being cynical

## 8. Consulting vs Industry: Choosing a Path
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Breadth vs depth; pace; ownership; exit options; work-life; fit with goals

### Definition
| Dimension | Consulting | Industry (operations, product, strategy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Learning** | Fast, broad across industries; many problems in few years | Deeper in one business; learn to run operations and P&L |
| **Ownership** | Advise; implementation sits with the client (except in implementation-heavy firms) | Own execution and results |
| **Pace and hours** | Intense, project-based, travel | Variable by company; more stable but can be intense |
| **Impact horizon** | Months per project | Years; see outcomes |
| **Exit options** | Strong: industry strategy, PE, start-ups, general management | Depends on the function; moves are within the sector |
| **Compensation** | High at entry; steep ladder | Lower at entry in many cases; ESOPs and long-term upside in some |
| **Risk** | Up-or-out at some firms; utilisation pressure | Layoffs, business cycles |

For a supply-chain MBA, industry paths (operations leader, planning head, procurement head, product and program management; see [[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]) pair well with consulting as a **2–4 year accelerator**: the stock reasons candidates give are "learn fast", "breadth" and "impact"; stronger answers explain what **specific capability** the person wants to build, and what they would do with it. Consider the **hybrid** options: operations practices inside Big 4 and specialist firms, internal strategy teams, and consulting-like roles in product and project management ([[037 PM Fundamentals & Lifecycle]]).

### Example
A strong "why consulting" answer for an operations candidate: "At my internship I led a plant-level project that cut changeover time by 18%; I enjoyed the problem-solving but wanted to see how it works across industries. Consulting will give me exposure to five or six manufacturing and supply-chain problems in two years, which I plan to take into an operations leadership role." The structure is: evidence, motive, plan.

### In the news
See news box. At IIM Calcutta, 38% of 2026 offers were consulting, so most of the batch chooses it; interviewers have heard "learning curve" many times, so your reason must be specific.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why consulting, when your background is in operations?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A specific, evidence-backed motive (not "learning curve" alone)
> - What you bring from operations (domain knowledge, hands-on experience)
> - A credible plan for the first 2–5 years
> - Awareness of the trade-offs (travel, ownership) and why you accept them

## 9. The MBA Campus Placement Process
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Registration, CV shortlist, GD/PI, case rounds, slots and clusters, summers and PPOs

### Definition
Campus processes differ by institute; the typical flow for Indian B-schools (and what SIOM-type campuses follow in outline) is:

1. **Preparation:** CV and LinkedIn polish ([[179 Resume, LinkedIn & Cold Outreach for Placements]]), case clubs, mock interviews, alumni connects, domain certifications.
2. **Registration and company presentations:** students register for roles or clusters; firms present and release job descriptions and cut-offs.
3. **Shortlisting:** CV-based, sometimes with a short test; firms release shortlists to the placement committee. Preferences (consulting vs other sectors) may be declared.
4. **Process day or week:** GD and/or aptitude (more common for Big 4 and operations firms; see [[177 Group Discussions & Extempore for MBA Placements]]), then case and fit interviews, and an HR round.
5. **Slots and clusters:** top schools use slot or cluster systems (firms placed into groups, with a limit on how many offers one student can hold at each stage). Lateral (experienced-hire) phases may come first, as with IIM Ahmedabad's January 2026 lateral phase.
6. **Offers and compensation:** offers, packages and joining terms ([[178 HR Round, Offers & Salary Negotiation in India]]).
7. **Summer internship and PPO:** the summer internship (SIP) can convert into a pre-placement offer (PPO) at many firms.

What a placement committee wants from you: clarity of target roles, a shortlist-worthy CV with quantified impact, evidence of case practice, and professional conduct. **Do not rely on any figure** in this note for your campus: ask your placement office for the firm list, cut-offs and process, which change every year.

### Example
An 8-week plan before a consulting cluster: weeks 1–2 CV and structure basics (frameworks, issue trees); weeks 3–5 two mock cases a day on alternate days with feedback; weeks 6–7 firm research, fit stories, mental maths; week 8 full mock interviews with the same format as the firm (see sub-topic 11).

### In the news
See news box. IIM Ahmedabad's placement process began with a lateral phase in January 2026 and reached 100% placement across 26 cohorts; IIM Calcutta reports 458 students placed against 542 offers (offers per student above 1), so many students choose among several offers; check how your own campus counts offers.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "You have shortlists from a strategy firm, a Big 4 and an operations consultancy. How do you prepare, and how will you choose?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A clear criteria (work, learning, team, location, compensation)
> - A prepared case and fit plan for each firm type
> - Understanding of the slot or cluster rules and timing
> - Respect for process and the placement committee's guidelines

## 10. Operations and Supply-Chain Consulting Practices
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ What the work is; engagement types; skills and case types; how to position a supply-chain MBA

### Definition
Operations and supply-chain consulting solves cost, service, speed and resilience problems. Typical engagement types:

| Engagement | What the team does | Related notes |

| **Planning and S&OP/IBP** | Demand and supply planning process design, tool selection | [[120 Integrated Business Planning (IBP) & S&OP Maturity]], [[004 Demand Forecasting & Planning]] |
| **Manufacturing and lean** | Productivity, OEE, quality, cost-out | [[007 Lean Manufacturing]], [[018 Capacity Management & OEE]] |

| **Resilience and risk** | Disruption diagnostics, dual-sourcing, scenario planning | [[015 Supply Chain Risk & Resilience]], [[141 Supply Chain Disruption Case Library (2011-2026)]] |
| **Technology and ERP** | Selection, design, implementation, PMO | [[174 Supply Chain Technology Landscape - Planning, Execution & Procure Tech]], [[200 SAP S-4HANA Migration, Data Migration & Testing]] |
| **Policy and India context** | PLI, Gati Shakti, logistics-cost and localisation programmes | [[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]] |

**Skills and case types:** operations cases (capacity, throughput, bottlenecks: [[026 Case Interview — Operations Cases]]), cost reduction and turnaround ([[160 Case Interview - Cost Reduction, Turnaround & Pricing]]), profitability, market entry, and sizing. ## 11. Preparation Plan, Resources and Common Mistakes
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Case volume, drills, feedback loop, firm research, stories; avoid memorised frameworks

### Definition
**Preparation components:**
- **Case practice:** 25–40 live cases with a partner over 6–8 weeks, mixed types, with feedback on structure, maths, synthesis and delivery; record some sessions.
- **Maths drills:** daily 10 minutes of percentages, growth rates, break-even, margin and unit economics; chart reading ([[029 Data Interpretation & Charts]]).
- **Frameworks as tools:** the structures in [[024 Consulting Frameworks]]; always tailor.
- **Market sizing:** [[028 Guesstimates & Market Sizing]], with the data in [[103 Key India Data Points to Memorize]].
- **Fit stories:** 6–8 stories mapped to competencies ([[049 STAR Stories — Leadership & Conflict]], [[051 Internship Learnings & Projects]], [[048 Tell Me About Yourself]]).
- **Firm research:** practices, recent publications, projects and values; for each target firm prepare a specific "why".
- **Business awareness:** [[180 Current Affairs & Economy Briefing for MBA Interviews (2025-26)]].
- **Communication:** [[162 Structured Communication - SCQA, Storylines & Case Delivery]].

**Common mistakes:** memorised frameworks recited without tailoring; ignoring the clarifying questions; losing the thread of the case; maths errors without a sense-check; a long, chronological summary instead of an answer; vague fit stories with "we" and no personal action; and poor energy in virtual rounds.

### Example
A weekly rhythm during the six-week run-up: Mon/Wed/Fri one live case each (alternating types), Tue/Thu maths and sizing, Sat one full mock interview with a fit segment, Sun review of the error log. After each case log three items: what went well, one structural error, one maths or communication error; track whether error types fall over time.

### In the news
See news box. As interview formats evolve (reported changes at McKinsey and Bain), the safest preparation covers both interviewer-led and candidate-led cases and a rigorous personal-experience story bank.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many cases have you practised, and what did you learn from them?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Gives a realistic count and the variety of cases
> - Names specific lessons (structuring, maths, synthesis)
> - Shows a feedback loop and improvement
> - Avoids implying the answer is a memorised framework

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: Consulting Economics, Leverage and the AI Shift
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
Firms make money from **fees per engagement** driven by **leverage, utilisation and realisation**:

$$\text{Revenue per professional}=\text{Billable days}\times\text{Daily rate}\times\text{Realisation}$$

- **Leverage (pyramid):** the ratio of junior staff to partners. A higher ratio lowers cost per unit of work; strategy firms use small, senior-heavy teams and premium pricing, while Big 4 and integrators use larger leveraged teams and volume.
- **Utilisation:** share of time that is billable; lower when waiting on staffing.
- **Realisation:** actual fees collected relative to the rate card.
- **Pricing:** fixed fee, time and materials, or outcome-linked/risk-sharing fees; a fixed fee shifts overrun risk to the firm.
- **Origination (selling):** partners must win work; client relationships drive revenue and promotion at senior levels.

**AI shift:** generative AI automates research, synthesis and slide production, raising questions on junior leverage and pricing; the sector summary on Wikipedia reports about 5.5% global consulting revenue growth in 2025, partly attributed to AI-adoption demand. Expect interviewers to probe how AI changes your own work and the client's operations ([[166 AI Product Management - LLM Products, Evals & Economics]], [[219 NLP, Embeddings & LLM Applications for Analysts]]).

### Example
Illustrative: a firm has 60 consultants at ₹4 lakh per consultant-week rate card; 40 billable weeks a year at 75% realisation. Revenue per consultant = 40 × 4 × 0.75 = ₹120 lakh (₹1.2 crore); for 60 consultants = ₹72 crore. If realisation drops to 65% (discounting), revenue falls to ₹62.4 crore (−13.3%): a 10-point realisation loss costs about ₹9.6 crore. (Illustrative figures, not any firm's actual rates.)

### In the news
See news box. Industry revenue growth of about 5.5% in 2025 and the large Big Four workforce (over 1.5 million) show a sector still scaling while changing how it uses junior capacity.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How do you think AI will change consulting, and what does that mean for how you build your career?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Understands the leverage and pricing model and where AI changes it
> - Separates tasks that get automated from the judgment and relationship work that remains
> - Gives a concrete example of using AI tools responsibly in analysis
> - Shows a plan to build differentiated skills (domain depth, analytics, client skills)

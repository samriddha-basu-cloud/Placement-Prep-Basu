## 1. Logistics Cost in India: How Much, and Why Estimates Differ
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 7.97% of GDP (FY24), older 13-14% claim, definitions, components

### Definition
**Logistics cost as a share of GDP** is the total spend on transport, warehousing, inventory carrying, packaging, handling and administration divided by GDP. India's figure is **contested because of method**: the older, widely quoted figure was **about 13-14% of GDP** (the Transport in India overview still notes 14% as a historic claim), while recent government-linked estimates are far lower. The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports **7.97% of GDP in FY24, down from 8.84% in FY23** ([Economic Survey ch. 8](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap08.pdf)). A major reason for the gap is **scope and weights**: whether in-house (own-account) transport, inventory carrying cost and sector-wise logistics intensity are counted, and whether the base is GDP or the value of goods. When quoting, always state the **source, year and definition**; international comparisons are only meaningful if definitions align.

Composition (conceptual): transport is the largest share, then warehousing, inventory carrying and handling; road carries the dominant share of freight, with rail and coastal shipping far smaller than in China or the EU ([[009 Logistics & Distribution]], [[125 Transportation Management Deep Dive]]). Per-km cost differences in the Wikipedia overview: rail about ₹1.96/km, waterways ₹2.30/km, road ₹3.78/km. Another headline metric is the World Bank **Logistics Performance Index (LPI)**: in 2023 Singapore led with a 4.3 score; India's position has varied between editions, so check the latest edition before quoting.

### Example
Illustrative arithmetic with an assumed GDP of ₹330 lakh crore: each **1 percentage point of GDP is about ₹3.3 lakh crore**; the reported fall from 8.84% to 7.97% is $8.84 - 7.97 = 0.87$ points, a relative fall of $0.87/8.84 = 9.8\%$, worth about ₹2.9 lakh crore a year at that GDP. For a company with ₹2,000 crore of sales and logistics cost at 12% of sales (₹240 crore), cutting 1.5 points through consolidation and rail mix saves $0.015 \times 2000 = ₹30$ crore: the same logic applied at firm level. Note that *national* ratios and *firm* ratios differ in definition: a firm's logistics cost is measured against sales.

### In the news
See news box for the 7.97% figure and the progress of corridors that feed it.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Is India's logistics cost 14% or 8% of GDP, and why does it matter?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Names both figures, source and year, and explains that scope and method explain the gap
> - Why it matters: input to competitiveness of exports, MSME margins and prices
> - Components and the modal-mix issue (road dependence)
> - Caution against comparing with other countries' ratios without checking definitions

## 2. National Logistics Policy 2022, ULIP and LEADS
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Cost reduction, LPI top 25 by 2030, data-driven decisions, platform integration

### Definition
The **National Logistics Policy (NLP)**, launched in **September 2022**, aims for an "integrated, cost-efficient, resilient, sustainable and trusted logistics ecosystem". DPIIT's materials state targets of **reducing logistics cost**, ranking among the **top 25 countries in the LPI by 2030**, and building **data-driven decision support**, with the **Unified Logistics Interface Platform (ULIP)** and a **Comprehensive Logistics Action Plan** as delivery mechanisms ([DPIIT](https://dpiit.gov.in/logistics-division/national-logistics-policy), [logistics.gov.in](https://logistics.gov.in/)). Date detail and the full list of action areas (such as digital freight and stakeholder grievance mechanisms) are not re-verified in this note: confirm in the policy document.

**ULIP.** A digital platform that connects government logistics data systems through APIs so that firms and logistics providers can track, plan and verify (for example rail, road, port, customs, vehicle and driver data) from one interface; the Economic Survey 2025-26 credits ULIP and LEADS with "actively improving logistics efficiency" ([ch. 9](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap09.pdf)). **LEADS (Logistics Ease Across Different States)** is an annual DPIIT index that benchmarks states on logistics infrastructure, services and operating environment; **edition years, categories and rankings are not verified here**, so check the latest edition. See also [[009 Logistics & Distribution]] (India's logistics ecosystem), [[174 Supply Chain Technology Landscape - Planning, Execution & Procure Tech]], [[016 Digital Supply Chain & Industry 4.0]].

### Example
Value of data integration (illustrative). A shipper uses 4 separate systems to verify a truck, driver, consignment and port status, taking 25 minutes per load; with an API platform it takes 5 minutes. For 200 loads a day, time saved $= 200 \times 20 = 4{,}000$ minutes $= 66.7$ hours a day, or about 8 full-time staff at 8 hours. At ₹800 a day per person the saving is about ₹6,400 a day ($= 8 \times 800$), roughly ₹19 lakh a year over 300 days; the larger gains are faster truck turnaround and fewer disputes. Quantified benefits like this are how NLP's "ease" is justified at firm level.

### In the news
See news box: the Economic Survey ties falling logistics cost partly to digitised, multimodal planning.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What is the National Logistics Policy and what would you expect it to change for a manufacturer?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Objectives (cost, LPI, data-driven) with the correct caution on targets and dates
> - ULIP as the data layer and LEADS as state benchmarking
> - A manufacturer-level effect: lower dwell time, better visibility, more predictable lead times
> - Honest on implementation gaps (state-level adoption, last mile, MSME digitisation)

## 3. PM Gati Shakti
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ GIS-based master plan, multimodal planning, 434 rail projects, ₹100 lakh crore framing

### Definition
**PM Gati Shakti** was announced on 15 August 2021, launched on **13 October 2021** and approved by the CCEA on 21 October 2021. It is a **National Master Plan** that places infrastructure layers (rail, road, ports, airports, waterways, industrial and economic zones, utilities) on a **GIS-based digital platform** so that ministries plan jointly and avoid conflicts such as a road cutting an unplanned rail line. It is often described as a plan worth around ₹100 lakh crore (about $1.2 trillion per Wikipedia). The Economic Survey says it has "institutionalised multimodal, GIS-enabled planning" and that **434 railway projects worth ₹11.17 lakh crore** were mapped across three economic corridors, with **122 sanctioned and 198 under appraisal** ([Economic Survey ch. 9](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap09.pdf); [Wikipedia: PM Gati Shakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM_Gati_Shakti)). The Network Planning Group evaluates projects against the master plan. Related: [[133 Food, Agri & Perishables Supply Chain - India]] and [[019 Facility Layout & Location]] for site selection logic.

### Example
Average size of mapped rail projects $= ₹11.17 \text{ lakh crore}/434 \approx ₹2{,}574$ crore per project. Sanctioned share by count $= 122/434 = 28\%$, under appraisal $198/434 = 46\%$, remainder 26% (pipeline). For a firm choosing a plant site, the same logic is a **layered site score**: distance to rail terminal, DFC access, port, power, labour and land, with weights; Gati Shakti makes these layers visible for government and private planners.

### In the news
See news box for the 434 projects and the link to DFC completion.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How does PM Gati Shakti help an industrial site decision?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Layered GIS planning, joint ministry decisions, avoiding duplication
> - Site-selection factors and how connectivity changes inbound/outbound cost
> - Risks: delays, land acquisition, last-mile connectors
> - Measures: project completion rate, cost overrun, dwell-time reductions

## 4. Freight Infrastructure: DFCs, Sagarmala, Bharatmala and Waterways
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 1,337 km EDFC, 1,504 km WDFC, 839 Sagarmala projects, 83,677 km Bharatmala

### Definition
**Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs).** The **Eastern DFC** (Ludhiana to Dankuni) is **1,337 km** and was completed by February 2024; the **Western DFC** (Dadri to JNPT, Mumbai) is **1,504 km**. Freight trains on the DFCs averaged **241 a day in FY24 and 403 a day in FY25**; reported average speeds are about 99 km/h (EDFC) and 89.5 km/h (WDFC); axle load is 32.5 tonnes against 25 tonnes on standard tracks ([Wikipedia: DFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedicated_freight_corridors_in_India)). The Economic Survey reports 96.4% of the 2,843 km network commissioned by October 2025.

**Sagarmala** (concept approved 25 March 2015): **839 projects worth about ₹5.79 lakh crore** to 2035, of which 241 projects (₹1.22 lakh crore) are completed, 234 (₹1.8 lakh crore) under implementation and 364 (₹2.78 lakh crore) in development; Sagarmala Development Company Ltd set up in 2016 ([Wikipedia: Sagarmala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagarmala_Programme)).

**Bharatmala Pariyojana** (launched 31 July 2015): **83,677 km** of roads, total estimated cost **₹10.63 trillion**, Phase-I 34,800 km, 44 economic corridors, aiming to move freight share on national highways from 40% to 80% ([Wikipedia: Bharatmala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatmala)). Waterways remain a small share; the Budget 2026-27 plans 20 new National Waterways and a coastal-cargo modal-share target of 12% by 2047 (see news box). See [[125 Transportation Management Deep Dive]], [[126 International Trade Documentation, Customs & Trade Finance]].

### Example
Trains per day rose from 241 to 403: $403/241 = 1.67$, a **67% increase**. Capacity logic: a WDFC double-stack container train carries far more than a conventional one; if one train displaces 70 trucks (illustrative) then 403 trains a day displace about 28,000 trucks a day (assumption-based, not a reported figure). Sagarmala progress by count $= 241/839 = 28.7\%$, by value $= 1.22/5.79 = 21.1\%$: completed projects are smaller than average, which is typical because big port-linked projects take longer. Bharatmala average cost is ₹10.63 trillion (₹10.63 lakh crore) / 83,677 km ≈ ₹12.7 crore per km.

### In the news
See news box: the Eastern DFC is complete, 96.4% of the network is commissioned, and new corridors (Dankuni to Surat) were announced in Budget 2026-27.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "India has built DFCs; why has rail's share of freight not risen faster?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - First/last-mile connectivity, terminal capacity, rake availability and pricing
> - Door-to-door service quality: trucks offer flexibility, rail offers cost for bulk
> - Policy levers: multimodal logistics parks, container terminals, tariffs, time-tabled freight services
> - Metrics: modal share, dwell time, tonne-km cost

## 5. GST and Supply Chain Network Design
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 2017 launch, warehouse consolidation, e-way bill, GST 2.0 (September 2025)

### Definition
GST took effect on **1 July 2017**, replacing a patchwork of indirect taxes, and removed the **cascading Central Sales Tax and entry-barrier effects** that made firms keep a warehouse in almost every state. The **e-way bill** system tracks goods movement above a value threshold (₹50,000 as per the Wikipedia overview). After the **GST 2.0 reform effective 22 September 2025**, rates are mostly **0%/5%, 18% and 40%**, eliminating the 12% and 28% slabs ([Wikipedia: GST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_and_Services_Tax_(India))). For networks, the effects are: fewer, larger hubs; **hub-and-spoke** designs; faster interstate movement; more focus on input tax credit (ITC) and invoicing discipline; and inverted-duty structures that distort sourcing. See [[227 GST & Indirect Tax for Supply Chains]], [[113 Network Design & Facility Location Modelling]], [[130 FMCG & Retail Distribution - India Route-to-Market]].

### Example
Network consolidation (illustrative): a firm moves from **20 state warehouses to 8 regional hubs**. If demand is independent and each warehouse holds safety stock proportional to $\sigma$, total safety stock scales with $\sqrt{n}$: $\sqrt{8/20} = 0.632$, a **37% reduction** in safety stock. If safety stock was ₹100 crore, the release is about ₹37 crore. Against it, average outbound distance rises. The saved stock is worth 37 × 0.15 = ₹5.6 crore a year in carrying cost. If the outbound freight bill of ₹200 crore grows 2% (₹4 crore) the move pays by about ₹1.6 crore a year; if it grows 6% (₹12 crore) it does not. This is a **trade-off to model, not assume**.

### In the news
GST 2.0 slab changes (September 2025) alter landed-cost calculations and competitive pricing; see [[227 GST & Indirect Tax for Supply Chains]].

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How did GST change warehouse networks, and does it still make sense to have a warehouse in every state?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Pre-GST tax barriers vs post-GST consolidation logic
> - Square-root rule for safety stock and the freight/service trade-off
> - State-specific needs (perishables, service time promise, e-commerce speed)
> - Compliance: e-way bill, ITC reconciliation

## 6. Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) Schemes
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 14 sectors, ₹1.97 lakh crore, incremental sales, results to Sept 2025

### Definition
PLI schemes pay firms a **percentage of incremental sales** of products made in India, over a fixed period, subject to investment and sales thresholds. **Economic Survey 2025-26 totals (to September 2025):** 14 sectors; outlay **₹1.97 lakh crore**; **806 approved applications**; investment realised over ₹2.0 lakh crore; incremental production and sales over **₹18.70 lakh crore**; employment over **12.6 lakh** (direct and indirect); exports over ₹8.20 lakh crore; **₹23,946 crore of incentives disbursed across 12 sectors** ([ch. 8](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap08.pdf)).

Sector outlays as originally notified (approximate; these individual figures come from the government notifications recalled for this note and were **not re-fetched**, but they sum to ₹1,97,807 crore, consistent with the Survey's ₹1.97 lakh crore):

| Sector | Outlay (₹ crore) | Sector | Outlay (₹ crore) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile and specified electronics | 40,951 | Solar PV modules | 24,000 |
| Automobiles and auto components | 25,938 | Advanced Chemistry Cell batteries | 18,100 |
| IT hardware (second version) | 17,000 | Pharmaceuticals | 15,000 |
| Telecom and networking | 12,195 | Food processing | 10,900 |
| Textiles (MMF and technical) | 10,683 | Bulk drugs | 6,940 |
| Specialty steel | 6,322 | White goods (ACs, LEDs) | 6,238 |
| Medical devices | 3,420 | Drones and components | 120 |

Design features: incentive tied to **incremental** sales over a base year, domestic value addition norms, and in some schemes thresholds for investment. The Budget 2026-27 increase of the electronics components scheme outlay from ₹22,919 crore to ₹40,000 crore shows a shift from "assemble" to "components". Links: [[134 Electronics & Semiconductor Supply Chain]], [[131 Automotive Supply Chain - JIT, Tiers & EVs]], [[132 Pharma & Healthcare Supply Chain]], [[142 Company Supply Chain Case Library]] (Apple in India).

### Example
Using the Survey totals: incentive per rupee of sales $= 23{,}946/18{,}70{,}000 = 1.28\%$ (₹ crore both sides); incentive per job $= ₹23{,}946 \text{ crore}/12.6 \text{ lakh} \approx ₹1.9$ lakh; leverage of investment to incentive $= 2{,}00{,}000/23{,}946 = 8.4\times$ (investment realised divided by incentives paid so far). Caveat: these are cumulative, incentives lag sales, and "incremental" sales depend on the base year, so ratios are indicative. A company deciding to bid for PLI should compare incentive NPV with the capex and the risk of missing thresholds ([[224 Capital Budgeting for Operations - Capex, Lease vs Buy & Replacement]]).

### In the news
See news box for the Survey's PLI scorecard.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Has PLI worked? How would you evaluate it?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Outcome measures: incremental sales, investment, jobs, exports, localisation depth
> - Sector differences (mobile phones strong; others slower), and the question of deadweight (would investment have happened anyway)
> - Component depth and value addition vs "screwdriver assembly"
> - Fiscal cost per job and sunset risk

## 7. Make in India, China+1 and Electronics
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 2014 launch, manufacturing share, FDI inflows, electronics exports, semiconductors

### Definition
**Make in India** was launched on **25 September 2014** with targets of 12-14% annual manufacturing growth and a **25% manufacturing share of GDP** (originally by 2022, later 2025); Wikipedia reports the share fell from 16.7% (2013-14) to 15.9% (2023-24), while the Economic Survey 2025-26 quotes manufacturing at 12.9% of GDP in 2023 with a target of 25% by 2035: **definitions differ (national accounts GVA vs international comparison), so cite the source**. Bright spots: **electronics**, where domestic production rose from 19.9% of smartphone sales (2014-15) to 95% by 2019, and **electronics exports from ₹18,000 crore in FY2015 to ₹5.45 lakh crore in FY2025** ([Wikipedia: Make in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_in_India); [Wikipedia: Semiconductor industry in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_industry_in_India)).

**China+1.** Firms diversify away from reliance on China to protect against tariffs and disruptions. Verified indicators: gross FDI inflows of **$81.0 billion in FY25 (+13%)** and **$64.7 billion in April-November 2025** against $55.8 billion a year earlier; exports of **$825.3 billion in FY25**; and diversification of exports after the US imposed 50% tariffs ([Economic Survey ch. 4](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap04.pdf)). Semiconductor projects: Micron at Sanand (**$2.75 billion**, construction from September 2023) and Tata-Powerchip at Dholera (**₹91,000 crore**, 50,000 wafer starts a month, target 2026). Competitiveness gaps: logistics cost, component depth, power and land cost, skilled labour, and customs duties on inputs. Notes: [[134 Electronics & Semiconductor Supply Chain]], [[141 Supply Chain Disruption Case Library (2011-2026)]], [[014 Global SCM & Sustainability]].

### Example
Electronics exports grew from ₹18,000 crore to ₹5.45 lakh crore: $545{,}000/18{,}000 = 30.3\times$ in ten years, a compound annual growth rate of $30.3^{1/10} - 1 = 41\%$ (about 40% a year). FDI Apr-Nov: $64.7/55.8 = 1.16$, a **16% rise**. A company weighing a China+1 move might compare landed costs: India plant labour cost lower but inbound components priced higher (duties, logistics): if component cost is 70% of cost and is 8% higher while conversion cost (20%) is 25% lower and overheads (10%) flat, net change $= 0.70 \times 8\% - 0.20 \times 25\% = 5.6\% - 5\% = +0.6\%$: near parity, so tariff savings decide.

### In the news
See news box: Budget 2026-27 raises the electronics component scheme to ₹40,000 crore; the Survey cites diversification under 50% US tariffs.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why has India not become a manufacturing hub like China, and what would you change?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Facts: manufacturing share, exports, component depth
> - Gaps: logistics, power, labour laws and skills, duty structure on inputs, scale
> - Interventions: PLI plus ecosystem (components), industrial clusters, FTAs, ease of doing business
> - Prioritisation by sector and time horizon

## 8. BIS Quality Control Orders (QCOs)
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 88 (2019) to 765 (2024), ISI mark, input-cost concern

### Definition
The **Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)**, India's national standards body since 1 April 1987 under the **BIS Act 2016** (effective 12 October 2017), runs the **ISI mark** and **Compulsory Registration Scheme (CRS)**. **Quality Control Orders (QCOs)** make BIS certification **mandatory** for notified products sold or imported, which protects consumers and curbs low-quality imports, but raises compliance cost and lead time. QCOs rose from **88 in 2019 to 765 in 2024**, about a ninefold increase; critics argue QCOs on **inputs and intermediates** raise costs for export-oriented sectors such as footwear and electronics ([Wikipedia: BIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Indian_Standards)). QCO policy has been adjusted over time, so check the current list. Supply chain impacts: supplier qualification and certification lead time, restricted sourcing options for imported components, and possible shortages when certifications lag. See [[011 Quality Management (TQM)]], [[121 Supplier Quality & Automotive Core Tools (APQP, PPAP, 8D)]], [[126 International Trade Documentation, Customs & Trade Finance]].

### Example
Compliance economics (illustrative): a firm imports 1,000 tonnes a month of an input at ₹100,000 a tonne (₹10 crore a month). A QCO adds certification and testing cost of 1.5% (₹15 lakh a month) and a 3-week lead-time buffer; safety stock for the extra 3 weeks $= 1000 \times 3/4.3 = 698$ tonnes $\approx ₹7$ crore of stock, carrying cost at 15% $= ₹1.05$ crore a year. Total annual cost $= 15 \times 12 = ₹1.8$ crore + ₹1.05 crore $= ₹2.85$ crore, about 2.4% of annual input spend ($2.85/120$). A QCO's price effect should be measured like this before opposing or supporting it.

### In the news
Not tied to the shared news box; the Budget 2026-27 emphasis on tariff rationalisation and exemptions for domestic items interacts with QCO policy (see sub-topic 11).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Are Quality Control Orders good for Indian manufacturing?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Consumer protection and import-quality benefits
> - Costs for downstream users, especially on inputs; export competitiveness
> - Suggests targeted QCOs (final consumer safety) and faster certification
> - A numeric illustration of compliance cost

## 9. GeM and Public Procurement
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Launched 9 Aug 2016, GFR Rule 149, ₹20 lakh crore, MSE share, 9.75% savings

### Definition
**Government e Marketplace (GeM)** was launched on **9 August 2016** under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry and is **mandatory for government procurement of available goods and services under Rule 149 of the General Financial Rules 2017**. Reported scale as of August 2026: procurement exceeding **₹20 lakh crore** since inception; **₹1.47 lakh crore GMV in the first four months of FY2026-27**; **1.64 lakh buyer organisations**, about **25.45 lakh sellers**; **12.25 lakh MSEs** accounting for **45.6% of cumulative GMV (₹9.07 lakh crore)**; more than 10,900 product categories and 330 service categories; and a World Bank assessment that buyers save about **9.75% on median price** ([Wikipedia: GeM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_e_Marketplace)). For suppliers, GeM means transparent bidding, catalogue management, and payment timelines; for the supply chain it is a large, rules-driven demand source. Related: [[002 Procurement & Strategic Sourcing]], [[122 Spend Analysis, Savings & Procurement Maturity]], [[136 Supply Chain Finance & Working Capital]] (MSME payment rules, TReDS).

### Example
Savings on ₹1.47 lakh crore of GMV at 9.75% of median price is about ₹14,300 crore (if 9.75% is read as a share of the price actually paid; relative to the higher median price the implied saving is $0.0975/(1 - 0.0975) \approx 10.8\%$ of the amount paid, about ₹15,900 crore): so "about ₹14,000-16,000 crore" over four months, an indicative estimate. MSE concentration: $9.07/20 = 45\%$ of ₹20 lakh crore, consistent with the 45.6% reported.

### In the news
Not tied to the shared news box; GeM is also a lever in Budget-era push for domestic manufacturing and MSME participation.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How should an MSME manufacturer approach GeM, and what are the risks?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Registration, catalogue, bidding strategy and price discipline
> - Working-capital risks from payment delays (and protections for MSMEs)
> - Capacity planning for lumpy government orders
> - Compliance with specifications and QCOs

## 10. Free Trade Agreements: EFTA, UK, EU, Oman and New Zealand
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ TEPA in force 1 Oct 2025, UK CETA signed 24 Jul 2025, EU announced 27 Jan 2026

### Definition
**India-EFTA TEPA** (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland): signed **10 March 2024**, in force **1 October 2025**; EFTA to eliminate or reduce duties on about **99.6%** of India's export value, India on about **95%** of EFTA exports; EFTA investment pledge **$100 billion over 15 years** aiming at about one million jobs; bilateral goods trade about $25 billion (2023) ([Wikipedia: India-EFTA TEPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93EFTA_Trade_and_Economic_Partnership_Agreement)).

**India-UK CETA:** signed **24 July 2025**; India to remove or reduce duties on about **90% of tariff lines (92% of current UK exports)**; **64% of UK product categories duty-free at once, rising to 85% over a decade**; reported in force **15 July 2026** ([Wikipedia: CETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_Comprehensive_Economic_and_Trade_Agreement)).

**India-EU FTA:** announced **27 January 2026** after nearly two decades; EU tariffs on nearly **97%** of EU exports to India are eliminated or cut; about **91%** of Indian exports face no duty on entry into force; autos cut from up to 110% to 40%; EU tariffs of **12-17% on textiles and apparel eliminated**; entry into force expected around early 2027 after Council, Parliament and Indian approvals ([Wikipedia: India-EU FTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93European_Union_Free_Trade_Agreement)). Also concluded in December 2025: **India-Oman CEPA** (duty-free access for 99.38% of India's exports by value as reported) and **India-New Zealand FTA** ([Economic Survey ch. 4](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap04.pdf)). Statuses change: **verify before quoting**. Supply chain relevance: rules of origin, certificates of origin, sourcing from partner countries, and landed-cost changes; see [[126 International Trade Documentation, Customs & Trade Finance]], [[014 Global SCM & Sustainability]] (CBAM).

### Example
An Indian apparel exporter ships ₹100 crore FOB to the EU facing a 12% duty. When the duty is eliminated, the buyer's landed price falls 12/112 = 10.7% if the exporter keeps price unchanged; alternatively the exporter can **raise its price by up to 12%** and keep the buyer's landed cost identical, adding $0.12 \times 100 = ₹12$ crore of revenue (all else equal). Rules of origin matter: if only 40% of value is Indian and the rule needs 50%, the duty cut is lost, so sourcing fabric domestically or from a partner can decide whether the benefit is captured.

See news box for the status of the three agreements.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would the India-UK or India-EU FTA change a client's supply chain?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Tariff schedule by product, phase-in period, rules of origin and certification
> - Landed-cost and pricing effects for both exports and imported inputs
> - Network: sourcing, final assembly location, logistics lanes
> - Risks: delayed ratification, non-tariff barriers, CBAM

## 11. Customs Reform and Trade Facilitation
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Tariff rationalisation, courier export cap removal, export timelines, duty-free inputs

### Definition
Customs reform targets **dwell time and cost** at ports and airports through risk-based clearance, digital filing and consolidated documentation, and reduces tariff distortions (high duties on inputs, low on finished goods or vice versa). **Budget 2026-27 measures (1 February 2026):** tariff rationalisation (removing exemptions on domestically manufactured items and simplifying the duty structure); raising the **duty-free input limit for seafood exports from 1% to 3% of FOB value**; extending the export timeline for leather and textile finished goods **from 6 months to 1 year**; removing the **₹10 lakh per-consignment value cap for courier exports** to support e-commerce exports; safe-harbour provisions for non-residents in bonded zones; and duty-free imports of specified processing inputs ([Budget highlights](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/bh1.pdf); [Budget speech](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_Speech.pdf)). Details of earlier measures (faceless assessment, single-window clearance, authorised economic operator programme) are covered in [[126 International Trade Documentation, Customs & Trade Finance]]. Link to inventory: dwell time in days is a direct pipeline-inventory cost ([[009 Logistics & Distribution]]).

### Example
Dwell time value (illustrative): imported components worth ₹5 crore a day arrive at a port; cutting dwell time from 5 days to 3 days reduces average inventory in the port stage by $5 \times 2 = ₹10$ crore. At 15% carrying cost that is ₹1.5 crore a year, before demurrage savings; at ₹5,000 per container-day demurrage on 40 containers a day for 2 days the saving is $5{,}000 \times 40 \times 2 = ₹4$ lakh a day, or about ₹14.6 crore over 365 days. Export timeline extension from 6 to 12 months similarly doubles the time a firm has to complete duty-free export obligations, cutting default risk and penalty cost.

### In the news
See news box and Budget measures above.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What customs reforms would help Indian exporters the most?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Dwell time, documentation, risk-based inspection, digital integration with ULIP
> - Duty structure: inverted duties, drawback and RoDTEP-type rebates
> - MSME and e-commerce exporters (courier channel)
> - Metrics: release time, cost per container, rejection rates

## 12. Union Budget 2025-27: Manufacturing Measures at a Glance
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Capex ₹12.2 lakh crore, seven sectors, container manufacturing, rare earth, waterways

### Definition
**Budget 2026-27 (1 February 2026)** emphasised scaling manufacturing in seven strategic areas: **Biopharma SHAKTI** (₹10,000 crore over five years, 3 new NIPERs, 1,000+ clinical trial sites); **Semiconductor Mission 2.0** (equipment, materials, Indian IP); **electronics components** (outlay from ₹22,919 crore to ₹40,000 crore); **rare earth corridors** (Odisha, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu); **textiles** (national fibre scheme); **capital goods** (hi-tech tool rooms, construction equipment initiative, **₹10,000 crore Container Manufacturing Scheme**); and revival of **200 legacy industrial clusters**. Logistics: seven high-speed rail "growth connectors", **20 new National Waterways**, **Dankuni-Surat DFCs**, **Coastal Cargo Promotion Scheme (6% to 12% by 2047)**. Fiscal: capex ₹12.2 lakh crore (₹11.2 lakh crore in FY2025-26) and a deficit target of **4.3% of GDP** ([Budget highlights](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/bh1.pdf); [Budget speech](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_Speech.pdf)). The Budget 2025-26 measures (Export Promotion Mission and a manufacturing mission) are not re-verified here. Context notes: [[180 Current Affairs & Economy Briefing for MBA Interviews (2025-26)]], [[103 Key India Data Points to Memorize]], [[223 Logistics, Manufacturing & Industry Data Points - India]].

### Example
Capex growth $12.2/11.2 - 1 = 8.9\%$; electronics components scheme growth $40{,}000/22{,}919 - 1 = 74.5\%$ (a near-doubling of an outlay, signalling priority). The ₹10,000 crore Container Manufacturing Scheme equals the price of about 3.3 lakh containers at an assumed ₹3 lakh each (₹10,000 crore / ₹3 lakh = 3.33 lakh), used only to show scale, not as a reported figure. Container availability affects freight rates; whether India imports most of its containers today is a premise to verify.

## 13. Interview Framing: "What Should India Do to Cut Logistics Cost?"
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Structure, levers, quantification, prioritisation

### Definition
Use a five-lever structure and attach a number to each:
1. **Mode mix:** shift bulk to rail, coastal and inland waterways (DFC, Sagarmala; coastal modal share 6% to 12% by 2047 in the Budget plan).
2. **Infrastructure and nodes:** multimodal logistics parks, container terminals, last-mile connectors (Gati Shakti planning).
3. **Process and policy:** GST and e-way bill efficiency, faster customs, fewer inter-agency checks, standard documents.
4. **Technology:** ULIP integration, visibility, digital freight matching, fleet formalisation.
5. **Industry practices:** consolidation, backhaul, standard load units, warehousing standards, cold chain.
Prioritise by **impact and ease**: process and data first (quick), nodes next, big infrastructure last. Remember the baseline: 7.97% of GDP in FY24 per the Survey, so frame the target as a **further cut of 1-2 points of GDP** rather than "from 14% to 8%". Cross-links: [[009 Logistics & Distribution]], [[125 Transportation Management Deep Dive]], [[113 Network Design & Facility Location Modelling]], [[024 Consulting Frameworks]].

### Example
Illustrative levers on a ₹100 base of logistics cost, with assumed shares and savings: transport ₹55 (shift 10% of tonne-km from road to rail at 30% lower cost: $55 \times 0.10 \times 0.30 = ₹1.65$), warehousing ₹15 (consolidation and utilisation, 8% saving $= ₹1.2$), inventory carrying ₹20 (better visibility, 5% cut $= ₹1.0$), admin and delays ₹10 (digital documentation, 15% cut $= ₹1.5$). Total saving $= 1.65 + 1.2 + 1.0 + 1.5 = ₹5.35$, or **5.35%** of logistics cost. On the national figure of 7.97% of GDP this equals $0.0797 \times 0.0535 = 0.43\%$ of GDP (about 0.4 points): a **realistic, quantified framing** is more credible than a headline target.

### In the news
See news box for current baseline and policy moves.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What should India do to cut logistics cost?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Baseline with source and year (7.97% of GDP in FY24) and caveat on definitions
> - Five levers with ranked priorities and approximate quantification
> - Policy tools in place (NLP, Gati Shakti, DFC, Sagarmala) and what is still missing (last mile, fragmented trucking, MSME digitisation)
> - **Ask the interviewer:** "National policy or a firm-level answer?"

## 14. ⭐ Advanced: From Policy to Plant: Location and Sourcing Decisions Under PLI, FTAs and QCOs
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
A company-level model turns policy into numbers. **Net landed benefit of a location** $= $ (production cost + logistics + duty + compliance cost) $-$ (incentives + tariff savings). Components: **PLI** (a percentage of incremental sales for a defined period), **tariff preference under an FTA** (only if rules of origin are met), **QCO costs** (certification, lead time), **GST and inverted-duty effects**, and **logistics cost** (distance to ports and customers). Prefer a **scenario view** because policies change (for example PLI sunset, FTA entry-into-force dates, QCO rollbacks). Related: [[113 Network Design & Facility Location Modelling]], [[224 Capital Budgeting for Operations - Capex, Lease vs Buy & Replacement]], [[226 Corporate Finance Essentials - Capital Structure & Cost of Capital]].

### Example
Compare two plant locations for a product with ₹500 crore annual sales (illustrative):
- **Site A (near port, in an incentive-eligible sector):** cost ₹400 crore + logistics ₹30 crore = ₹430 crore; PLI at 4% of incremental sales ₹100 crore $= ₹4$ crore; QCO compliance ₹3 crore: net $= 430 + 3 - 4 = ₹429$ crore.
- **Site B (inland, cheaper land and labour):** cost ₹385 crore + logistics ₹45 crore = ₹430 crore; no incentive; QCO ₹3 crore: net $= ₹433$ crore.
Site A wins by ₹4 crore a year. If the PLI ends, A's net becomes 433, equal to B's. Sensitivity: if logistics for B falls by ₹7 crore after a freight corridor opens, B's net $= ₹426$ crore and **B beats A by ₹3 crore**. The decision flips on a policy variable, so a robust choice builds **option value** (modular plant, phased capex) or negotiates incentives.

See news box: corridors, PLI payments and FTAs change location economics.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Where should a client put its next plant in India given incentives and logistics?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Net landed cost model with each policy variable as an input
> - Sensitivity and scenario analysis on incentive tenure, freight corridor timing, FTA entry
> - Non-cost factors: talent, supplier ecosystem, power reliability, state policy
> - Phased investment to retain flexibility

## 1. GD Formats: Topic, Abstract, Case, Fishbowl and Leaderless
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ factual, controversial, abstract, case-based, fishbowl, leaderless; typical size and time; what each format rewards

### Definition
A **group discussion (GD)** places 8 to 12 candidates around a topic for about 10 to 20 minutes while evaluators observe. Common formats:

| Format | What it is | What it rewards |
|---|---|---|
| **Factual / current-affairs topic** | "India's UPI success: lessons for the world" | Facts, data, structure |
| **Controversial / opinion topic** | "Should RBI raise rates now?" | Balanced argument, composure |
| **Abstract topic** | "Blue", "Zero", "A closed door" | Creativity, framing, structure from nothing |
| **Case-based GD** | A business scenario with data and a decision | Analysis, prioritisation, group consensus |
| **Fishbowl** | Inner circle discusses while outer circle observes, then rotates | Listening, entering and exiting gracefully |
| **Leaderless task / role play** | The group must solve a problem or allocate resources | Collaboration, leadership emergence |
| **Chairman's / leadership GD** | Used by some firms (e.g. Tata's TAS) to observe leadership behaviour | Facilitation, influence without dominance |

Time maths for planning: in a 15-minute GD with 12 people the average share is $15\times60/12 = 75$ seconds each, so three tight interventions of about 25 seconds beat one long speech. With 10 people the share is 90 seconds; with 8 it is 112.5 seconds.

Related: [[028 Guesstimates & Market Sizing]] (structuring numbers), [[029 Data Interpretation & Charts]] (case GDs with data), [[162 Structured Communication - SCQA, Storylines & Case Delivery]].

### Example
Topic "Quick commerce: boom or bubble?" in a 12-person panel. A weak entry: "Quick commerce is very good and growing fast." A strong entry: "Let us first agree what we mean by 'success': customer convenience, unit economics, and worker welfare. I suggest we look at each in turn, and I will start with the economics." This gives the group a structure and sets you up as a facilitator.

### In the news
See news box. The Tata Group's GD stage shows that big recruiters evaluate how you treat the group, not merely what you say.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How do you approach a group discussion? Walk me through your strategy."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Awareness of formats and their different rewards
> - A structure: frame, contribute, build on others, summarise
> - Balance of content and teamwork
> - Time awareness (average speaking time)
> - Examples of past GDs and what you learned

## 2. What Evaluators Assess: Criteria and a Scoring Rubric
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ content, structure, communication, listening and team behaviour, leadership, composure; quality over quantity

### Definition
Panels usually score some mix of:
1. **Content and knowledge:** relevance, facts, depth, originality.
2. **Structure and analytical thinking:** clear framework, logic, ability to link points.
3. **Communication:** clarity, concision, voice, language.
4. **Team behaviour and listening:** building on others, inviting quieter members, not interrupting.
5. **Leadership and initiative:** moving the group forward, summarising, resolving deadlock.
6. **Composure and attitude:** handling aggression, humility, openness to changing view.

Evaluators generally penalise: dominating, shouting, monologues, data fabrication, off-topic wandering, silence, and trying to "win" an argument. Some schools and recruiters score entry and exit separately; do not assume "first speaker" or "most words" wins.

### Example
Illustrative weighted rubric (score 1-5; weights 30/20/20/20/10 for content, structure, communication, teamwork/listening, leadership):
| Candidate | Content | Structure | Comm. | Teamwork | Leadership | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (balanced) | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 3 | $0.3\times4+0.2\times3+0.2\times4+0.2\times5+0.1\times3=3.90$ |
| B (loud expert) | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 5 | $1.5+0.8+0.6+0.4+0.5=3.80$ |
| C (quiet analyst) | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 2 | $0.9+0.8+0.8+0.8+0.2=3.50$ |
Candidate A outscores the louder, more knowledgeable B because teamwork carries weight. Rubrics vary, but the pattern, that team behaviour can offset raw content, is typical.

### In the news
See news box. A Chairman's GD (Tata) is explicitly a leadership assessment, so leadership and teamwork criteria dominate over trivia.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What do you think evaluators look for in a GD?" / "Tell me about a GD you took part in and how you contributed."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Criteria listed with examples of behaviour (not just words)
> - Quality over quantity: 3 to 4 sharp interventions
> - Teamwork behaviours (invite, build, summarise)
> - Self-reflection on a real GD
> - Awareness that rubric weights vary by recruiter

## 3. Entry Strategies: When and How to Start
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ initiating (high reward, high risk), second-speaker strategy, framing, definitions, when not to start

### Definition
**Initiating** a GD can earn credit (visibility, setting the agenda) but a weak start is remembered. Rules:
- Start only if you understand the topic and can frame it in 20 to 30 seconds: **definition + scope + structure** (e.g., "economic, social and policy angles").
- Good opening types: a defining sentence ("By quick commerce we mean...") + a one-line framework; a surprising verified fact; a clear question that structures the discussion.
- **If you cannot start, enter early:** be the 2nd to 4th speaker by building on the first ("Adding to Priya's point on unit economics, the other side is..."). Entry should add something new.
- **Never** open with a statistic you cannot support, a joke that could offend, or a rambling story.
- If the topic is abstract, start by giving a **working interpretation** and invite others to add theirs.

### Example
Topic: "Should RBI raise interest rates in October 2026?" Entry: "Repo is 5.25% and inflation touched 4.82% in August, above the 4% target for three months, with oil above $100. I suggest we examine this on three lines: inflation outlook, growth impact, and global factors." (20 seconds; uses figures from the news box; creates a structure.) Check the actual policy outcome before using this in a live GD.

### In the news
See news box. The RBI item is a ready example of an entry containing a fact, a structure and a neutral framing.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Is it better to start a GD or wait?" 

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Start only with clarity and a structure; otherwise enter early with a build-on
> - The entry adds a framework, not just an opinion
> - Facts that can be defended
> - Time discipline (20 to 30 seconds)
> - Neutral language to invite discussion

## 4. Mid-GD: Structuring a Point with PEEL, Building and Using Data
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ PEEL (Point, Evidence, Explain, Link); reference others; numbers; counterpoints; staying on topic

### Definition
**PEEL** keeps each contribution crisp (25 to 30 seconds):
- **P**oint: one-sentence claim.
- **E**vidence: fact, number, example.
- **E**xplain: why it matters (so what).
- **L**ink: connect to the topic, to the previous speaker, or to the next angle.

Other mid-GD tools:
- **Build:** "Building on Rahul's point about logistics cost, the data show..."
- **Bridge:** link two speakers' views to create common ground.
- **Redirect:** "We are discussing causes; shall we move to solutions?"
- **Quantify carefully:** use a few reliable numbers; round and attribute ("MoSPI's Q1 release shows services growing about 10%").
- **Counterpoint with respect:** "I see the benefit; the risk is..."
- Use frameworks to cover angles: PESTLE, stakeholders, short-term vs long-term, India vs global ([[023 Business Fundamentals & Strategy]], [[024 Consulting Frameworks]]).

### Example
Topic: "India's growth is services-led; should it focus on manufacturing?" PEEL intervention: **Point:** "Both sectors are growing fast, but they create different kinds of jobs." **Evidence:** "In Q1 FY27 secondary grew 8.6% and tertiary 10% per the national accounts release." **Explain:** "Manufacturing absorbs more semi-skilled workers per rupee of capital, while services add higher value per worker." **Link:** "So the question for policy is not either-or but which constraints, such as logistics cost and skills, to ease first, which connects to Rahul's earlier point."

### In the news
See news box. The Q1 FY27 sector figures give a reliable evidence line for several economy topics.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How do you make sure your points in a GD stand out and are not just opinions?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - PEEL or similar structure
> - Evidence with attribution and rounding
> - Building on others by name
> - Linking points to a bigger frame
> - Acknowledging counterpoints

## 5. Exit Strategies and Summarising the Discussion
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ summary structure; neutrality; conclusion vs summary; 30-45 seconds; closing when time is ending

### Definition
A **summary** (when the panel or time signals the end, or when the discussion is clearly closing) shows structure and leadership. Format (30 to 45 seconds):
1. **Context:** the topic and the main question.
2. **Views:** two or three major viewpoints, fairly (for, against, middle ground).
3. **Agreement and disagreement:** what the group aligned on, what remained open.
4. **Way forward:** a balanced concluding line, not your personal verdict unless the group reached one.

Rules: do not introduce new arguments; do not attribute views to people you did not hear; if no consensus, say so and state the main trade-off. If you are not asked to summarise, a short closing statement in the last minute is still useful; wait for a natural pause and keep it short.

### Example
Summary for "Quick commerce: boom or bubble?": "We discussed whether quick commerce is a sustainable model. One view was that convenience and rising order frequency support growth; the other that heavy discounting and dark-store costs strain unit economics. We agreed that profitability depends on order value and density, and that worker welfare and regulation matter. There was no consensus on timing, but the group leaned towards a consolidation phase with fewer, stronger players." (about 70 words, roughly 30 seconds at 140 words per minute.)

### In the news
See news box. For topics like the RBI decision where the group splits (hike vs hold), a neutral summary of the trade-off (inflation control vs growth) is a safe and impressive close.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "You are asked to summarise a GD in 45 seconds. How do you do it?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Fair coverage of all main views
> - Clear structure (context, views, agreement, way forward)
> - No new points or personal attacks
> - Time discipline
> - Calm, confident tone

## 6. Handling Aggressors, Dominators, Silence and Derailment
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ aggressive speakers, interrupters, silent members, off-topic drift, deadlock, factual disputes

### Definition
Tactics:
- **Aggressor / interrupter:** stay calm; wait for a pause; "May I complete this point, then I would like to hear yours." Do not match volume.
- **Dominator (monologues):** acknowledge, then redirect: "That is a useful point; let me add a different angle." Bring in others by name.
- **Silent members:** invite them by name with an easy question: "Anita, from a supply chain perspective, what do you think?" Evaluators notice inclusion.
- **Off-topic drift:** "That is interesting; let us return to our main question: ..."
- **Deadlock:** propose criteria or a middle path: "Could we agree on the criteria first: cost, speed, risk?"
- **Factual dispute:** "We seem to have different numbers; for this discussion let us use ranges and move on."
- **You are the quiet one:** enter within the first few minutes with a PEEL point, then pace yourself; one strong early contribution beats many late ones.

### Example
Aggressor says: "Your point is nonsense; RBI must hike!" Response: "I understand the urgency on inflation. My point is that growth is also at risk if rates rise too fast; can we compare the two risks?" (calm, acknowledges, redirects to structured comparison). Then continue: "Priya, you mentioned oil; how would you weigh it?" You have defused conflict and included another member.

### In the news
See news box. Topics tied to current events (RBI, oil) often produce heated positions; composure itself is assessed.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "In a GD, one participant keeps interrupting you. What do you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Calm tone and no retaliation
> - Assertive but polite phrases
> - Redirecting to the topic and inviting others
> - Protecting the group's time and objective
> - A real example where you handled this

## 7. Abstract and Case-Based GDs
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ interpret then structure; multiple lenses; case: goal, constraints, options, decision; data use

### Definition
**Abstract topics** ("Red", "Zero is not a number", "The glass is half full"): spend 20 seconds defining a lens, then structure: literal, symbolic, business and societal angles; connect back to something concrete (a business or social example). Avoid forced philosophy; aim for a coherent thread.

**Case-based GDs** present a scenario (for example a retailer with falling margins, or a plant deciding where to expand). Process:
1. Clarify objective and constraints (budget, time, risk).
2. Agree criteria and weights (cost, speed, risk, customer impact).
3. Generate 2 to 3 options.
4. Evaluate quickly with the data given; show simple arithmetic.
5. Decide, plan next steps and risks; agree the group's recommendation.
Use consulting-style structure ([[162 Structured Communication - SCQA, Storylines & Case Delivery]], [[025 Case Interview — Profitability]], [[026 Case Interview — Operations Cases]]).

### Example
Abstract topic: "Zero." Lens: "Zero as a starting point, a limit, and a target." Business examples: zero-based budgeting (starting point), zero inventory or zero defects (target; [[007 Lean Manufacturing]], [[008 Six Sigma & Quality Tools]]), zero tolerance policies (limit). Social: zero hunger as an SDG. Conclusion: zero is powerful when it is a clear target with feasible steps, and harmful when it is an unrealistic absolute.

Case: "A company's logistics cost is 11% of sales, competitors are at 9%. Where do you start?" Criteria: size of saving, speed, risk. Options: network (warehouse count), transport (mode and carrier mix), inventory (stock holding). Quick maths: on ₹1,000 crore sales, closing 2 points of gap is ₹20 crore ($1000\times0.02$). Recommend a quick audit of transport rates and utilisation first (fast and lower risk), then a network study ([[113 Network Design & Facility Location Modelling]]).

### In the news
See news box. Case-GD facts such as freight or rate-sensitive costs benefit from macro context (oil price and inflation), tying the case to current events.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How do you approach an abstract GD topic like 'Blue'?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A clear lens in the first 20 seconds
> - Multiple angles organised into a structure
> - Concrete business or social examples
> - A thread that returns to a conclusion
> - Avoiding over-abstract language

## 8. Sample Topics Set A: Economy and Policy (7 Topics)
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ balanced points for and against; verified figures; remember to check dates before quoting

### Definition
Each topic gives 3 to 4 balanced points. Figures are from sources checked on 4 October 2026; verify live figures before any GD.

**1. "India's growth: services-led or manufacturing-led?"** Facts: Q1 FY27 secondary +8.6%, tertiary +10%, primary +2.9% (news box). Points: (a) services drive value added and exports (IT, professional services); (b) manufacturing creates mass semi-skilled jobs and supply chain depth ([[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]]); (c) logistics cost, skills and power are the shared constraints; (d) the answer is complementarity, with services supporting manufacturing competitiveness.

**2. "Should RBI raise rates in October 2026?"** Facts: repo 5.25%, CPI 4.82% (Aug), crude above $100, hike expected by poll (news box; check outcome). For: contain inflation expectations, keep real rates positive (5.25% minus 4.82% is only 0.43 percentage points), support the rupee. Against: growth is strong now, supply-side oil shock is not demand-driven, hikes hurt borrowers and MSMEs. Balance: data-dependent small hike with guidance, plus fiscal and supply measures.

**3. "UPI should be free forever."** Facts: 24.51 billion transactions and ₹29.82 lakh crore in August 2026; average ticket about ₹1,217 (news box). For free: financial inclusion, network effects, low-value payments. Against: banks and payment apps bear costs; sustainability of the ecosystem and security investment. Balance: transparent targeted charges on large merchants or credit-linked use, with small-ticket person-to-person transfers protected; check current policy.

**4. "India's labour codes: a reform or a burden?"** Facts: four labour codes effective 21 November 2025; standard wages definition (basic at least 50% of CTC per reports), gratuity for fixed-term staff after one year ([KPMG India](https://kpmg.com/in/en/blogs/2025/12/implementation-of-labour-codes-what-changes-and-road-ahead.html), [Paytm Money](https://www.paytmmoney.com/blog/how-new-labour-codes-redefine-wages-and-impact-your-entire-pay-structure/)). For: simplification, social security, formalisation. Against: lower take-home for some employees, higher compliance cost for MSMEs, state-level rules vary. Balance: phased adoption with clear guidance. See [[178 HR Round, Offers & Salary Negotiation in India]].

**5. "Welfare schemes versus capital expenditure."** Points: (a) welfare supports demand and poverty reduction; (b) capex has multiplier and productivity effects; (c) fiscal deficit limits; (d) targeting and leakage reduction (digital public infrastructure) can lift both. No single figure is needed; say that budgets balance both.

**6. "A weaker rupee is good for exports."** Points: (a) exporters gain competitiveness; (b) import-heavy sectors (oil, electronics) pay more and inflation rises; (c) the effect is limited by import content of exports; (d) volatility matters more than the level. Link to oil price news and RBI decision.

**7. "Is 'Make in India' (and PLI) delivering?"** Points: (a) electronics and mobile manufacturing expansion; (b) dependence on imported components; (c) fiscal cost of incentives; (d) need for logistics and ease-of-doing-business reforms. See [[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]] and [[134 Electronics & Semiconductor Supply Chain]].

### Example
Delivering topic 2 within 90 seconds: Point (inflation above target), Evidence (4.82% vs 4%), Explain (oil shock raises costs; real rate only 0.43 points positive), Link (growth risk and the other side's view). Close with "a small, guided hike balances both."

### In the news
See news box. Topics 1 to 3 draw directly on the three data points in the news box; topic 4 uses the labour code sources listed above.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Give your view on [an economy topic] in 60 seconds." / "What are the pros and cons of rate hikes now?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A balanced for/against structure
> - Two verifiable data points, quoted precisely
> - Stakeholder view (consumers, firms, government)
> - A pragmatic way forward
> - Awareness of data dates and what could change

## 9. Sample Topics Set B: Supply Chain and Business (7 Topics)
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ resilience vs efficiency; quick commerce; China+1; gig work; automation; logistics cost; sustainability

### Definition
**8. "Just-in-time versus just-in-case after the oil and shipping shocks."** Facts: a World Bank analysis reports that Brent rose about 65% (roughly $46 a barrel) by end-March 2026, the largest monthly rise ever, as Strait of Hormuz disruption cut global oil supply by 10.1 million barrels a day in March; an early April ceasefire gave only temporary relief ([World Bank Blogs](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/strait-of-hormuz-disruption-sends-oil-prices-surging)). For JIT: lower inventory cost, higher efficiency. For JIC: buffer against shocks; strategic safety stock for critical items. Balance: segment by criticality and lead-time risk, hold buffers where disruption cost is high ([[015 Supply Chain Risk & Resilience]], [[007 Lean Manufacturing]], [[003 Inventory Management]]).

**9. "Quick commerce: boom or bubble?"** Points: (a) convenience and rising order frequency; (b) heavy discounting, dark-store rentals and rider costs strain unit economics; (c) impact on kiranas and brands' channel strategy; (d) worker welfare and regulation. Balance: likely consolidation with profitability tied to order value and density ([[129 E-commerce & Quick-Commerce Fulfilment]], [[130 FMCG & Retail Distribution - India Route-to-Market]]).

**10. "Can India become the next China+1 manufacturing hub?"** Points: (a) large domestic market, PLI and a young workforce; (b) logistics cost, power reliability, land and labour-law complexity; (c) component ecosystem and scale gaps; (d) tariff volatility (US tariff changes in 2025-26, see topic 20) and competition from Vietnam and others. Balance: sector-wise (electronics, pharma, chemicals) strategy plus infrastructure delivery ([[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]]).

**11. "Gig and platform workers should get social security."** Facts: the Code on Social Security is one of the four labour codes effective 21 November 2025 (KPMG); it is widely reported to provide for social security coverage of gig and platform workers, but the coverage details were not verified in this session and depend on rules and schemes. For: income security, safety. Against: higher platform costs, possible reduced flexibility, classification disputes. Balance: contribution models, portable benefits, and transparent algorithms.

**12. "Will warehouse automation destroy jobs?"** Points: (a) automation reduces repetitive picking jobs and raises accuracy; (b) creates roles in maintenance, data and robot supervision; (c) adoption depends on labour cost vs capex; in India labour is cheap so semi-automation first; (d) reskilling needed ([[127 Warehouse Engineering - Racking, Sizing & Material Handling]], [[128 Warehouse Labour, WES-WCS & Yard Management]]).

**13. "Reducing India's logistics cost: infrastructure or technology?"** Points: (a) roads, rail, ports and multimodal hubs under Gati Shakti; (b) digital layers (e-way bill, FASTag, unified logistics platforms); (c) fragmented trucking and low utilisation; (d) shift to rail and coastal shipping. Balance: both, with last-mile and trucker formalisation. Avoid quoting a single "logistics cost % of GDP" number unless verified ([[009 Logistics & Distribution]], [[223 Logistics, Manufacturing & Industry Data Points - India]]).

**14. "Sustainability in supply chains: cost or competitive advantage?"** Points: (a) compliance and export access (see CBAM, topic 17); (b) energy and waste savings; (c) investor and customer demand; (d) upfront capex, data and supplier readiness challenges ([[014 Global SCM & Sustainability]], [[135 Reverse Logistics, Remanufacturing & EPR in India]]).

### Example
Mini-speech for topic 8 (about 25 seconds): "The recent oil shock showed that lean networks have thin cushions. But holding everything in buffer is costly. A sensible middle path is segmentation: keep buffers and dual sources for critical, long-lead items, and keep lean flow for stable, short-lead items. Inventory carrying cost is commonly 20 to 25% of stock value per year, so the buffer must earn that back in avoided disruption."

### In the news
See news box for macro context (RBI and oil dilemma) and the World Bank link above for the oil shock. For topic 11, the effective date of the labour codes is in the KPMG link in sub-topic 8.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Discuss: resilience versus efficiency in supply chains." / "Quick commerce is not sustainable. Agree or disagree?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Clear definitions and a balanced frame (efficiency vs resilience, convenience vs cost)
> - Real examples (oil shock, Indian quick commerce, auto-parts supply chains)
> - A segmentation or criteria-based conclusion
> - Quantification of trade-offs where possible
> - Stakeholder view: customers, firms, workers, government

## 10. Sample Topics Set C: AI, ESG and Geopolitics (6 Topics)
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ AI and jobs, AI regulation, CBAM, ESG, energy security, tariffs; all with dated facts

### Definition
**15. "Will AI replace MBAs and managers?"** Facts: the India AI Impact Summit (16 to 21 February 2026) reported over US$200 billion in AI-related investment expected, with India adding 20,000 GPUs to more than 38,000 already available under the IndiaAI Mission ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234343&reg=3&lang=1)). For: routine analysis, reporting and forecasting can be automated. Against: judgement, stakeholder management, ethics and domain context remain human; AI raises the bar on analytical productivity. Balance: managers who use AI well will replace those who do not; reskill ([[099 ML for Operations & SCM]], [[220 Responsible AI, Explainability & Model Governance]]).

**16. "Should AI be regulated, or will regulation kill innovation?"** Facts: the Summit Declaration was endorsed by 92 countries and international organisations (PIB, above). For regulation: safety, bias, privacy, accountability. Against: compliance burden on start-ups, slower innovation, fragmented rules. Balance: risk-based, proportionate regulation with sandboxes and clear liability.

**17. "EU's carbon border tax (CBAM): a trade barrier or a climate tool?"** Facts: CBAM entered its definitive phase on 1 January 2026, covering sectors such as steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, power, paper, glass and refining; Indian exports to the EU are dominated by steel, iron and aluminium, and a Global Trade Research Initiative estimate cited in press says exporters may need to cut prices by 15 to 22% to absorb the cost (about ₹150 to ₹220 on each ₹1,000 of price) ([Vajiram & Ravi summary](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/eus-cbam-begins-impact-on-indias-steel-and-aluminium-exports/); treat the estimate as one study's view). For: prevents carbon leakage, rewards clean producers. Against: disguised protectionism, burden on developing economies and MSMEs. Balance: negotiate recognition of domestic carbon pricing, decarbonise exports, build emissions data systems.

**18. "Is ESG reporting meaningful or greenwashing?"** Points: (a) disclosure improves accountability and investor decisions; (b) inconsistent metrics and unverified claims invite greenwashing; (c) assurance, standardisation and supply-chain (Scope 3) data are the real tests; (d) costs for small suppliers. Link [[014 Global SCM & Sustainability]].

**19. "West Asia conflict and India's energy security."** Facts: oil above $100 a barrel (October 2026 Business Standard report) and the 2026 Hormuz disruption (World Bank) from sub-topic 9. Points: (a) India imports most of its crude, so price shocks hit inflation, currency and the fiscal position; (b) diversification of suppliers, strategic reserves, renewables and EV adoption; (c) shipping insurance and route risks affect supply chains; (d) diplomacy and long-term contracts.

**20. "US tariffs and India's trade strategy."** Facts (verify, fast-moving): an interim US-India agreement announced in early February 2026 cut US tariffs on Indian goods to 18% and removed the 25% Russian-oil-related penalty; it is not legally binding and could change; reports also describe a new 10% Section 301 levy from 24 July 2026 on a large share of Indian exports ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com/insights/newsletters/tax/2026/hot-topics/feb-17/us-and-india-announce-interim-agreement); the July 2026 item is from a secondary source, [TIGI](https://tigi.global/articles/from-50-to-18-to-a-fresh-10-inside-indias-rollercoaster-year-of-us-tariff-diplom), so confirm before quoting). Points: (a) US is a major export market, so exposure is high; (b) diversify with FTAs (EU, UK, others); (c) climb value chains and quality; (d) domestic reforms to cut costs. Balance: hedged strategy, not a single-market bet.

### Example
Mini-speech for topic 17 (30 seconds): "CBAM is both a climate tool and a trade barrier, depending on perspective. For India's steel and aluminium exporters it means higher costs, and one study estimates price cuts of 15 to 22% to absorb them. The practical response is not only negotiation, but decarbonising production and building emissions data so our exporters can prove their carbon intensity. That turns a barrier into a quality signal."

### In the news
See news box for the RBI-oil context and the sources linked within topics for AI, CBAM and tariffs. These are the topics most likely to feature in 2026-27 campus GDs.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Discuss: AI will take away more jobs than it creates." / "CBAM: protectionism in green clothing?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Balanced stance with at least one verified data point
> - Time frame and scope (short vs long term, India vs global)
> - Stakeholder impacts (workers, firms, government, consumers)
> - A practical way forward, not only criticism
> - Caution on fast-changing facts ("as of this month...")

## 11. Extempore, JAM and Short-Speech Structure
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ 60-second structure; hook, 3 points, close; fillers; abstract and personal topics; practice routine

### Definition
**Extempore** (also called JAM, "Just A Minute") is a short unprepared speech (typically 1 to 2 minutes) after a minute or less of thinking time. Reliable structure for 60 seconds (about 120 to 150 words):
1. **Hook (about 10 seconds):** a definition, a question or a verified fact.
2. **Three points (about 15 seconds each):** use a frame (past, present, future; individual, business, society; pros, cons, way forward; PESTLE).
3. **Close (about 5 seconds):** a one-line conclusion or call to action.
Time check: $10+3\times15+5=60$ seconds.

Tips: choose a stance quickly (agree or disagree) and defend it consistently; use one concrete example; pause instead of saying "umm"; if you blank, restate the topic and move to your frame; keep emotion and humour appropriate. Practise with topics from every domain: current affairs ([[180 Current Affairs & Economy Briefing for MBA Interviews (2025-26)]]), business, supply chain, personal ("my biggest lesson"), abstract ("A mirror").

### Example
Topic: "UPI has changed India." Hook: "In August 2026, UPI handled about 24.5 billion transactions in a month." Points: (1) **Individual:** convenience and inclusion, even street vendors accept QR payments; (2) **Business:** lower cash handling, data for credit; (3) **Economy:** formalisation and a global export of India's digital public infrastructure model. Close: "The next step is making it safe, reliable and sustainable for all." (about 55 seconds, 12 seconds of slack).

### In the news
See news box. The UPI figures give a fresh, checkable hook for a payments or digital India extempore.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Speak for one minute on: 'Is work from office necessary?'" / "Talk about a subject of your choice for two minutes."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A clear opening and a consistent stance
> - Structure with 2 to 3 points and a closing line
> - Concrete example or fact
> - Controlled pace (about 130 words per minute) and few fillers
> - Poise and eye contact despite surprise

## 12. Body Language, Voice and Online GD Etiquette
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ posture, eye contact, gestures, tone, pace; listening signals; virtual GD: camera, audio, turn-taking

### Definition
- **Posture and presence:** sit upright, lean slightly forward, hands visible on the table; avoid crossing arms, fidgeting or pointing.
- **Eye contact:** spread it across the group, not just the evaluator or the dominant speaker.
- **Voice:** moderate pace, audible volume, varied tone; short pauses before key points.
- **Listening signals:** nodding, note-taking, using names when building ("As Sana said...").
- **Dress and conduct:** formal, neat; handle disagreement with polite language.
- **Online GDs:** check camera, light and audio early; mute when not speaking; use the "raise hand" tool or brief verbal cues ("May I add..."); keep contributions shorter because turn-taking is slower; look at the camera when speaking; keep notes off-screen and be honest about any rules on tools.

### Example
Two candidates make the same point. Candidate X speaks to the floor, in a monotone, arms folded, finishing with "so, yeah." Candidate Y glances at colleagues, uses a clear pace, adds "building on Ayesha's point" and ends with "which suggests we should prioritise X." Evaluators rate Y higher on communication and teamwork even though the content is the same.

### In the news
See news box. In hybrid recruiting, virtual group exercises and recorded interviews are common (see [[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]); online etiquette is itself evaluated.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How do you make an impact in an online GD when you cannot read body language?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Concise contributions with clear verbal signposts
> - Technical readiness (audio, camera, connectivity)
> - Active listening cues and naming colleagues
> - Polite interruption techniques
> - Calm and consistent tone

## 13. Common Mistakes and a Self-Review Checklist
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ dominating, silence, fabricated data, off-topic, repetition, personal attacks, no summary; post-GD reflection

### Definition
**Frequent mistakes:**
1. **Speaking too little** (below 2 or 3 interventions) or **too much** (more than 25% of airtime).
2. **No structure:** jumping between points; repeating others.
3. **Fabricated or imprecise facts:** inventing statistics; use ranges and attribution instead.
4. **Aggression or sarcasm;** interrupting; personal remarks.
5. **Taking extreme positions** with no nuance on sensitive topics (politics, religion, gender).
6. **Ignoring the group:** not responding to others; not including quieter members.
7. **Going off topic** or giving a lecture.
8. **Weak start or end:** rambling opening, no closing.
9. **Memorised templates** used regardless of topic.
10. **Slang, filler words and phone-checking.**

**Self-review checklist after each practice GD:** Did I enter within the first third? How many interventions, and did each use PEEL? Did I quote at least one verifiable figure? Did I build on at least two people by name? Did I invite someone in? Did I summarise or close? Did I stay calm? What is one change for next time? Record practice GDs on video and review them ([[107 Common Mistakes & How to Avoid]] has the interview-wide list).

### Example
Self-review of a 12-minute practice GD: 3 interventions (target 3 to 4), 55 seconds each, one a long story (too long, shorten to 25 seconds); 1 statistic quoted but unattributed (add "MoSPI"); built on 1 person (target 2); no summary (prepare a 30-second one). Next practice goal: 4 interventions at 25 seconds each, 2 builds, 1 attributed fact, summary ready. Tracking changes this way makes progress measurable.

### In the news
See news box. Current-affairs figures help only if accurate and attributed; fabricated numbers are among the quickest ways to lose credibility.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What mistakes do candidates commonly make in GDs, and what did you learn from your own?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A concrete list tied to evaluator criteria
> - Honest self-reflection with an example
> - The improvement system (record, review, repeat)
> - Balance between assertiveness and teamwork
> - Evidence of preparation across topic types

## 14. ⭐ Advanced: Leadership GDs, Fishbowl Dynamics and Quantifying Under Pressure
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**Leadership GDs** (such as the Tata "Chairman's GD" format described on the TAS page) test whether you can **lift the group** rather than win airtime. Behaviours that signal leadership:
- Propose a process in the first minutes: objective, criteria, time split (e.g., 3 minutes diagnose, 5 options, 3 decide, 1 summary).
- Include and protect: ask quieter members; handle conflict by reframing to the objective.
- Synthesise: connect two competing views into a combined option.
- Keep time and decisions: "Shall we converge on two options?"
- Own the group result and credit others.

**Fishbowl:** the inner group speaks while the outer circle observes; roles may swap. Enter by adding a new angle, not repeating; exit gracefully ("I will let others contribute now") when you have made your point; observers take notes on the process and give feedback.

**Quantifying under pressure:** use simple arithmetic to move debates: "If a hike of 25 basis points lifts the repo to 5.50%, the gap over 4.82% inflation widens to 0.68 points; the question is whether that is enough." Or for a case: "If logistics cost falls from 11% to 10% on ₹1,000 crore sales, saving is ₹10 crore." Round, state assumptions, and check the sums quietly.

### Example
Leaderless case: "Allocate a ₹50 lakh budget among three priorities: safety training, warehouse automation, and driver incentives." Leader move: "Let us agree criteria first: impact, urgency, cost-effectiveness. Then give each a rough score and allocate in proportion, protecting a floor for safety." Allocation after scoring: safety 40% (₹20 lakh), automation 35% (₹17.5 lakh), driver incentives 25% (₹12.5 lakh); total ₹50 lakh ($20+17.5+12.5=50$). The group agrees, and you close with a 30-second summary and next steps.

### In the news
See news box. The Tata TAS stage mix (gamified assessment, leadership GD, final interview) shows that elite programmes test leadership behaviours across several formats; prepare for that, not just for topic knowledge.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you lead a GD without being the loudest voice?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Process leadership: objectives, criteria, timekeeping
> - Inclusion of quieter members and conflict handling
> - Synthesis and a concise summary
> - Credit to others and ownership of the outcome
> - Short, accurate quantification when helpful

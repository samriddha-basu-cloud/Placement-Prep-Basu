## 1. How Interviewers Use Current Affairs, and How to Use This Briefing
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ three question types, fact-so-what-caveat, the 15 numbers, dating every fact

### Definition
Current-affairs questions in MBA placement interviews (consulting, product, project and operations roles) take three forms: **recall** ("what is the repo rate?"), **impact** ("how does a weaker rupee affect an auto-component exporter?") and **opinion** ("should the RBI hike?"). Interviewers rarely want a lecture; they test whether you follow the economy, can connect a macro fact to a business, and can hold a reasoned view without being dogmatic. Group-discussion and extempore preparation overlaps heavily ([[177 Group Discussions & Extempore for MBA Placements]]), and consulting interviewers will use these facts as inputs to cases ([[024 Consulting Frameworks]], [[161 Consulting Firms & Recruitment Landscape in India]]).

**Method: fact, so-what, caveat.** State the number **with its date**, say what it means for the sector or company in question, then name one caveat (projection, base effect, data revision). **Mark certainty honestly**: Actual (released data), Provisional (will be revised), Projection (forecast), Reported (secondary source). Every table in this note carries that status and a source link.

## 2. Growth: GDP, the New Series and Forecasts
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ FY26 7.7%, Q1 FY27 7.8%, base year 2022-23, forecast spread 6.4–7.2%

### Definition
**GDP** is the value of final goods and services produced; **real** GDP strips out price changes, **nominal** does not, and **GVA** (gross value added) is GDP less net product taxes. India publishes quarterly estimates (about 60 days after quarter-end), a provisional annual estimate around the end of May or early June (5 June in 2026), and revisions later; so any growth figure is **provisional**. In February 2026 the statistical office switched to a **new GDP series with base year 2022-23**, which changed levels and growth rates versus the older 2011-12 series, so compare figures only within one series. Growth is "strong" if it is near or above the roughly 7% potential rate cited by the Economic Survey.

### Example
Verify headline arithmetic yourself: FY26 real GDP ₹323.12 lakh crore vs ₹299.89 lakh crore in FY25 gives 323.12 / 299.89 - 1 = 7.75%, which rounds to the reported 7.7% (the release states growth on unrounded data). Q4 FY26: 87.77 / 81.40 - 1 = 7.83%, reported 7.8%.

## 3. Inflation, the RBI and Interest Rates
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ repo 5.25%, CPI 4.82% (base 2024), WPI 9.92%, MPC 5–7 Oct 2026, stance neutral

### Definition
The **repo rate** is the rate at which the RBI lends short-term to banks; changing it moves lending and deposit rates (monetary transmission). The **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** has six members and meets roughly every two months; its mandate is price stability with a **4% medium-term CPI target** (inflation-targeting framework with a tolerance band; check whether the post-March-2026 framework review changed anything). **CPI** tracks retail prices (food has a large weight); **WPI** tracks wholesale prices and is more sensitive to fuel and metals. A **neutral stance** means the RBI can move either way. Other rates: **SDF** (floor of the corridor, 5.00%), **MSF and Bank Rate** (5.50%). A supply-driven inflation spike (oil, vegetables) is harder for a rate hike to fix than demand-driven inflation, which is why the RBI's wording matters.

A thin real rate is the usual argument for hiking; the counter-argument is that fuel and food inflation is supply-driven and growth is already guided down to 6.7%.

> The August 2026 MPC voted unanimously to hold at 5.25% and said it needed greater clarity on the inflation path before acting; it flagged West Asia tensions, US tariffs, an uneven monsoon and global policy divergence. ### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Should the RBI raise rates now?" / "How does a rate hike affect a manufacturing company's working capital?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Real rate arithmetic with dated numbers, and the supply-vs-demand inflation distinction
> - Both sides: a hike supports the rupee and anchors expectations; it slows credit and investment
> - Sector effects: higher borrowing cost for capex and inventory financing ([[136 Supply Chain Finance & Working Capital]]), rate-sensitive demand (autos, housing)
> - Acknowledges the 7 October outcome may already be known and updates accordingly
> - Avoids claiming certainty about the RBI's next move

## 4. Union Budget 2026-27 and the Fiscal Position
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ fiscal deficit 4.3% target, FY26 at 4.4%, capex ₹12.2 lakh crore, debt-to-GDP 55.6%, new Income Tax Act

### Definition
The **fiscal deficit** is total expenditure minus non-debt receipts, i.e. the amount the government must borrow; it is quoted as a share of GDP. The **revenue deficit** excludes capital spending. **Capital expenditure (capex)** builds assets (roads, railways, ports) and has a higher growth multiplier than revenue spending. The **Union Budget** is presented on 1 February; "BE" is the budget estimate and "RE" the revised estimate. Debt-to-GDP and the borrowing programme influence bond yields, which feed into corporate borrowing costs. Policy context for manufacturing and logistics is in [[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]].

## 5. External Shock: West Asia, Oil, the Rupee and Trade Data
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Hormuz exposure (about 40% of crude, about 90% of LPG imports), crude near $107, rupee about ₹96, FPI outflows

### Definition
India imports about 88% of its petroleum needs, so oil is the main transmission channel from West Asia to inflation, the current account and the rupee. A **weaker rupee** raises import costs (oil, electronics, edible oil) but helps exporters' rupee revenue; the net effect depends on import intensity. The **current account deficit (CAD)** is the gap on goods, services, income and transfers; India usually runs a goods deficit offset by a **services surplus** and remittances. **FPI** (foreign portfolio investor) flows drive short-run currency swings. For operations roles, the channel runs through fuel surcharges, freight and insurance rates, LPG and gas availability, and input costs ([[141 Supply Chain Disruption Case Library (2011-2026)]], [[123 Commodity Price Risk, Hedging & Contract Pricing Mechanisms]], [[015 Supply Chain Risk & Resilience]]).

> - Transmission channels: inflation, current account, rupee, fiscal subsidies, corporate margins
> - Quantifies exposure (88% import dependence, Hormuz share) with dates
> - Business levers: pass-through pricing, fuel surcharge clauses, hedging, mode and route shifts, safety stock for LPG-dependent products
> - Separates winners (exporters, upstream) from losers (import-heavy, airlines, paints, chemicals)
> - Notes data are market prints that change daily

## 6. US Tariffs and the India-US Trade Deal
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ 50% in 2025, 18% deal in Feb 2026, IEEPA struck down, 10% Section 301, deal unsigned

### Definition
A **tariff** is a tax on imports; US presidents have used different legal bases, and the basis matters because courts can strike them down. **IEEPA** (a national-emergency law) was used for the 2025 "reciprocal" and Russian-oil tariffs; **Section 122** allows a temporary balance-of-payments surcharge capped at 15% for 150 days; **Section 301** lets the US Trade Representative impose duties against practices it finds unreasonable and has no automatic expiry. For exporters (textiles, gems and jewellery, auto components, pharma, electronics) the tariff gap versus competitors such as Vietnam or Bangladesh decides orders. Trade documentation and customs context: [[126 International Trade Documentation, Customs & Trade Finance]].

|---|---|---|

| 6–9 Feb 2026 | India-US interim trade deal announced: reciprocal tariff cut from 25% to 18% and the additional 25% removed in recognition of India's commitment to stop buying Russian oil; India to eliminate or reduce tariffs on US industrial goods and many farm products and to buy over $500 billion of US energy, technology, coal and other products; a framework toward a Bilateral Trade Agreement | [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/02/fact-sheet-the-united-states-and-india-announce-historic-trade-deal/) |

## 7. Free Trade Agreements: UK, EFTA and EU
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ UK CETA in force 15 Jul 2026, EFTA TEPA in force 1 Oct 2025, EU FTA concluded 27 Jan 2026, signing pending

### Definition
A **free trade agreement (FTA)** removes or reduces tariffs on most goods between partners and often covers services, investment, rules of origin and standards. Preferential tariffs apply only to goods that meet **rules of origin** (enough local value addition), which is where compliance work for supply-chain teams lies. A **Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA)** and a **Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA)** are FTAs with broader chapters. Concluding a deal, signing it and bringing it into force are different milestones.

|---|---|---|---|

## 12. AI and Data Regulation
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ India AI Governance Guidelines (5 Nov 2025), IT Rules amendment (10 Feb 2026), DPDP Rules (Nov 2025), EU AI Act delays

### Definition
India has taken a **principles-and-sectoral** approach to AI rather than a single AI statute, backed by specific rules on synthetic media and personal data. The **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** is the privacy law; its **Rules** bring it into force in phases. The **IT Rules** govern intermediaries (platforms). The **EU AI Act** classifies AI by risk (prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk transparency, minimal) and applies to Indian firms that sell into the EU, so product and operations teams need a view of both regimes. Related notes: [[166 AI Product Management - LLM Products, Evals & Economics]], [[220 Responsible AI, Explainability & Model Governance]] and [[175 Data Quality, Master Data & Data Governance]].

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Describes India's approach (guidelines plus targeted rules) and the EU's risk-based statute
> - Names concrete obligations (labelling, takedown, consent, breach notification, penalty tiers)
> - Product implications: data minimisation, consent flows, model documentation, human oversight, logging
> - Trade-off: innovation speed vs harm prevention; sandboxes as a middle path
> - Flags that EU dates are provisional and Indian phases run to 2027

## 13. Markets, Corporate Events and Hiring
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Nifty down about 13% in 2026, record IPO quarter, Jio in the pipeline, white-collar hiring up 14%

### Definition
Interviewers (especially for consulting and finance-adjacent roles) like a few current **corporate and market** talking points: how equity markets are doing, which large listings are coming, and how the job market for your profile looks. Always separate **facts** (collections, index levels) from **reports and expectations** (valuations, listing dates). **IPO** terms: **OFS** (offer for sale) means existing holders sell; **public float** is the share of equity held by the public. A fall in the Nifty with record IPO fundraising is a notable divergence: primary issuance continues while secondary-market sentiment is weak.

## 14. ⭐ Advanced: The "Know Before the Interview" Checklist and How to Discuss Opinions
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**Checklist logic:** a refresh routine of 30 minutes the night before an interview beats a week of passive reading. Re-check anything marked Projection, Provisional or Pending in this note, update it, and carry the **fewest numbers you can defend**.

**Opinion framework (60-90 seconds):** (1) **Position** in one sentence; (2) **Evidence**: two dated numbers; (3) **Counter-argument**: the strongest case against you; (4) **So-what** for the sector or company in the room; (5) **What would change my mind**. Avoid party politics and religion, and attribute data to sources rather than to "everyone knows". It is fine to say "as of early October" and to update when the interviewer adds a new fact.

### Example
**Checklist (tick each before the interview):**

- [ ] Repo rate and the **7 October 2026 MPC outcome** (this note was checked before the decision); next MPC date
- [ ] Latest CPI (September print due mid-October) and the RBI inflation projection
- [ ] Latest GDP print (Q2 FY27 due about end-November) and the FY27 forecast range
- [ ] Budget 2026-27 headline: fiscal deficit 4.3%, capex ₹12.2 lakh crore, and two scheme names with outlay
- [ ] USD/INR level and Brent price on the day (market data)
- [ ] US tariff rate now, and whether the India-US deal has been signed
- [ ] FTA status: UK (in force 15 Jul 2026), EFTA (in force 1 Oct 2025), EU (signing and entry-into-force dates)
- [ ] GST: slabs (5/18/40) and the latest monthly collection
- [ ] Manufacturing PMI (latest) and one PLI statistic
- [ ] Logistics cost 7.97% of GDP (FY24 estimate) and one infrastructure platform
- [ ] Non-fossil capacity (300.5 GW at 31 Jul 2026) and the 500 GW target
- [ ] One AI or data regulation (DPDP, IT Rules) with its date
- [ ] One company or sector news item in the firm you are interviewing with (from their results, press release or a reputable business daily)
- [ ] Two numbers for **your own target role** (for example freight cost trends for operations, consulting offer share for consulting, GCC hiring for product)
- [ ] A note of which numbers are projections, so you say "projected" aloud

**Three model answers (condensed):**

*Should the RBI hike?* "My lean is a small, cautious hike if the 7 October decision has not happened, with a clear forward-guidance message. Evidence: CPI is 4.82% against a 4% target and WPI is 9.92% with fuel and power at 22.93%; the real policy rate is only 0.43 points. Counter-argument: the pressure is supply-driven by oil, rate hikes do little for it, and growth is forecast to slow to 6.7%. For an operations business, a hike raises inventory carrying cost, so I would reduce days of inventory and watch supplier credit terms. I would change my mind if oil falls sharply or core inflation stays near 3.9% with food prices easing."

*Is the 10% US tariff good news?* "It is better than the 50% of 2025 and the 18% deal rate, but it is not settled: the legal basis changed from emergency powers to Section 301, and the interim deal is unsigned. For a textile exporter with a 12% margin, a 10% duty absorbs most of the margin unless it is shared with the buyer, so I would diversify towards the UK and EFTA, where preferential access is now live, while keeping US inventory buffers flexible."

*Is oil at $107 a crisis?* "It is a stress, not yet a crisis: crude imports are about 88% of needs, the import bill is up 48% to $74.8 billion for April to August, and the rupee is near 96. Offsets are strong services exports and 7.8% growth. For a logistics company the actions are fuel-surcharge clauses, route and modal optimisation, and customer repricing."

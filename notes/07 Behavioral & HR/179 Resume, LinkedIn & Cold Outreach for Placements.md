## 1. The One-Page MBA Resume: Structure and Formatting
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ one page, reverse-chronological, section order, Indian campus conventions, what to leave out

### Definition
A campus resume is a **one-page evidence sheet** whose only job is to win a 30-second scan and then supply interview questions. Section order for an MBA with a technical background and internships:

1. **Header:** name, phone, professional email, LinkedIn URL (customised), city. No photo (unless the placement-cell format requires one), no date of birth, marital status or full postal address, which add nothing to selection and invite bias.
2. **Education** (first, because B-school students are recruited on the programme): MBA programme, institute, years, CGPA or percentile as the institute reports it; then B.Tech with CGPA; Class XII and X percentages if the format or recruiter asks (many Indian campus formats do).
3. **Work experience and internships:** reverse chronological, 2 to 4 bullets each, every bullet in action-metric-result form (sub-topic 2).
4. **Projects:** two or three, each with problem, method, tools, result.
5. **Leadership and extracurriculars:** roles with scope and outcome.
6. **Skills and certifications:** grouped (Tools: SQL, Python, Excel, Power BI, SAP MM; Methods: Lean, Six Sigma, forecasting), with levels only if verifiable.
7. **Awards and achievements** (merge into the section above if short).

**Formatting rules:** single column; 10 to 11 pt body font; margins about 0.5 to 0.75 inch; one font family; consistent date format (Jun 2025 - Aug 2025); bullets begin with a strong past-tense verb; no tables, text boxes, icons or skill-rating bars; save as PDF unless the portal demands DOCX; file name like `Firstname_Lastname_MBA_SCM.pdf`. **Follow your institute's mandated template** (your placement cell usually fixes the layout) and apply the content rules here inside it. Pair the resume with a consistent story: [[048 Tell Me About Yourself]] and [[051 Internship Learnings & Projects]].

### Example
Skeleton (replace every bracket with a fact you can defend):
```text
FIRSTNAME LASTNAME | Nashik | +91 9XXXXXXXXX | name@gmail.com | linkedin.com/in/yourname

EDUCATION
MBA (Operations & Supply Chain Management), SIOM Nashik            2024 - 2026   CGPA [x.xx]
B.Tech (Computer Science & Engineering), [University]               2020 - 2024   CGPA [x.xx]

INTERNSHIP EXPERIENCE
[Company], [Role]                                                   [Mon YYYY - Mon YYYY]
- [Action verb] [what] using [method/tool], [metric before -> after], [business result]
- ...

PROJECTS
[Project name] (Python, SQL, Power BI)                              [Mon YYYY]
- [Problem in one clause]; [method]; [quantified outcome]

LEADERSHIP | SKILLS & CERTIFICATIONS | ACHIEVEMENTS
```
A reasonable word budget for one page is 450 to 600 words; if you are over, cut the oldest or weakest bullets, not the margins.

### In the news
See news box. With consulting taking 46% of offers at a top IIM and recruiters facing hundreds of applications per role, a clean one-page, evidence-dense format is the baseline expectation.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Walk me through your resume." / "Why is this project on your resume?" / (to the placement cell or in HR rounds) "Why one page?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Every line on the resume is something you can explain in 60 seconds with numbers
> - Order and emphasis chosen for the target role, not chronology alone
> - Knows the institute template and why it exists (comparability across students)
> - Consistent facts across resume, LinkedIn and spoken answers
> - Prepared STAR stories for each bullet ([[049 STAR Stories — Leadership & Conflict]])

## 2. Action-Metric-Result Bullets, with 15 Before/After Examples
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ verb + task + method + number + outcome; baseline and contribution; defensibility test

### Definition
Strong bullets follow **Action - Metric - Result** (also written XYZ: "accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z"):

**[Strong verb] + [what you did] + [how: method or tool] + [metric: baseline to outcome] + [business impact].**

Rules: (1) lead with the **outcome verb** (reduced, built, automated, led), not "responsible for" or "worked on"; (2) give a **baseline and a result** (from 18% to 12%) rather than a bare percentage; (3) **name the method or tool** so the claim is credible and keyword-searchable; (4) state **your contribution** (led, analysed, built) versus the team's; (5) quantify even soft work (people led, outlets, SKUs, plants, hours, rupees); (6) one idea per bullet, 1 to 2 lines.

**Defensibility test for every number:** What was the baseline? How was it measured? Over what period? What was your share? Could you reproduce the calculation on a whiteboard? If not, soften or drop it. Never invent a metric: interviewers probe the numbers first. Relative vs absolute: going from 18% to 12% is a **6-percentage-point** fall and a **33% relative** reduction; state which you mean.

### Example
**Fifteen before/after pairs.** These are **illustrative** (the numbers are fictional, chosen so the arithmetic checks); replace them with your own verified figures from your tracker ([[051 Internship Learnings & Projects]]).

| # | Before (weak) | After (action, metric, result) | Arithmetic check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Responsible for inventory management. | Cut slow-moving stock from ₹46 lakh to ₹36 lakh (22% reduction) across 140 SKUs by re-setting reorder points from 12 months of demand data in Excel. | 10 / 46 = 21.7% |
| 2 | Worked on reducing machine idle time. | Reduced line idle time from 18% to 12% of shift hours (33% relative) by re-sequencing changeovers, recovering about 130 machine-hours a month on a 2,200-hour base. | 6 / 18 = 33.3%; 6% x 2,200 = 132 |
| 3 | Prepared MIS reports. | Automated the weekly MIS pack with Power Query and pivots, cutting preparation from 6 hours to 1.5 hours (75%) and removing copy-paste errors across 9 plants. | 4.5 / 6 = 75% |
| 4 | Coordinated with suppliers. | Raised on-time delivery of 12 key suppliers from 78% to 91% (+13 points) in 4 months using weekly scorecards and escalation calls. | 91 - 78 = 13 |
| 5 | Handled logistics planning. | Merged 3 part-load routes into 2 full-truck routes, lowering freight cost 9% (₹3.4 lakh a month on a ₹38 lakh base). | 3.4 / 38 = 8.9% |
| 6 | Did data analysis using SQL. | Wrote SQL (CTEs, window functions) on a 2.3 million-row order table to show that 14% of SKUs caused 61% of late deliveries; the finding changed safety stock for 40 SKUs. | counts as stated |
| 7 | Made dashboards in Power BI. | Built a Power BI OTIF dashboard used by 25 plant managers; it replaced 3 manual Excel reports and saved about 8 hours a week. | counts as stated |
| 8 | Did market research for a project. | Ran 30 expert interviews and a 120-retailer survey; sized the addressable market at ₹8,000 crore (40,000 outlets x ₹20 lakh annual spend) and shaped a two-city pilot. | 40,000 x 20 lakh = ₹8,000 crore |
| 9 | Participated in a case competition. | Placed in the top 5 of 312 teams (top 1.6%) in a national case competition, presenting a margin-recovery plan to a panel of 4 industry judges. | 5 / 312 = 1.6% |
| 10 | Helped with product features. | Prioritised a 12-story backlog with RICE and shipped a checkout redesign that lifted completion from 62% to 68% (+6 points, +9.7% relative) in an A/B test of 18,000 users. | 6 / 62 = 9.7% |
| 11 | Conducted user interviews. | Ran 15 user interviews and synthesised 4 pain points; the top one (delivery-slot visibility) became the sprint goal and cut support tickets 18%. | counts as stated |
| 12 | Managed a college event. | Led a 40-member team to deliver a ₹6 lakh event for 1,200 attendees on schedule and 4% under budget (₹24,000 saved). | 4% of 6 lakh = ₹24,000 |
| 13 | Monitored project schedule. | Built a CPM schedule for a 38-activity plant relocation, identified a 21-day critical path and re-sequenced 3 tasks to finish 4 days early. | counts as stated |
| 14 | Member of the operations club. | Organised 6 supply-chain workshops for 250 students and grew club membership 35% (120 to 162). | 162 / 120 = 1.35 |
| 15 | Good at Excel and Python. | Automated a demand-forecast refresh in Python (pandas, statsmodels) for 85 SKUs, cutting MAPE from 24% to 17% (7 points) and refresh time from 2 days to 20 minutes. | 7 / 24 = 29% relative |

How to convert your own work: list the task, then ask "how many, how much, how fast, how often, compared with what?" Before-and-after pairs (baseline to result) beat single numbers, and rupee impact beats percentages when you can defend it. Tools referenced here are covered in [[043 Advanced Excel (Pivot, Solver, Forecasting)]], [[045 SQL for Operations Analytics]], [[044 Power BI & DAX]] and [[046 Python for Operations]].

### In the news
See news box. AI sourcing tools rank profiles on specific, searchable evidence (tools, methods, outcomes), which is exactly what action-metric-result bullets supply.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "You say you reduced idle time by a third. How did you measure it, and what exactly did you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Baseline, method, period and sample for the number
> - Clear individual contribution vs team effort
> - A one-sentence cause-and-effect story (problem, action, result, learning)
> - Honest limits (pilot only, estimate, single plant)
> - Consistency with what the guide or company mentor would say if asked

## 3. ATS Basics: How Software Reads Your Resume
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ parsing, keywords in context, knockout questions, myth of auto-reject, formatting that breaks parsers

### Definition
An **applicant tracking system (ATS)** (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, Taleo, Naukri and company portals) stores applications, parses resumes into fields (name, employers, dates, skills), and lets recruiters search, filter and track candidates. Parsing quality depends on layout; **text in headers and footers, tables, multi-column layouts, text boxes, images and icons** may parse incorrectly or be dropped, whereas a simple single-column document with standard headings parses well.

What actually happens: recruiters mostly **search and filter** (by skills, years, institute, location) and read the results; some firms apply **knockout questions** (work authorisation, minimum CGPA, a required certification) and, increasingly, AI-based match scores. The viral claim that "75% of resumes are auto-rejected by ATS" is poorly sourced; the real bottleneck is volume and human time (see news box, with its small-sample caveat). On campus the "ATS" is often the **placement portal and the company's shortlisting by CGPA, branch and profile fit**, so eligibility criteria matter before the resume is read.

Practical rules: use **standard headings** (Education, Experience, Projects, Skills); put **keywords in context** (bullets that show SQL or SAP MM in use, not a keyword dump); mirror the job description's wording when true (supply planning, S&OP, OTIF, RICE); spell out acronyms once; avoid graphics and skill bars; keep contact details in the body, not a header; export a text-selectable PDF (not a scanned image); test by copy-pasting the PDF into a plain text editor and checking the order.

### Example
Job description phrase: "demand planning, S&OP, forecast accuracy, SAP APO/IBP, advanced Excel". A resume bullet that survives both a keyword search and a human read: "Improved SKU-level forecast accuracy from 76% to 83% (+7 points) by testing moving-average and exponential-smoothing models in Excel for an S&OP review of 85 SKUs." It contains the exact terms (forecast accuracy, S&OP, Excel) in a context that proves use, with a baseline and result. Contrast the keyword dump: "Skills: demand planning, S&OP, SAP, APO, IBP, Excel, forecasting, analytics, leadership, teamwork." (no proof, no context, and ranks no better with a human reader). Check: 83 - 76 = 7 points. Related: [[004 Demand Forecasting & Planning]], [[198 SAP IBP, APO & Demand-Driven Planning]].

### In the news
See news box. LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant plugs into ATS platforms such as Greenhouse and Workday, so the profile and the ATS record are increasingly searched together by AI; consistency between the two matters.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> (Often in HR or career-cell sessions) "How do you make your resume ATS-friendly?" / "How do you tailor your resume to a job description?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Single-column, standard headings, selectable PDF, no graphics or text boxes
> - Keywords in context mirrored from the job description, without exaggeration
> - Knows ATS parse and filter, with a human reading the shortlist
> - Knockout and eligibility criteria checked before applying
> - Applies early and tracks applications

## 4. Tailoring by Role: Consulting, Product, Operations, Project Management and Analytics
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ same facts, different emphasis; keyword set per role; bullet pattern per role

### Definition
Keep **one master resume** with all verified facts, then create a one-page **variant per role family**. Change the order of bullets, the verbs and the keywords, never the facts.

| Role family | What recruiters scan for | Bullet pattern | Keywords and artefacts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consulting | Structured thinking, quantified impact, leadership, communication, academics | "Diagnosed X, recommended Y, achieved Z"; scope (team size, ₹ value, stakeholders) | Hypothesis-driven, market sizing, profitability, case competition rank, client or leadership roles |
| Product management | User focus, shipped outcomes, data and tech fluency, prioritisation | "Discovered pain point, prioritised, shipped, metric moved" | User research, A/B test, RICE, PRD, funnel, retention, SQL, APIs ([[163 PM Interview Types & Answer Frameworks]]) |
| Operations and supply chain | Cost, service level, inventory, throughput, process discipline, plant exposure | "Reduced [cost/time/stock] from A to B using [method]" | OEE, OTIF, inventory turns, S&OP, Lean, Six Sigma, SAP MM/PP, Kaizen, 5S |
| Project management | Scope, schedule, budget, risk, stakeholders, delivery on time | "Delivered [project] on time and within budget; managed risks" | WBS, CPM, Gantt, RAID log, MS Project, Jira, PMP or CAPM ([[171 Project Management Interview Questions & Numericals]]) |
| Analytics and data | Tools plus applied business outcomes | "Built [model/dashboard/pipeline] that did [outcome]" | SQL, Python, Power BI, Tableau, forecasting, regression, GitHub link |

**Tailoring workflow (15 minutes per application):** read the job description and mark the top 8 requirements; map each to a bullet from your master resume; reorder so those bullets sit first; adjust verbs and keywords; update the optional two-line summary; re-check one page; save a version name. For consulting, check the firm's guidance on format and include the case-competition and leadership evidence ([[161 Consulting Firms & Recruitment Landscape in India]], [[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]).

### Example
One internship fact, three tailored bullets (illustrative numbers): "Idle time at the packaging line fell from 18% to 12%."
- **Operations:** "Reduced packaging-line idle time from 18% to 12% of shift hours by re-sequencing changeovers and standardising shift handover (Lean, time study)."
- **Consulting:** "Diagnosed the root cause of 6 points of lost capacity on a packaging line through time studies and a Pareto of stoppages; recommended a changeover re-sequence adopted by 3 shifts."
- **Analytics:** "Analysed 4 weeks of line-stoppage logs in Python and Excel (Pareto, control chart) to isolate changeover as the largest loss and quantify a 6-point idle-time reduction."
Same fact, three emphases. Each is defensible because it describes work you actually did.

### In the news
See news box. Consulting dominates campus offers (46% at IIM Bangalore), automotive and FMCG are among the strongest hiring sectors in the August 2026 Naukri index (+19% and +10%), and AI/ML and GCC roles are growing, so several role families are open to a CSE-plus-operations profile; tailor, do not scatter.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "You have applied to consulting, operations and product roles. What is your story?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A coherent thread (technical and operations strength turned into decisions and impact)
> - Role-specific evidence for each family without contradicting the facts
> - Reasoned prioritisation and a primary target
> - Consistency between the resume version submitted and the story told
> - Awareness of what each recruiter values

## 5. Projects, Certifications, Internships and Extracurriculars
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ project write-up template, certifications that help, "in progress" honesty, leadership with scope

### Definition
**Projects** carry weight when internships are limited: each project entry needs a **problem, data, method, tools, result and your role**. **Certifications** support (not replace) evidence; list only those completed (or "in progress, expected Mon YYYY") and relevant to the target. Reasonable choices for an Operations and SCM MBA with a technical base: Lean Six Sigma Green Belt, an ASCM credential (CSCP, CPIM; see [[144 SCM Body of Knowledge Map - CSCP, CPIM, CLTD & SCPro]]), PMI CAPM or PMP (for project roles), SAP module certification or training ([[079 SAP Fundamentals & Architecture]]), Microsoft Power BI Data Analyst, SQL or Python courses with a graded project. **Extracurriculars** should show scope: team size, budget, audience, result, not only the title.

**Project write-up template (3 bullets):**
1. **Problem and data:** one clause on the business problem and the dataset (size, source).
2. **Method:** the analytical approach and tools (SQL, Python, Power BI, Solver).
3. **Result and recommendation:** quantified outcome or insight, and your role.

Honest-claims rule: a certification you cannot discuss for five minutes is a liability. A project you cannot reproduce is a liability. Academic and club projects are legitimate as long as they are labelled correctly.

### Example
Three project entries a CSE-plus-Ops candidate might carry (illustrative numbers):
- **Inventory ABC-XYZ segmentation (Python, Excel):** Classified 1,200 SKUs by value (ABC) and demand variability (XYZ) using a public retail dataset; found that 18% of SKUs (A class) carried 78% of sales value and recommended differentiated review cycles; published as a notebook on GitHub. (Check: 18% of 1,200 = 216 SKUs.) Concept link: [[003 Inventory Management]].
- **OTIF analytics dashboard (SQL, Power BI):** Wrote SQL on a sample order and delivery database (the schema and queries in [[181 SQL Interview Problem Bank]]) and built a dashboard showing OTIF by carrier and month, with a drill-through to failing orders.
- **Warehouse slotting simulation (Python, SimPy):** Simulated pick-path distance under ABC slotting vs random slotting for a 5,000-location layout; slotting cut average travel distance 22% in the model (state that it is a simulation). See [[068 Operations-Specific Python (PuLP, SimPy)]].
Certification line: "Lean Six Sigma Green Belt (completed Mon YYYY, [issuer]); ASCM CSCP (in progress, exam planned Mon YYYY)".

### In the news
See news box. With fresher hiring up 15% and AI/ML roles up 31% in August 2026 (Naukri JobSpeak), recruiters have more openings but also more applicants, and visible, verifiable project work is a common tie-breaker for a candidate whose internship record is short.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Tell me about your best project." / "Why this certification? What did you learn?" / "What was your specific contribution to the club event?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Problem, method, result, learning, and what you would do differently
> - A clear individual contribution and the tools used
> - Honest labelling of academic or simulated work
> - Evidence of depth (can answer one level of follow-up)
> - Relevance to the target role

## 6. The LinkedIn Profile Checklist
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ headline, About, Experience, Featured, skills, URL, photo, settings, activity, consistency with resume

### Definition
Recruiters and alumni check LinkedIn **before** replying to a cold message, and AI sourcing tools search it (see news box). The profile should match the resume and be easy to find with the words recruiters search for. Checklist:

- [ ] **Photo:** clear, professional, face visible, plain background, recent. Add a simple banner.
- [ ] **Headline (220 characters):** target role and proof, not just "MBA student". Pattern: `MBA (Ops & SCM) | B.Tech CSE | Supply chain analytics, SQL, Python | Seeking operations and consulting roles`.
- [ ] **Custom URL:** `linkedin.com/in/firstname-lastname`, used on the resume.
- [ ] **About (first 2 lines matter most):** 3 to 5 short paragraphs or lines: who you are, what you do, 2 proof points with numbers, what you are looking for. First person is normal on LinkedIn.
- [ ] **Experience:** each internship with 2 to 4 action-metric-result bullets identical in facts to the resume; add media links where allowed.
- [ ] **Education:** institute, programme, dates, activities and societies.
- [ ] **Projects and Featured:** pin the best project, dashboard, GitHub repo or published article.
- [ ] **Skills:** 15 to 25, with the top 3 aligned to your target role; seek endorsements from teammates and mentors.
- [ ] **Certifications:** with issuer and credential link.
- [ ] **Recommendations:** 2 to 3 from managers, professors or club leads (ask specifically).
- [ ] **Settings:** open to work (visible to recruiters only if you prefer), location, industry, "open to" roles; public profile on.
- [ ] **Activity:** follow target companies and 20 relevant people; comment thoughtfully on 2 posts a week; post occasionally about a project or learning (a short post on an analysis, not self-praise).
- [ ] **Consistency:** dates, titles and numbers match the resume; no spelling errors.
- [ ] **Privacy hygiene:** no confidential employer data; no personal phone number in public About.

**Search visibility:** put role terms where recruiters search: headline, About, skills and job titles (Supply Chain Analyst, Operations Intern, Demand Planning). A career-change profile should show the new direction in headline and About, otherwise AI sourcing may not surface it (a point made in the BusinessToday article about LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant).

### Example
Before and after "About" opening (illustrative):
- Before: "MBA student at SIOM Nashik. Hardworking and passionate about operations."
- After: "I turn operations data into decisions. CSE engineer turned MBA (Ops & SCM) with internship experience in manufacturing: cut line idle time from 18% to 12% and automated a 9-plant weekly MIS pack from 6 hours to 1.5. I work with SQL, Python and Power BI and am looking for supply chain analytics and operations roles."
The second version names the value, proof, tools and target role in about 50 words, and every number is checkable (idle time 6 points; MIS 75% faster).

### In the news
See news box. LinkedIn is positioning AI agents as the first reader of profiles; a complete, keyword-aware profile with proof statements is cheap insurance.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Is your LinkedIn up to date? Walk me through your profile." / (by alumni) "Why should I help you?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Profile matches the resume, tells a consistent role story and includes proof points
> - Active but not spammy: thoughtful comments and an occasional project post
> - Recommendations and certifications that can be verified
> - Awareness that profile text is searchable by recruiters and AI tools
> - Professional tone and a clear ask (role types, locations)

## 7. Networking and Alumni Outreach: Strategy and Tracker
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ who to contact, why alumni, give-first, 15-minute calls, tracker, funnel arithmetic, platform limits

### Definition
Most good outcomes come from **warm, specific, low-ask conversations**: information calls, not job requests. Start with **alumni** (shared institute, high reply rates), then second-degree connections, then practitioners who posted on your topic. Make the first ask small (15 minutes, two questions), show you did homework, and **give first** (a relevant article, a thoughtful comment, a short summary of your analysis). Respect time, never attach a resume to the first message, and say thank you.

**Process:** (1) build a target list of 30 to 50 companies by role; (2) find 3 to 5 people per company (alumni, role-holders, hiring managers); (3) send personalised connection notes; (4) follow with a short message on acceptance; (5) hold 15-minute calls with prepared questions; (6) send a thank-you the same day; (7) follow up after 7 to 10 days with progress; (8) log everything in a tracker. LinkedIn platform limits (third-party summary dated 1 June 2026, so verify): connection notes are capped at 300 characters, free accounts get only about 5 personalised notes a month, and most accounts can send roughly 100 to 200 invitations a week; do not automate or mass-send.

**Funnel arithmetic (a planning assumption, not a benchmark):** 40 notes x 30% acceptance = 12 conversations opened; x about 40% reply with time = about 5 calls; 1 or 2 of those may lead to a referral or advice that changes an application. Plan volume accordingly and start early.

### Example
Tracker columns (a spreadsheet is enough): Name | Company | Role | Link (alumni, shared interest) | Date sent | Status (sent, accepted, replied, call booked, call done) | Next step and date | Notes (what you learned) | Thank-you sent | Referral asked? Review it every Sunday and clear the "next step" list.

Prepared questions for a 15-minute call: How did you get into [function]? What does a typical week look like? Which skills matter most in the first year? What do hiring managers look for in freshers? What surprised you? Is there something I should read or build to be a stronger candidate? Close with: "Is there anyone else you think I should speak to?" (an introduction is worth more than a referral request). Conversation topics: [[161 Consulting Firms & Recruitment Landscape in India]] for firm context and [[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]] for your story.

### In the news
See news box. LinkedIn says its AI assistant lifts InMail acceptance by 66% for recruiters who use it (company claim), which means recruiters will be messaging candidates more precisely; well-targeted, personalised messages from candidates are the equal and opposite effort.

## 8. Cold Outreach Templates: Connection Notes, Messages and Emails
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ short, specific, one ask, character counts, subject lines, follow-up cadence

### Definition
A good cold message has five parts: **who you are (one line), why them (specific), what you ask (small), why it is easy to say yes (time-bound, flexible), thanks.** Avoid: "I need a job", mass-copy text, attachments in the first message, flattery without specifics, asking for a referral from a stranger, long paragraphs, and demands. Use a real subject line for email. Character counts below are for the filled sample text (LinkedIn connection notes allow 300 characters per third-party sources; keep notes under 300).

### Example
**Template A: connection note to an alumnus (262 characters).**
> Hi Priya, I am Rohan, an MBA (Operations & SCM) student at SIOM Nashik. I noticed you moved from plant operations into supply chain planning at your company. I would value 15 minutes to learn how you made that move. Happy to work around your schedule. Thank you!

**Template B: connection note to a practitioner without a shared institute (207 characters).**
> Hello Mr Kulkarni, I am an MBA student at SIOM Nashik exploring supply chain analytics roles. Your network design work is very relevant to my goals. I would be glad to connect and learn from your experience.

**Template C: message after the connection is accepted (about 300 characters).**
> Thank you for connecting, Priya. I am preparing for operations and supply chain roles and read your post on S&OP. Could I ask two questions about your move into planning? A 15-minute call this week or next, at a time that suits you, would help a lot. If you are busy, even a reply here would be valued.

**Template D: cold email (alumnus or hiring manager).**
> Subject: SIOM Nashik MBA student: 15 minutes on supply chain planning careers
>
> Dear Mr/Ms [Surname],
>
> I am [Name], a first-year MBA student (Operations & SCM) at SIOM Nashik with a B.Tech in Computer Science. I found your profile through [alumni directory / your article on X]. In my internship at [company] I [one-line result, for example reduced line idle time from 18% to 12%], and I am now exploring [function] roles.
>
> Could I ask you two questions about [specific topic] in a 15-minute call? I am flexible on time and can share what I am working on beforehand.
>
> Thank you for considering it.
> [Name] | [phone] | [LinkedIn URL]

**Template E: follow-up after 7 to 10 days with no reply (one nudge only).**
> Hi Priya, a quick follow-up in case my earlier note got buried. I have since [built a small S&OP analysis / completed X], which I would be glad to share. If it is not a good time, no problem at all. Thank you.

**Template F: thank-you after a call (same day).**
> Thank you for your time today, Priya. Your advice on [specific point] was very useful, and I will [action you will take]. I will update you once I have done it. Regards, Rohan.

**Cadence:** send, wait 7 to 10 days, one follow-up, then stop. Log outcomes. Never send the same text to everyone: change the "why them" line each time.

### In the news
See news box. Recruiters now have AI tools that draft and send personalised messages at scale, so generic candidate messages will blend into the noise; specificity (a named project, a post, a shared institute) is the differentiator.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Draft a message to an alumnus asking for guidance." / "How do you follow up when someone does not reply?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Short, specific, a single small ask with a time box
> - "Why you" evidence, and no resume attachment at first contact
> - One polite follow-up and then stop
> - Thank-you and a progress update to close the loop
> - Appropriate tone for seniority, and platform-compliant behaviour

## 9. Referral Requests
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ when to ask, make it easy, what to send, internal portal etiquette, how to follow through

### Definition
A **referral** means an employee submits or endorses your application through the company's internal system, often moving it into a recruiter's queue and sometimes fast-tracking it. Many companies run employee referral programmes with bonuses, so employees can be willing, but they risk their credibility, so the request must be **specific, easy and fair**. Ask **after** a conversation or a prior connection, not as the first message, and only for roles you genuinely fit.

**Make it easy:** give the job ID or link, your resume (PDF) and a **3-line fit summary** (role fit, one proof point, one relevant tool), say whether you have already applied, and offer to answer questions. Also: accept a "no" gracefully (some firms restrict referrals for campus drives), and update the person on the outcome. If you receive a referral, apply through the link they provide so it is tracked, and do not bypass it.

### Example
**Template R: referral request to a connection at a target company (about 415 characters).**
> Hi Anil, hope you are well. I am applying for the Supply Chain Analyst role (Job ID 12345) at your company. My background: B.Tech (CSE), MBA (Ops & SCM, SIOM Nashik), and an internship where I cut line idle time from 18% to 12%. Would you be comfortable referring me? I have attached my resume and a 3-line summary so it takes you under two minutes. Happy to answer any questions, and no worries if this is not possible.

**Three-line fit summary to attach (about 60 words):** "Role fit: supply chain analytics with SQL, Python and Power BI. Proof: reduced line idle time from 18% to 12% and automated a 9-plant weekly MIS pack from 6 hours to 1.5 (illustrative numbers; use your own). Relevant tools: SQL window functions, Power BI OTIF dashboard, SAP MM basics."

**Referral etiquette:** do not ask more than one person per company to refer you to the same role; never share an employee's name on your resume; thank them; do not ask for a referral for a role you could not defend in an interview. Interview preparation after a referral should be stronger, since your referrer's reputation is attached ([[143 SCM Interview Question Bank]], [[158 Operations Management Interview Question Bank & Numericals]]).

### In the news
See news box. As AI screening tools such as LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant plug into the same ATS platforms, a referral is a human signal that complements the algorithmic profile ranking.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you ask someone for a referral? What if they decline?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Builds a relationship first; asks after a conversation, for a role that fits
> - Makes it easy: job ID, resume, 3-line summary, deadline
> - Graceful with a "no" and grateful with a "yes"
> - Applies as instructed and follows up with an update
> - Understands the referrer's reputational stake

## 10. GitHub and Portfolio for Analytics and Operations Roles
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ three strong repos, README template, notebooks and SQL, dashboards, data privacy, link hygiene

### Definition
A portfolio is **proof of skill** for analytics, product and technical operations roles, and a differentiator for a CSE-plus-MBA profile. Quality beats quantity: **three well-documented projects** beat twenty empty repositories. Each repo should have a **README** that a recruiter can read in 60 seconds: problem, data (source, size, licence), approach, results (a chart or number), how to run, and what you would do next. Keep code readable (functions, comments where non-obvious), pin the best three repositories on your profile, and use a clean commit history.

**Suggested projects for an Ops and SCM profile:** (1) forecasting and inventory policy on a public dataset ([[066 Demand Forecasting & Time Series]], [[046 Python for Operations]]); (2) a SQL analytics project on a sales-and-delivery schema with OTIF, ABC and cohort queries ([[181 SQL Interview Problem Bank]], [[061 SQL for SCM & Business Analytics]]); (3) a Power BI or Tableau dashboard published publicly ([[044 Power BI & DAX]], [[172 Tableau & Looker Studio - BI Tool Comparison]], [[047 MIS & Dashboard Design]]); optional: an optimisation or simulation model ([[068 Operations-Specific Python (PuLP, SimPy)]]) and a data-cleaning and EDA notebook ([[186 Python Data Cleaning & EDA Playbook]]).

**Data and privacy rules:** never publish confidential company data; use public datasets (government open data portals, Kaggle) or synthetic data and say so; remove API keys and personal data from notebooks and history; give credit for datasets and tutorials.

### Example
README skeleton:
```markdown
# Inventory ABC-XYZ Segmentation for a Retail Dataset
**Problem:** Which SKUs deserve tight control vs a relaxed review cycle?
**Data:** Public retail transactions (about 540,000 rows, 1,200 SKUs; source and licence linked).
**Approach:** ABC by revenue (80/15/5), XYZ by coefficient of variation (<0.5, 0.5-1.0, >1.0); SQL for aggregation, pandas for segmentation.
**Results:** 18% of SKUs (216) carried 78% of sales; AX items need weekly review; CZ items are candidates for stock-outs by design.
**Run it:** `pip install -r requirements.txt` then open `notebooks/01_segmentation.ipynb`.
**Next steps:** safety-stock by segment; compare with a service-level policy.
```
(Illustrative numbers; use your real results. Check: 216 / 1,200 = 18%.) Add a link to the live dashboard or notebook on the resume and LinkedIn Featured section, and test the links in a private browser window.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked

> [!tip] Strong answer includes

## 11. Red Flags and the Final Quality Check
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ typos, vague verbs, inflated claims, formatting traps, mismatches, gaps, authenticity

### Definition
Recruiters reject quickly on **avoidable signals**. Use this audit before every submission:

| Red flag | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Typos, inconsistent dates or fonts | Signals low attention to detail (fatal for ops and consulting roles) | Read aloud; check dates against LinkedIn; run a spell-check in English (India) |
| "Responsible for", "worked on", "helped" | No evidence of outcome | Rewrite as action-metric-result |
| Unsupported or inflated numbers | Interviewers probe and the lie unravels | Apply the defensibility test; use ranges or "approx." honestly |
| Two pages, tables, columns, icons, skill bars, photo | Parsing and readability problems; against most campus formats | One page, single column, plain text |
| Keyword dump or 25 tools | Looks like padding | 8 to 12 tools you can defend, shown in bullets |
| Unexplained gaps | Raises questions | One line explanation (exam preparation, family, project) and prepare the story |
| Generic text or AI phrasing across all bullets | Sounds inauthentic and is easy to spot | Use your own words and specifics |
| Irrelevant hobbies or long personal details | Wastes space | Keep one line of real interests that spark conversation |
| Mismatch between resume, LinkedIn and the form | Credibility issue | Keep one source of truth and update all three |
| Broken or private links | Wasted proof | Test links in a private window |
| Unprofessional email or file name | First impression | Firstname.Lastname@..., `Name_Role.pdf` |
| Confidential employer data | Ethical and legal risk | Use anonymised or public figures |

**Final check (5 minutes):** one page; every bullet starts with a verb and has a number or a clear outcome; every number is defensible; role keywords from the job description appear naturally; links work; the PDF text is selectable; the file name is professional; facts match LinkedIn.

### Example
Audit one bullet: "Worked on supply chain optimisation using Excel and helped to reduce costs significantly in the company (team of 5)." Problems: vague verb, no baseline, "significantly" is not a number, tool not tied to a result, no individual contribution. Rewrite: "Cut outbound freight cost 9% (₹3.4 lakh a month on a ₹38 lakh base) by merging 3 part-load routes into 2 full-truck routes; built the Excel route-load model and presented it to the plant head." (Check: 3.4 / 38 = 8.9%.) The rewritten bullet answers the five defensibility questions before they are asked.

### In the news
See news box. With 400–600 applications per entry-level role (Enhancv recruiter study) and AI tools shrinking the number of profiles recruiters read (LinkedIn's claimed 80% reduction), small credibility flaws can decide shortlists.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What are the biggest mistakes in resumes you see?" / "Your resume says you led a team of 40. How did you manage conflict?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Awareness of common flags and a personal pre-submission checklist
> - Every claim backed by a story and numbers
> - Calmly handles probing and admits limits honestly
> - Applies resume discipline to LinkedIn and the GitHub profile
> - Understands that a resume is a promise the interview must keep ([[052 Strengths, Weaknesses, Failures]])

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: Using AI Tools Well, and Measuring Your Application Funnel
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**AI tools** (large language models, resume checkers) are useful for **editing**, not for **inventing**. Good uses: tighten wording, check grammar, suggest stronger verbs, convert a messy list of tasks into bullet drafts that **you** then fill with true numbers, rehearse questions on your resume, and compare your resume with a job description to find gaps. Bad uses: letting a tool create metrics, achievements or skills you do not have (a hallucinated number becomes your lie in the interview), pasting confidential employer data into public tools, and sending identical AI-written outreach to many people. Recruiters increasingly use AI too (see news box), and generic phrasing is easy to spot.

**Measure your funnel** like a project: applications sent, shortlists, interviews, offers; messages sent, replies, calls, referrals. Compute conversion rates per stage and learn where you lose (for example 40 applications, 6 shortlists = 15% shortlist rate; low shortlist rates point to resume or targeting, low interview-to-offer points to interview skills). Review weekly and adjust one variable at a time (resume version, role family, message text). Plan for **timing**: many recruiters review in order received, and early applications improve visibility (Enhancv study in the news box).

### Example
A weekly review table (illustrative numbers): Week 3: 40 outreach notes, 14 accepted (35%), 6 replies (43% of accepted), 4 calls, 1 referral; 12 applications, 3 shortlists (25%), 1 interview. Reading it: acceptance and reply rates are healthy, so the notes work; the shortlist rate is acceptable; the bottleneck is converting interviews, so spend the next week on case, SQL and HR preparation ([[177 Group Discussions & Extempore for MBA Placements]], [[178 HR Round, Offers & Salary Negotiation in India]], [[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]). Checks: 14 / 40 = 35%; 6 / 14 = 42.9%; 3 / 12 = 25%.

Prompting pattern that keeps you honest: "Here are my real facts: [list with numbers]. Rewrite as 3 one-line resume bullets in action-metric-result form. Do not add any numbers or tools that are not in my list."

### In the news
See news box. LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant and similar tools make recruiter-side AI standard; the candidate-side counter is authentic, specific, verifiable content and a disciplined, measured outreach process.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Did you use AI to write your resume? How do you make sure everything on it is accurate?" / "How would you manage a large job-search pipeline?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Uses AI for editing and rehearsal, never for facts; verifies every number against evidence
> - Protects confidential data and writes in own voice
> - Tracks a funnel with stage-wise conversion and iterates one variable at a time
> - Applies early and personalises outreach
> - Can explain every line of the resume without notes

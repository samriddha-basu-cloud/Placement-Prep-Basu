## 1. The HR Round: Purpose, Format and What Is Really Being Tested
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ screening for fit, stability, expectations and risk; typical sequence; consistency checks; closing the offer

### Definition
The HR round (often the last) tests things technical panels cannot: **motivation and fit, stability and mobility, salary expectation and negotiability, attitude and integrity, and offer-acceptance risk**. In campus hiring it is usually short (15 to 30 minutes) and may be a formality after panel selection, but red flags (inflexibility, dishonesty, hostile attitude) can still end an offer. In lateral hiring it also decides the compensation band.

Typical sequence: icebreaker and self-introduction ([[048 Tell Me About Yourself]]) → "why us / why this role" ([[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]) → strengths, weaknesses and failures ([[052 Strengths, Weaknesses, Failures]]) → relocation, shifts, travel, bond, notice → salary and other offers → your questions.

Principles for every answer: **truthful, consistent with your resume, positive in framing, specific, and flexible where it is cheap to be flexible.** Prepare facts first (current CTC and its structure, notice period, other processes you are in, your joining constraints) so that answers are calm and accurate.

### Example
An interviewer asks, "You said you want a consulting career, but you also applied to FMCG. Why?" Weak: "I am keeping my options open." Strong: "My interest is in solving operations problems with data. Consulting offers variety and speed; the FMCG route offers depth and ownership of a supply chain. My preference is consulting, and that is why I prepared case work for six months, but I applied to FMCG because I value the operations learning." This is honest, shows a priority and does not insult either employer.

### In the news
See news box. In a tighter fresher market, HR rounds also test whether you will actually join and stay: clarity about your constraints and commitment helps recruiters hold your seat.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Tell me about yourself and why you want to join us. What are you looking for in your first job?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A concise story that links background, skills and the role
> - Specific reasons for this company and role
> - Priorities you can defend (learning, ownership, growth)
> - Honesty about alternatives without negativity
> - A calm, confident close with a question for the interviewer

## 2. HR Question Bank I: Why Leaving, Strengths, Relocation, Shifts, Bond and Notice
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ model answer structures for the most repeated questions; do not badmouth; flexible but not naive

### Definition
Use these structures (adapt with your facts):

| Question | Strong structure | What to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **"Why are you leaving your current job?" (lateral)** | Pull not push: what you want to learn or do next; gratitude for the current role; one reason tied to the new role | Blaming manager or salary only |
| **"Your strengths?"** | 2 strengths, each with a short proof and relevance (see [[052 Strengths, Weaknesses, Failures]]) | Generic lists ("hardworking, team player") with no proof |
| **"Are you willing to relocate?"** | "Yes. I am open to [locations]; I have one constraint: [honest, if any] and I will plan around it." | Saying yes and later refusing |
| **"Shifts, travel, plant or field postings?"** | Willingness plus curiosity about the rotation and learning plan | "Only metro, only day shift" |
| **"Bond or service agreement?"** | Ask the terms (duration, amount, conditions, whether it applies to training); "I am comfortable if it is reasonable and the programme invests in my development." | Rejecting outright; or agreeing blindly (read the clauses, see sub-topic 14) |
| **"Notice period?"** | Exact period, flexibility and buyout possibilities ("my notice is 60 days; my company often releases earlier on handover") | Overpromising an early release you cannot guarantee |
| **"Where do you see yourself in five years?"** | Skill and role progression aligned to the company's ladder; humility | "In your chair" or vague ambition |
| **"Any questions for us?"** | 2 to 3 thoughtful questions (first-year learning plan, how performance is measured, rotation policy) | "What is the salary?" as the first question |

### Example
Question: "Would you accept a plant posting in a small town?" Answer: "Yes. For an operations role the plant is where the learning is. I would like to understand the first-year learning plan and the usual rotation to other locations. I have done my internship at a plant, so I am comfortable with shift-based work." If you truly cannot (family or health), state it early and politely; hiding a constraint is worse than declaring it.

### In the news
See news box. Fixed-term and conditional contracts are more common after the labour codes; asking clarifying questions about contract type and bond terms is normal and professional.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "We have a two-year service bond for this programme. Is that a problem for you?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Acceptance conditional on understanding terms (amount, duration, exit options)
> - Interest in the investment the company makes in your development
> - No emotional reaction or threats
> - A follow-up request to see the clause in writing
> - Awareness that bonds must be reasonable to be enforceable (consult a lawyer for specifics)

## 3. Salary Questions: Expected CTC, Current CTC and Other Offers
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ campus (fixed bands) vs lateral (negotiable); anchoring; ranges; honesty about current pay; disclosing other offers

### Definition
**Campus:** pay is usually a fixed band per role (decided before the process); "expected salary" questions are typically answered with "I am comfortable with the company's standard offer for this role; I care most about the learning and the role." Do not push for numbers that the placement process does not allow.

**Lateral:** compensation is negotiable. Preparation:
1. Know your **current structure**: fixed, variable (target vs actual), joining bonus, ESOPs/RSUs, benefits (see sub-topic 4).
2. Research **market range** (peers, recruiters, public data points; the news box gives one top-campus anchor).
3. State a **range** whose lower end you would accept, anchored on market value and role scope, not on a percentage of your current CTC alone.
4. Talk **total compensation** and what is guaranteed vs variable.

**Honesty rules:** state current CTC accurately (background checks and payslips can verify); you can say "I would prefer to discuss the value of the role and my expectation rather than anchor on my current pay," but do not inflate. For **other offers**, it is fine to say you are in other processes, with timelines, and to share an offer only if it is real. Never invent offers.

### Example
Lateral candidate with fixed ₹12 lakh and target variable ₹1.2 lakh. Recruiter asks, "What are you expecting?" Answer: "My current fixed is ₹12 lakh with a ₹1.2 lakh target variable. For the scope you described, ownership of a regional planning team, my expectation is ₹15 to 16.5 lakh fixed, depending on variable and benefits." A 30% hike gives $12\times1.3=₹15.6$ lakh, so the range comfortably includes the typical lateral ask while leaving room to settle at the lower end. Be ready to justify with scope, skills and market data.

### In the news
See news box. IIM Calcutta's average of ₹36 LPA and median ₹35 LPA show the ceiling at elite campuses; your own range should reflect your institute, role and sector, not headline numbers.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What are your salary expectations?" / "What is your current CTC?" / "Do you have other offers?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A researched range with rationale, not a single number
> - Honest current pay and structure
> - Focus on total compensation and role scope
> - Real timelines for other processes without pressure tactics
> - Flexibility on non-cash terms (role, learning, location, review cycle)

## 4. Understanding CTC in India: Fixed, Variable, ESOP/RSU, PF, Gratuity and Benefits
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ CTC vs gross vs net; components; employer cost items that you never receive in cash; taxable vs exempt

### Definition
**CTC (cost to company)** is the total annual cost the employer incurs on you, including items you receive in cash and items you do not (employer PF, gratuity accrual, insurance). Components:
- **Basic:** anchor for PF, gratuity and HRA; under the labour codes expected to be at least about 50% of total pay.
- **HRA (house rent allowance):** exempt in the old tax regime subject to conditions, not in the new regime.
- **Special / other allowances:** balancing component, taxable.
- **Employer PF contribution:** 12% of PF wages (of this, 8.33% goes to the pension scheme on a wage capped at ₹15,000, so at most ₹1,250 a month, and the balance to the provident fund account); an equal 12% employee contribution is deducted from your pay.
- **Gratuity:** lump sum at exit after 5 years of service (1 year for fixed-term staff under the codes). Formula: $$\text{Gratuity}=\text{last drawn basic (+DA)}\times\frac{15}{26}\times\text{years of service}$$ The employer's annual accrual is $15/26/12 \approx 4.81\%$ of basic.
- **Variable pay / bonus:** performance-linked; target is not guaranteed; check payout timing and discretion.
- **Joining or retention bonus:** one-off, usually with a clawback if you leave within 6 to 12 months.
- **ESOP / RSU:** equity grants vesting over time (see sub-topic 7).
- **Insurance, meal and travel benefits, NPS (employer), professional tax** (a small state-specific annual levy, about ₹2,400 to ₹2,500 a year in states such as Karnataka and Maharashtra; verify your state).

**Gross vs in-hand:** in-hand = gross cash pay minus employee PF, professional tax and income tax (TDS), plus any reimbursements. Typically 75 to 85% of CTC for fixed-pay employees in the middle bands.

### Example
CTC ₹16 lakh, built with these (illustrative) rules: basic 50% = ₹8,00,000; HRA 40% of basic = ₹3,20,000; employer PF 12% of basic = ₹96,000; gratuity accrual $8{,}00{,}000\times15/26/12\approx₹38{,}462$; target variable 10% = ₹1,60,000; employer's group insurance ₹12,000; special allowance = balance = $16{,}00{,}000-8{,}00{,}000-3{,}20{,}000-96{,}000-38{,}462-1{,}60{,}000-12{,}000=₹1{,}73{,}538$. Cash you can actually receive (basic + HRA + special + variable) is ₹14,53,538, which is 90.8% of CTC; the remaining ₹1,46,462 (PF, gratuity accrual and insurance) is paid by the employer outside your monthly credit.

### In the news
See news box. Under the new wages definition, a CTC built with a low basic and big allowances may be restructured, changing PF and gratuity even if the headline CTC stays the same.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Explain the difference between CTC and take-home. How is gratuity calculated?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Components, taxable vs exempt, employer-side vs cash items
> - Gratuity formula with the 15/26 factor and the 5-year rule (1 year for fixed-term under the codes)
> - Variable pay: target vs guaranteed; clawbacks
> - A worked example with arithmetic
> - Awareness that structure matters as much as the CTC headline

## 5. In-Hand Salary Calculation: A Worked Example (New Regime, FY 2026-27)
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ gross cash, standard deduction, slab tax with 4% cess, rebate and marginal relief, PF, professional tax, monthly TDS

### Definition
Steps:
1. **Gross cash pay** = basic + HRA + special allowance + variable (if paid).
2. **Taxable salary** = gross cash − standard deduction (₹75,000, new regime). Employer PF (within limits), gratuity accrual and employer insurance are not taxed in your hands during employment.
3. **Tax** on slabs (new regime FY 2026-27, see news box) + 4% health and education cess; apply the rebate (zero tax up to ₹12 lakh taxable income) and **marginal relief** just above ₹12 lakh so that tax does not exceed the income above ₹12 lakh.
4. **In-hand** = gross cash − employee PF (12% of basic) − professional tax − tax.

### Example
Using the ₹16 lakh CTC structure from sub-topic 4 (gross fixed cash ₹12,93,538 = basic 8,00,000 + HRA 3,20,000 + special 1,73,538; variable ₹1,60,000):

**Case A: variable paid at 100%.** Gross cash = ₹14,53,538. Taxable = 14,53,538 − 75,000 = ₹13,78,538. Slab tax: 4 to 8 lakh at 5% = ₹20,000; 8 to 12 lakh at 10% = ₹40,000; 12 lakh to 13,78,538 at 15% = $178{,}538\times0.15=₹26{,}781$; total ₹86,781; plus 4% cess = ₹90,252 (the rebate does not apply above ₹12 lakh and marginal relief is not needed because ₹90,252 is below the income above ₹12 lakh). Deductions: employee PF ₹96,000, professional tax ₹2,500 (state dependent). Annual in-hand = 14,53,538 − 96,000 − 2,500 − 90,252 = **₹12,64,786** (about ₹1,05,399 a month on average, 79.0% of CTC). In the twelve monthly payslips (variable paid once), expect about ₹1,07,795 gross, less PF ₹8,000, professional tax about ₹208 and TDS about ₹7,521 = **about ₹92,066 a month**, with the variable arriving as a lump sum.

**Case B: variable paid at 0%.** Gross cash = ₹12,93,538; taxable = ₹12,18,538. Slab tax would be 20,000 + 40,000 + 0.15 × 18,538 = ₹62,781, but marginal relief caps tax at the income above ₹12 lakh = ₹18,538; with cess, tax = ₹19,280. In-hand = 12,93,538 − 96,000 − 2,500 − 19,280 = **₹11,75,758** (₹97,980 a month). Note that losing ₹1,60,000 of variable reduces annual in-hand by only ₹89,028 (12,64,786 − 11,75,758) because tax falls sharply: this is why the marginal relief zone matters.

```python
def tax_new(ti):                      # new regime, FY 2026-27; ti = taxable income
    slabs = [(4e5,0),(8e5,.05),(12e5,.10),(16e5,.15),(20e5,.20),(24e5,.25),(1e18,.30)]
    prev = t = 0
    for up, r in slabs:
        if ti > prev: t += (min(ti, up) - prev) * r
        prev = up
    if ti <= 12e5: return 0                       # rebate
    return min(t, ti - 12e5) * 1.04               # marginal relief, then 4% cess
print(round(tax_new(1378538)), round(tax_new(1218538)))  # 90252 19280
```

### In the news
See news box. The ₹12 lakh rebate and ₹75,000 standard deduction mean salaried income up to about ₹12.75 lakh pays no income tax in this regime, so for lower-band offers the PF and professional tax are the main deductions.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Your offer is ₹16 lakh CTC. What will your monthly in-hand be?" (a numerical question in HR or finance-oriented rounds)

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Separation of cash and non-cash components in CTC
> - Gross → taxable → tax → in-hand steps, with the standard deduction
> - Employee PF (12% of basic) and professional tax
> - Note on variable pay timing and tax effects
> - Statement that regime, state and employer policy change the answer

## 6. Labour Codes and the 50% Rule: Effect on PF, Gratuity and Take-Home
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ wage definition; basic at least 50% of remuneration; PF on higher base; gratuity accrual rises; take-home can fall for fixed CTC; employer approaches differ

### Definition
The Code on Wages defines **wages** so that certain allowances cannot exceed a share of total remuneration, which in practice pushes basic towards at least 50% of pay (reports and employer FAQs use "basic at least 50% of CTC"). Effects when CTC is held constant:
- PF contribution (12% of PF wages) rises for employee and employer if PF is computed on actual basic.
- Gratuity accrual (based on basic) rises, and the lump-sum eventually paid at exit is larger.
- Cash take-home falls because both employee PF deduction and the employer's PF share (which sits inside CTC) increase.

Caveats: many employers compute PF on a capped wage of ₹15,000 a month (so a maximum employee contribution of $15000\times12\%\times12=₹21{,}600$ a year), in which case a higher basic does not change PF; some employers raise CTC to protect take-home; rules continue to be clarified.

### Example
CTC ₹10 lakh. **Before:** basic ₹3,50,000 (35%) → PF at 12% = ₹42,000 (employee) and ₹42,000 (employer). **After:** basic ₹5,00,000 (50%) → PF ₹60,000 each. Employee PF rises ₹18,000 and the employer PF rises ₹18,000; if CTC is fixed, the extra employer PF is taken from allowances, so annual cash falls by 18,000 + 18,000 = ₹36,000, or ₹3,000 a month, before tax effects (employer PF is not taxed, so taxable pay also falls a little). One explainer reports a ₹1,500 monthly fall counting only the employee side; the difference arises from whether the employer's PF share is inside the CTC. The upside: ₹36,000 a year more goes into long-term savings and gratuity accrues faster (accrual on ₹5,00,000 basic is $5{,}00{,}000\times15/26/12\approx₹24{,}038$ versus ₹16,827 at ₹3,50,000 basic).

### In the news
See news box. When comparing offers after November 2025, ask each employer whether the CTC reflects the new wage definition and whether PF is on actual basic or on the ₹15,000 ceiling.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How do the new labour codes affect a fresher's salary structure?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - The 50% wage idea and which components move
> - Direction of effects (PF and gratuity up, take-home down at fixed CTC) with a numerical example
> - Exception: PF on the ₹15,000 ceiling, and employer choices
> - Gratuity for fixed-term employees after one year
> - Advice: compare in-hand and long-term benefits, ask HR to clarify

## 7. Variable Pay, Joining Bonus, ESOP/RSU, Bonds and Clawbacks
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ guaranteed vs target; discretion; vesting and cliff; risk-adjusted value of equity; service bonds; clawback terms

### Definition
- **Variable pay:** the target may be paid at, above or below 100%. Ask: formula (company, team, individual), payout history, timing, and whether it is discretionary.
- **Joining bonus:** one-off cash; typically clawed back if you leave within a stated period (often 12 months). Compare with notice-buyout cost.
- **ESOP (options)** give the right to buy shares at a strike price; **RSUs** deliver shares at vesting. Key terms: grant value, vesting schedule (for example 25% a year over 4 years, sometimes with a one-year cliff), whether shares are listed or unlisted (liquidity), tax at vesting/exercise and sale, and what happens on resignation (unvested lapse).
- **Service bond:** a clause requiring a minimum service period or repayment of a sum (training cost or a fixed amount). Enforceability depends on reasonableness and the contract; always read the exact clause.
- **Retention bonus and clawback:** deferred payments conditional on staying.

Value equity with a **risk adjustment**: $$PV=\sum_{t}\frac{V_t\times p_t}{(1+r)^t}$$ where $V_t$ is the vesting tranche, $p_t$ the probability you are still there, and $r$ the discount rate for risk and time.

### Example
RSU grant ₹12 lakh vesting equally over 4 years (₹3 lakh a year), nominal. With probability of still being employed at each vesting of 90%, 75%, 60% and 50%, and a 12% discount rate: $PV=\frac{3\times0.9}{1.12}+\frac{3\times0.75}{1.12^2}+\frac{3\times0.6}{1.12^3}+\frac{3\times0.5}{1.12^4}=2.41+1.79+1.28+0.95=₹6.44$ lakh in total, about ₹1.61 lakh a year, versus the headline ₹3 lakh a year. (Assumes a flat share price; real value moves with it.) A **₹3 lakh service bond** over 24 months is ₹12,500 per month of unserved time, a useful way to price your exit option. A joining bonus of ₹2 lakh with a 12-month clawback is not a gift: if you leave in month 6, you may return all of it.

### In the news
See news box. Where deferred joining or conditional offers occur, check whether joining bonuses and bonds start from the date of offer or the actual date of joining.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Offer A has ₹4 lakh more in RSUs than Offer B but lower fixed pay. Which is better?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Compare guaranteed cash first, then risk-adjust variable and equity
> - Vesting, cliff, liquidity and company risk (listed vs unlisted)
> - Clawbacks and bonds priced as an exit cost
> - Personal factors: risk appetite, loans, time horizon
> - Ask for written grant details; use an NPV-style calculation

## 8. Evaluating and Ranking Campus Offers
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ total compensation is only one criterion; learning, brand, role fit, location, growth path, work-life, stability; weighted matrix; reversibility

### Definition
Many campuses follow placement policies (for example one student, one job, or a "dream offer" rule); read your institute's rules before applying. When you hold a choice, evaluate:
1. **Role and learning:** what you will do and learn in 2 to 3 years; manager quality; rotations.
2. **Brand and exit options:** how the firm is seen for later moves.
3. **Compensation:** guaranteed cash, variable, equity, bonuses, and in-hand after tax (sub-topic 5).
4. **Growth path:** promotion cycle, time to the next level, pay progression.
5. **Location and lifestyle:** city, travel, hours, flexibility.
6. **Stability and risk:** company health, hiring freezes, deferred joining history (see news box).
7. **Fit with long-term goals** (consulting, product, operations leadership, higher studies).

Compensation compares fairly only after normalising: guaranteed cash, risk-adjusted variable and equity, taxes, location cost of living. A weighted matrix makes trade-offs explicit and also forces you to state your own priorities.

### Example
Three offers (₹ lakh, illustrative): **A** Consulting: fixed 18, target bonus 2, joining bonus 1. **B** FMCG operations: fixed 16, target variable 1.6. **C** Tech product: base 15, bonus 1.5, RSU 12 over 4 years. Year-1 expected cash with variables at 80% payout: A $=18+0.8\times2+1=₹20.6$ lakh; B $=16+0.8\times1.6=₹17.28$ lakh; C $=15+0.8\times1.5=₹16.2$ lakh plus the first RSU tranche risk-adjusted $3\times0.9/1.12=₹2.41$ lakh, so ₹18.61 lakh. Weighted scorecard (weights: Year-1 cash 0.20, 3-year pay growth 0.15, learning and brand 0.25, role fit 0.20, location and work-life 0.10, stability and exit options 0.10; scores out of 5): A scores 4, 5, 5, 4, 2, 4 = **4.20**; B scores 3, 3, 4, 5, 3, 5 = **3.85**; C scores 5, 4, 4, 3, 4, 3 = **3.90**. A leads because of learning and brand; if you instead weight location 0.25 and learning 0.10 (others unchanged), the scores become A 3.75, B 3.70 and C 3.90, so the Tech offer leads: the ranking flips, so test your assumptions.

### In the news
See news box. IIM Calcutta's sector data (consulting 38% of offers; roughly ₹40 LPA average) show why pay differs by track, but also why comparing only headline CTC across tracks misleads.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "You hold offers from two companies. How will you decide, and why should we believe you will join us?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A decision framework (learning, role, growth, compensation, location, risk)
> - Honesty without disloyalty: reasons to choose this company specifically
> - Commitment signals: preparing for the role, clear timelines
> - No bluffing about offers
> - Respect for placement policy and deadlines

## 9. Negotiation: Campus versus Lateral
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ leverage differs; campus negotiations are limited and collective; lateral negotiations hinge on alternatives, scope and timing; scripts; walk-away point

### Definition
**Campus:** bands are standardised; individual negotiation on base pay is rare, but you can negotiate or clarify **non-cash terms** (joining date, location preference, role or function, rotation, relocation support, joining bonus where applicable, clarity on bond). Respect the institute's placement committee rules (they may negotiate on behalf of the class). Never use fake offers.

**Lateral:** you have leverage if your skills are in demand, you hold a real alternative or the employer has urgency. Framework:
1. **BATNA:** your best alternative (staying, another offer).
2. **Target, acceptable, walk-away** numbers for fixed pay, variable, joining bonus, equity, title.
3. **Anchor** with a researched, justified range.
4. **Trade** across components (more fixed vs bigger joining bonus vs earlier review vs seniority title).
5. **Get it in writing** (offer letter) before resigning.
6. **Tone:** collaborative, enthusiastic, fact-based; thank the recruiter.

Script: "I am excited about the role and the team. Based on the scope and my research, I was expecting fixed pay in the ₹X to Y range. Is there flexibility to move towards that?" If the answer is no: "Could we look at a joining bonus or a six-month review to bridge the gap?"

### Example
Offer: fixed ₹14 lakh, variable ₹1.4 lakh; your target ₹16 lakh fixed. Employer says its band caps fixed at ₹14.5 lakh. Counter: accept ₹14.5 lakh fixed plus a ₹2 lakh joining bonus (clawback 12 months) and a performance review at 6 months. Compare: ₹14.5L + ₹1.4L target variable = ₹15.9 lakh versus the original ₹15.4 lakh; with a joining bonus, year-1 cash is ₹17.9 lakh, which meets your total goal even though the fixed number fell short.

### In the news
See news box. In a market with fresher hiring slowdowns, leverage for freshers is limited; leverage for experienced candidates in skilled areas (supply chain analytics, planning, product) is stronger. Prioritise a clear joining date and written terms.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Would you negotiate your offer? How?" / "The company offers 10% less than expected. What do you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Differentiated approach for campus and lateral
> - BATNA, researched range, trade-offs across components
> - Respect and enthusiasm, not ultimatums
> - Written confirmation and clear timelines
> - Willingness to walk away if fundamentals do not fit

## 10. PPO Conversion: From Internship to Pre-Placement Offer
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ internship as a long interview; deliverables, relationships, feedback; presentation to leaders; stipend vs PPO; decision timing

### Definition
A **pre-placement offer (PPO)** is a full-time offer made at the end of a summer internship. Convert it by treating the internship as a continuous interview:
1. **Clarify expectations** in week 1 (deliverables, success criteria, mentor, who decides PPOs).
2. **Deliver a measurable outcome** early (a baseline, an analysis, a pilot) and track impact ([[051 Internship Learnings & Projects]]).
3. **Seek feedback weekly** and act on it visibly.
4. **Build relationships** with the manager, peers and cross-functional contacts; display values (see [[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]).
5. **Final presentation:** a crisp storyline with problem, method, results, recommendations ([[162 Structured Communication - SCQA, Storylines & Case Delivery]]).
6. **Ask about the process** (timeline, criteria) near the end; express interest clearly.

Decision issues: whether to accept a PPO early (placement rules may restrict later participation), compare it with the open market using sub-topic 8, and clarify whether the PPO terms (role, location, compensation) are the same as the final placement band.

### Example
An operations intern's project cuts picking travel time by 12% in a pilot zone; the intern documented baseline measurements, validated with the supervisor and presented a roll-out plan with a cost estimate. In the PPO decision meeting the manager cites the data, ownership and the intern's response to feedback. The lesson: a documented, owned outcome beats long hours.

### In the news
See news box. With deferred joining at several firms, ask your PPO employer for an expected joining month and confirm that the offer will be honoured on that timeline.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you convert your internship into a PPO?" / "You received a PPO but also want to sit for placements. What will you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A plan: expectations, deliverables, feedback, relationships, presentation
> - Measurable impact and learning
> - Ethical handling of PPO and placement policy
> - Willingness to discuss the offer's terms before deciding
> - Gratitude and professionalism either way

## 11. Joining Etiquette, Notice, Background Verification, and Deferred or Rescinded Offers
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ accept in writing; respect deadlines; resign professionally; documents and BGV; handling deferment and revocation; keep options legal

### Definition
- **Accepting:** read the offer letter, ask questions, accept by the deadline; once accepted, honour it. Reneging damages your reputation and may harm your institute's relationship with the recruiter.
- **Before joining:** keep contact with HR, complete documents (identity, education, previous employment, relieving letter), consent for **background verification (BGV)**, medical tests if needed, and confirm joining date, location and reporting.
- **Resigning (lateral):** give written notice per contract; hand over carefully; avoid badmouthing; keep your manager informed; negotiate early release if appropriate; get the relieving and experience letters.
- **Deferred joining or delayed onboarding:** companies sometimes push the date due to demand changes. Respond by (a) requesting a written revised date, (b) asking about interim support or stipend, (c) continuing your search legally if your contract permits, and (d) staying professional.
- **Rescinded (revoked) offers:** reasons include business downturn, BGV failure, falsified information, or failure to meet conditions (e.g. final degree not completed). If revoked unfairly, check the offer letter and any contract, speak to HR in writing, involve your institute's placement office, and keep records. Legal remedies depend on facts, so consult a lawyer for specifics.

### Example
Timeline for a campus joiner: accept the offer within the stated 7-day window; submit documents within 2 weeks; BGV initiated; joining letter with a date; if the date is deferred, email HR: "Thank you for the update. Could you confirm the revised date and whether the offer terms remain unchanged? I would appreciate a written confirmation." Meanwhile keep up your skills and keep a record of communication. A lateral hire with a 90-day notice negotiates early release at 60 days, and the new employer agrees to a later start date rather than a buyout.

### In the news
See news box. Reports of seven-month deferments and candidates awaiting joining letters show why written confirmations and a plan B matter.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What will you do if your joining date is delayed?" / "You have accepted our offer but receive a better one. What will you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Professionalism and honesty; honouring commitments
> - Clear communication in writing; asking for revised dates
> - Awareness of contract terms and placement rules
> - Contingency planning without breaching trust
> - Long-term reputation as a decision factor

## 12. Ethics, References and Integrity in the Offer Process
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ truthful resume and salary; reference checks; no fake offers; confidentiality; conflict of interest; non-solicitation and non-compete; honest exits

### Definition
Integrity issues are verified more often than candidates assume:
- **Resume and salary:** employment dates, titles, grades and pay can be verified through BGV and payslips. Misrepresentation can lead to offer withdrawal or termination for cause.
- **References:** choose people who know your work; ask permission; brief them on the role; prepare for a call.
- **Fake or inflated offers:** using fabricated offers to negotiate is dishonest and usually detected; it can end a process and damage your institute's credibility.
- **Confidential information:** do not carry or share previous employers' data.
- **Contractual restraints:** non-compete, non-solicitation and notice clauses vary in enforceability; read them and take advice if they restrict your move.
- **Multiple processes:** informing recruiters of competing processes is fine; accepting multiple offers with no intention to join is not.
- **Social media and public posts:** keep it consistent with your resume ([[179 Resume, LinkedIn & Cold Outreach for Placements]]).

### Example
A candidate lists a ₹14 lakh current CTC but the payslip shows ₹12 lakh fixed with ₹2 lakh variable not yet paid. When the recruiter asks for payslips, the discrepancy appears. The candidate explains, but the credibility damage is done. The right approach is to state fixed, target variable and received variable separately from the start and say how you computed total CTC.

### In the news
See news box. In competitive markets with deferred or revoked offers, a clean verification record protects you if the employer reviews hiring decisions.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Have you ever been asked to bend the truth, for example about your salary or experience?" / "Who can we contact for a reference?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Clear stance on honesty with an example of how you handled pressure
> - Accurate, verifiable data on compensation and experience
> - Respect for confidentiality and previous employers
> - Prepared references who know your work
> - Awareness of contractual obligations and legal advice

## 13. ⭐ Advanced: Lateral Hikes, Counter-Offers and Notice Buyouts
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**Hike math:** $$\text{Hike \%}=\frac{\text{new fixed}-\text{old fixed}}{\text{old fixed}}\times100$$ Compare like with like: fixed to fixed, total target to total target. Typical lateral hike asks of 20 to 40% are market chatter, not rules; actual ranges depend on role, level and sector, and a 30% hike on a very low base may still be below market.

**Counter-offer from your current employer:** consider why you wanted to leave; money rarely fixes role, manager or growth issues. If you take it, get terms in writing (role, pay, timeline). Reputation risk exists with the new employer if you accept and then retract.

**Notice-period buyout:** a new employer may pay your current employer for early release; typically capped by contract at pay for the remaining notice days. If your monthly gross is ₹1,00,000 and the remaining notice is 90 days (3 months), the buyout is about ₹3,00,000. Negotiate who bears it, and whether it is reflected as a recoverable joining cost.

**Total reward comparison:** include benefits, insurance, leave, work mode (hybrid), PF structure, learning budget, and exit terms.

### Example
Current: fixed ₹12 lakh + target variable ₹1.2 lakh. Offer: fixed ₹15.6 lakh + target variable ₹1.5 lakh + joining bonus ₹2 lakh (12-month clawback). Fixed hike $=(15.6-12)/12=30\%$. Year-1 cash with variable at 100% for both: current ₹13.2 lakh; new $15.6+1.5+2=₹19.1$ lakh, a rise of 44.7%. With variables paid at 80% for both, current is $12+0.8\times1.2=₹12.96$ lakh and new is $15.6+0.8\times1.5+2=₹18.8$ lakh, a rise of 45.1%. In year 2 the joining bonus disappears, so compare year-2 cash too: $15.6+1.2=₹16.8$ lakh against $12.96$ lakh is a 29.6% rise, close to the 30% fixed hike. Treat the joining bonus as one-time and check its clawback.

### In the news
See news box. The new tax regime's ₹12 lakh rebate cutoff and marginal relief mean that around the ₹12 to 13 lakh taxable band a hike is partly absorbed by tax: model after-tax gain, not only pre-tax hike.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "You received a counter-offer from your current employer. What will you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Root-cause thinking on why you wanted to move
> - Total reward comparison and after-tax effect
> - Written terms and a decision date
> - Professional communication with both employers
> - Consideration of reputation and long-term trust

## 14. ⭐ Advanced: Reading the Offer Letter and Contract Clauses
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
Read these clauses before signing (not legal advice; consult a lawyer for disputes):
- **Compensation annexure:** fixed vs variable, definitions of CTC, treatment of PF base (actual or ceiling), gratuity eligibility and conditions.
- **Variable pay and bonus policy:** discretion language, payout dates, proration, clawback or forfeiture on resignation.
- **Probation and confirmation:** length, notice during probation, performance criteria.
- **Notice period and garden leave:** length, buyout provisions, who pays.
- **Bond or training cost recovery:** amount, duration, pro-rata reduction, conditions.
- **Non-compete and non-solicitation:** scope, geography, duration; enforceability under Indian contract law is limited for post-employment non-competes, but non-solicitation and confidentiality are commonly enforced; seek advice.
- **Transfer/relocation and mobility:** right to transfer; relocation support; clawback.
- **Background verification and conditions precedent:** what can cause withdrawal.
- **Equity documents:** grant letter, vesting schedule, treatment on exit, tax.
- **Termination and dispute resolution:** notice, severance, jurisdiction.

### Example
Checklist application: the offer lists CTC ₹16 lakh but states "variable pay is entirely discretionary and subject to company performance". Question to HR: "What was the average payout over the last 3 years for this level, and when is it paid?" The letter also says "bond of ₹3 lakh for 24 months": ask whether it reduces pro-rata (₹12,500 per month of unserved period) and whether it applies if the company relocates you.

### In the news
See news box. After the labour codes, HR teams are revising salary templates; asking for a written breakdown of the new structure avoids surprises later.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What would you check before accepting an offer letter?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A systematic list: compensation, variable policy, notice, bond, probation, restrictions
> - Specific questions to HR (not accusations)
> - Written clarification of ambiguous clauses
> - Willingness to seek advice for complex terms
> - Reflection on priorities and non-negotiables

## 1. Tell Me About Yourself (TMAY)
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ Present-Past-Future structure; 90-second version; hook opening

### Definition
"Tell me about yourself" is an **opening pitch**, not an autobiography. Interviewers use it to judge communication, structure, self-awareness and fit within the first two minutes, and it sets the agenda for follow-up questions (they will probe what you mention).

**Present-Past-Future (PPF) structure:**
1. **Present (about 25 s):** who you are now, with a hook (a headline achievement or identity).
2. **Past (about 40 s):** two or three experiences that built the skills relevant to this role, each with a measurable result.
3. **Future (about 25 s):** why this role and company, linking your direction to what they do.

**90-second version:** about 200 to 230 spoken words (at roughly 140 to 150 words per minute). Prepare also a 30-second and a 3-minute version.

**Hook opening:** start with a distinctive, relevant line rather than "My name is..." only, e.g. "I am an engineer who moved into operations because I like fixing how work actually flows."

Rules: relevant, not chronological; numbers over adjectives; end by handing the conversation back; avoid personal trivia and reciting the CV.

### Example
Skeleton (fill with your verified facts):
"I am Samriddha Basu, an MBA candidate in Operations and Supply Chain at SIOM Nashik with a computer-science engineering background **[Present + hook]**. Before MBA I **[engineering experience/project]**; during MBA I **[internship: problem, action, quantified result]** and **[leadership role: scope, result]** **[Past]**. I want to bring this mix of data skills and operations thinking to **[role]** at **[company]**, where **[specific reason]** **[Future]**."

### In the news
See news box. With consulting among the largest recruiters at top B-schools, interviewers hear many similar introductions; a hook and quantified proof stand out.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Tell me about yourself." / "Walk me through your CV." / "Introduce yourself in a minute."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Clear PPF structure, 60 to 90 seconds
> - A hook and 2 to 3 quantified proof points
> - Tailored to the role and company
> - Ends with a bridge to the role ("which is why this role excites me")
> - Practised aloud, delivered naturally, not memorised word for word

## 2. Professional Summary Pitch
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ B.Tech CSE + MBA Ops; internship highlights; placement goal

### Definition
Your **professional summary pitch** compresses academic base, experience and goal into 3 to 4 sentences, usable as the TMAY core, the top of the CV, a LinkedIn headline or a networking introduction.

Formula: **[Identity] + [differentiator] + [proof] + [goal]**.

Example building blocks for your profile (verify and replace with exact facts):
- Identity: B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering, now MBA in Operations and Supply Chain Management (SIOM Nashik).
- Differentiator: engineering and data skills applied to operations problems.
- Proof: internship outcomes and projects from your tracker (e.g. idle-time reduction at CEAT, MIS reporting at JBM) with the exact numbers you can defend.
- Goal: a role where you analyse operations, design solutions and deliver measurable improvement.

Be ready to defend every number: how it was measured, baseline, your own contribution vs the team's.

### Example
"I combine a CSE degree with an MBA in Operations to solve process and data problems end to end. In my internship at **[company]**, I **[action]**, reducing **[metric]** by **[X%]**; I also led **[initiative]**. I am looking for **[role type]** where I can turn data into operational improvements at scale."

### In the news
See news box. Median packages dipping while consulting offers remain high suggests recruiters value demonstrable results over generic profiles.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Give me your elevator pitch." / "Why should we look at your profile?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Four-part formula, under 30 seconds
> - Numbers you can defend if probed
> - Differentiator linked to the role
> - A clear goal (not vague "learning")

## 3. Career Transition Story
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ CSE → Operations; why the shift; how skills transfer

### Definition
Interviewers ask "why did you move from engineering to operations?" to test whether the shift was deliberate. A strong story has **trigger, evidence, decision, transferable skills, proof**.

Structure:
1. **Trigger:** what first drew you to operations (a project, an observation, a problem you could not stop thinking about). Use a real one.
2. **Evidence:** what you did to test the interest (courses, internship, projects).
3. **Decision:** why an MBA in Ops and SCM was the right step.
4. **Transfer:** CSE skills that apply: structured problem-solving, data modelling, automation, systems thinking, comfort with SQL/Python/dashboards.
5. **Proof:** a result from the internship or a project.

Avoid: negativity about coding or tech, "I was not good at coding", or implying you are escaping something. Frame as moving **towards** the problem space (physical flows and decisions) with a technical edge.

### Example
Skeleton: "As an engineer I enjoyed building software, but I kept being drawn to **[real trigger]**. I tested that interest through **[experience]**, which showed me operations is where data and decisions meet real cost and service. The MBA gave me the domain depth; my CSE background lets me **[automate/analyse]**, for example **[project and result]**."

### In the news
See news box. Operations and consulting recruiters are looking for analytical, technology-fluent candidates; a transition story with proof shows both.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why are you moving from CSE to operations?" / "Why not stay in IT?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A genuine trigger, not a generic "I like operations"
> - Moving towards, not away from
> - Specific transferable skills with an example
> - Evidence that the shift is tested (internship, project)
> - Commitment to the new path

## 4. Connecting Your Profile to Role
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ Tailor TMAY to Consulting vs PM vs Operations per interview

### Definition
The same background should be **emphasised differently** depending on the role. Keep facts constant, change the **lens and the ordering**.

| Role | What the interviewer scans for | Emphasise |
|---|---|---|
| Consultant | Structured thinking, problem solving, communication, client impact | Cases or projects with a clear problem, analysis and recommendation; breadth across industries; leadership |
| Product Manager | User empathy, prioritisation, tech fluency, ownership | Tech background, building tools or dashboards for users, trade-offs and metrics |
| Operations Manager | Process excellence, execution, people handling, numbers | Plant or process improvement, KPIs, shop-floor exposure, stakeholder management |
| Project Manager | Planning, risk, delivery, coordination | Event or project delivery, timelines, stakeholders, scope control |

Method: read the job description, pick the top three requirements, and make sure your pitch shows evidence for each. Research the company so your future statement is specific.

### Example
One internship result (e.g. idle-time reduction) is told three ways: for **consulting**, as a structured diagnosis and recommendation; for **PM**, as understanding the user's pain and building a data tool; for **operations**, as a bottleneck removed with a measurable KPI change.

### In the news
See news box. Because consulting is a major recruiter, expect case-style probing; for operations roles expect KPI and process depth.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How does your background fit this role?" / "Why should we choose you for this position?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Maps three JD requirements to your evidence
> - Adjusts emphasis, not facts
> - Names the company-specific reason
> - Ends with the value you will add in the first year

## 5. ⭐ Advanced: Handling TMAY Follow-up Probes
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
Whatever you say in TMAY becomes the interview agenda. Prepare for follow-ups on every claim: "What exactly was your role?", "How did you measure that?", "What would you do differently?", "What was the hardest part?".

Preparation grid for each claim in your pitch:
- **Context** (one line), **your action** (use "I" for your work, "we" for team), **metric and baseline**, **obstacle**, **learning**.

Plant two "hooks" deliberately (a topic you are strong on) so the interviewer goes where you are prepared, without bluffing. Never inflate numbers; if you do not know a detail, say how you would find out.

### Example
If you say "reduced idle time by [X]%", expect: "Idle time of what? Over what period? How was it measured? What was your contribution?" Have the baseline, method and your specific part ready.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Tell me more about that project." / "How did you calculate that number?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Clear separation of your contribution from the team's
> - Defined baseline and measurement method
> - Honest about limits and what you learned
> - Concise answer, then pause for the next question

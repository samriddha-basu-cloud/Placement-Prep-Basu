## 1. OA Formats, Scoring and Section Strategy
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Sectional timers; accuracy vs attempts; negative-marking expected value; read the instructions screen

### Definition
Typical MBA-campus OA: 45-90 minutes; sections for quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal ability, data interpretation; sometimes a business-awareness or domain section (supply chain basics for operations roles), a psychometric questionnaire and an essay or email-writing task. Sections may be **individually timed** (no going back) and have **sectional cut-offs**.

**Negative marking expected value (EV)** for a 4-option question:
- +1 / −0.25: random guess EV = 0.25 × 1 − 0.75 × 0.25 = **+0.0625**; after eliminating one option, EV = 1/3 − (2/3)(0.25) = **+0.167**. Guess.
- +1 / −1/3: random guess EV = 0.25 − 0.75/3 = **0**; after eliminating one option, EV = 1/3 − (2/3)/3 = **+0.111**. Guess only after eliminating at least one option.
- No negative marking: attempt everything.

**Section strategy:** two passes. Pass 1: solve every question you can do in under 60-75 seconds; mark the rest. Pass 2: return to marked questions. Never spend over 2.5 minutes on one question.

### Example
A 30-minute quant section of 25 questions: target 20 attempts at 90% accuracy (18 correct) rather than 25 attempts at 70% (17.5 correct minus negatives). With +1/−0.25: 20 attempts at 90% → 18 − 0.5 = **17.5**; 25 attempts at 70% → 17.5 − 1.875 = **15.6**.

See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Not asked; it is the gate before the interview. A low OA score usually ends the process regardless of CV.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Read the instruction screen (marking scheme, section timing, calculator rules)
> - Two-pass strategy and an EV-based guessing rule
> - Practice under the same timing

## 2. Percentages, Successive Change and Profit & Loss
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Fraction-percentage table; successive change a + b + ab/100; markup and discount chains

### Definition
Memorise: 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/7 ≈ 14.29%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 ≈ 11.11%, 1/11 ≈ 9.09%, 1/12 ≈ 8.33%, 1/16 = 6.25%.
- **Successive change:** net % = a + b + ab/100.
- If A is r% more than B, B is r/(100 + r) × 100% less than A.
- **Profit & loss:** SP = CP × (1 + markup) × (1 − discount); profit % on CP.
- Successive discounts d1, d2: total = d1 + d2 − d1·d2/100.

### Example
1. Price +20% then −20%: net = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = **−4%**.
2. Discounts of 20% and 10%: 20 + 10 − 2 = **28%**.
3. CP ₹800, marked up 40%, discount 15%: SP = 800 × 1.4 × 0.85 = **₹952**; profit **19%**.
4. A's salary is 25% more than B's; B's is less than A's by 25/125 = **20%**.
5. Inventory valued ₹50 lakh falls 10% in price but quantity rises 30%: value change = −10 + 30 − 3 = **+17%** (₹58.5 lakh).

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA quant; also mental maths inside cases and guesstimates ([[102 Guesstimate Framework]]).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Fraction equivalents for speed
> - The a + b + ab/100 rule
> - Sanity checks (net change of +x then −x is always negative)

## 3. Ratios, Mixtures, Partnerships and Averages
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Alligation; partnership = capital × time; average correction tricks

### Definition
- **Alligation:** mixing two items with values $a$ (cheap) and $b$ (dear) to get mean $m$: quantity ratio cheap : dear = (b − m) : (m − a).
- **Partnership:** profit shared in ratio of capital × time.
- **Averages:** new average = old average + (change in total)/n.
- **Weighted average** for DI and KPIs (fill rate across DCs weighted by volume).

### Example
1. Rice at ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg mixed to sell at a cost of ₹46/kg: ratio = (60 − 46) : (46 − 40) = 14 : 6 = **7 : 3**.
2. A invests ₹50,000 for 12 months; B ₹75,000 for 8 months: 6,00,000 : 6,00,000 = **1 : 1**.
3. Average of 10 numbers is 50; one number 95 was read as 59. Correct average = 50 + (95 − 59)/10 = **53.6**.
4. Two DCs: fill rates 98% on 8,000 lines and 90% on 2,000 lines; network fill rate = (7,840 + 1,800)/10,000 = **96.4%**, not the simple average 94% ([[012 Supply Chain Analytics & KPIs]]).

See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA quant; weighted averages appear in data-interpretation and case maths.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - The alligation cross
> - Weighted, not simple, averages for KPIs

## 4. Interest, Growth and CAGR
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ SI vs CI; rule of 72; CAGR approximations; EMI intuition

### Definition
- Simple interest SI = P·r·t; compound amount = P(1 + r)^t.
- CI − SI for 2 years = P(r)².
- **Rule of 72:** doubling time ≈ 72 / r%.
- **CAGR** = (End / Start)^(1/n) − 1. Approximation for small growth: total % / n, adjusted down.
- Link to finance: NPV and discounting in [[109 Valuation Basics (NPV, IRR, DCF)]].

### Example
1. ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years: SI = **₹2,000**; CI = **₹2,100**; difference = 10,000 × 0.01 = **₹100**.
2. Money doubles in 8 years at simple interest: rate = 100/8 = **12.5%**.
3. Doubling at 9% compound: 72/9 ≈ **8 years**.
4. Revenue 100 → 150 in 3 years: CAGR = 1.5^(1/3) − 1 = **14.5%** (not 16.7%).
5. A market at ₹20,000 crore growing 12% a year reaches about 20,000 × 1.12⁵ ≈ ₹35,250 crore in 5 years (1.12⁵ ≈ 1.762).

### In the news
See news box. Growth rates in news (GDP, sector growth) are year-on-year or CAGR; check which ([[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]]).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA quant, DI and guesstimates ("market grows at 12%; size in 2030?").

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Rule of 72 and CAGR formula for quick estimates
> - Saying "compounded" vs "simple" explicitly

## 5. Time-Speed-Distance and Time & Work
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Average speed is harmonic; relative speed; work rates add; pipes and cisterns

### Definition
- km/h to m/s: × 5/18.
- **Average speed** for equal distances at $u$ and $v$: 2uv/(u + v).
- **Relative speed:** add if opposite directions, subtract if same direction; crossing distance = sum of lengths.
- **Work:** rates add: if A takes $a$ days and B $b$ days, together $ab/(a + b)$.
- **Pipes:** inlet rates positive, outlets negative.

### Example
1. Truck goes Pune→Mumbai at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h: average = 2 × 60 × 40 / 100 = **48 km/h** (not 50).
2. Trains 150 m and 100 m long at 54 and 36 km/h in opposite directions: relative speed 90 km/h = 25 m/s; crossing time = 250/25 = **10 s**.
3. A finishes in 12 days, B in 18: together 12 × 18 / 30 = **7.2 days**.
4. Pipe fills in 6 h; drain empties in 9 h; both open: net rate 1/6 − 1/9 = 1/18 → **18 h**.
5. Warehouse: 4 pickers pick 1,200 lines in 5 h; lines per picker-hour = 60. To pick 1,800 lines in 4 h you need 1,800 / (60 × 4) = **7.5 → 8 pickers** ([[128 Warehouse Labour, WES-WCS & Yard Management]]).

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA quant; capacity versions appear in operations interviews ([[018 Capacity Management & OEE]]).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Harmonic mean for average speed
> - Rate-based thinking (units per hour) for all work problems

## 6. Counting, Probability and Clocks
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ nCr; complement rule; independent events; clock-angle formula

### Definition
- Combinations C(n, r) = n!/(r!(n − r)!); permutations P(n, r) = n!/(n − r)!.
- "At least one" = 1 − P(none) (complement).
- Independent events multiply; mutually exclusive events add.
- Clock angle at H:M = |30H − 5.5M| (take 360 minus it if over 180).
More puzzles in [[217 Probability Puzzles & Applied Problem Solving]].

### Example
1. Committee of 3 from 5 men and 4 women with at least one woman: C(9,3) − C(5,3) = 84 − 10 = **74**.
2. Two dice, sum 7: 6/36 = **1/6**.
3. At least one six in two throws: 1 − (5/6)² = **11/36** ≈ 0.306.
4. Angle at 3:40: |90 − 220| = **130°**.
5. A supplier is on time 90% of the time, independently; probability all of 3 consecutive deliveries are on time = 0.9³ = **0.729**.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA quant; probability also appears in consulting puzzles and stats rounds ([[087 Probability Fundamentals]]).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Complement rule for "at least"
> - Writing the sample space for small problems instead of guessing

## 7. Data Interpretation: Speed Techniques
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Read titles, units, base years; approximate; percentage-point vs percent; weighted growth

### Definition
Steps for each DI set: (1) read the title, units (₹ crore vs ₹ lakh, % vs pp), time period and notes; (2) read all questions before calculating, and compute shared intermediate values once; (3) approximate when options are far apart; (4) watch traps: **percentage points vs percent**, base effects, share vs absolute values, cumulative vs annual. See [[029 Data Interpretation & Charts]].

Weighted growth: total growth = Σ (segment share × segment growth).

### Example
1. Segment A is 40% of ₹500 crore revenue and grows 10%; the rest grows 20%. Total growth = 0.4 × 10 + 0.6 × 20 = **16%** (revenue ₹580 crore).
2. Market share rises from 20% to 25%: that is **+5 percentage points**, or **+25%** relative.
3. Which is bigger, 37/53 or 41/61? 37/53 ≈ 0.698; 41/61 ≈ 0.672; first is bigger (cross-multiply: 37 × 61 = 2,257 > 41 × 53 = 2,173).
4. Approximation: 4,987 × 0.196 ≈ 5,000 × 0.2 = 1,000, minus a little; actual 977.5.

### In the news
See news box. News figures need the same discipline: "inflation rose from 4.8% to 5.5%" is a 0.7 percentage-point increase ([[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]]).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA DI sets; consulting written tests and case exhibits.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Units and base checks first
> - Approximation with option elimination
> - pp vs % precision

## 8. Logical Reasoning: Series, Coding, Relations, Syllogisms, Arrangements
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Pattern families; Venn diagrams for syllogisms; grid method for arrangements

### Definition
- **Series:** differences (and differences of differences), ×/÷ patterns, squares/cubes, n(n + 1), alternating series, primes.
- **Coding-decoding:** letter positions (A = 1 … Z = 26), shifts, reversals.
- **Blood relations:** draw a family tree with symbols (+ male, − female).
- **Syllogisms:** draw minimal Venn diagrams; a conclusion follows only if it holds in **every** possible diagram.
- **Arrangements (linear, circular, floor, scheduling):** fix the most constrained element first, build a grid, test cases.
- **Direction sense:** sketch with a compass.

### Example
1. 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? → n(n + 1): **42**.
2. 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, ? → ×2 − 1: **65**.
3. If CAT = 3120 (C = 3, A = 1, T = 20), DOG = **4157** (D = 4, O = 15, G = 7).
4. "Pointing to a boy, Riya says, 'His mother is the only daughter of my mother.'" The only daughter of Riya's mother is Riya herself, so Riya is the boy's **mother**.
5. Syllogism: "All managers are graduates. Some graduates are engineers." Conclusion "Some managers are engineers": **does not follow** (the engineers may be graduates who are not managers).
6. Arrangement: five trucks P, Q, R, S, T at docks 1-5; R is at dock 3; P is next to R; Q is at an end; T is not next to Q. Cases: Q at 1 → dock 2 can't be T, so P at 2 or 4… one valid answer: Q-1, S-2, R-3, P-4, T-5. Build the grid and test each case rather than guessing.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA logical section; puzzles sometimes asked live in consulting and PM interviews.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Draw before thinking (trees, grids, Venn diagrams)
> - Test every case for "must be true" questions

## 9. Critical Reasoning and Data Sufficiency
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Argument = premises + assumption → conclusion; strengthen/weaken/assume/infer; DS: test each statement alone then together

### Definition
**Critical reasoning** question types: find the **assumption** (unstated premise needed for the conclusion), **strengthen**, **weaken**, **inference** (must be true), **flaw**, **evaluate**. Method: identify the conclusion, then the gap between evidence and conclusion; the right answer usually targets that gap. Same skill as testing a case hypothesis ([[024 Consulting Frameworks]]).

**Data sufficiency:** decide whether statement (1) alone, (2) alone, both together, either alone, or neither is enough to answer. You do not need the answer, only whether it can be determined.

### Example
1. "Our new dark store in Pune raised quick-commerce orders 40% in its first month, so opening dark stores in other cities will raise orders 40% too." Assumption: other cities have similar demand density and the Pune lift was not launch-promotion driven. Weakener: "Pune's launch month included a 50%-off promotion." ([[129 E-commerce & Quick-Commerce Fulfilment]])
2. DS: "What is the profit margin of product X?" (1) Price is ₹500. (2) Cost is 80% of price. Statement (2) alone gives margin = 20%: **(2) alone is sufficient**; (1) alone is not.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA verbal/logical; the same thinking is tested in cases ("what would make you doubt this?").

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Locate conclusion and gap
> - Answer choices that attack or bridge the gap

## 10. Verbal Ability: RC, Para-Jumbles, Grammar and Vocabulary
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Read for structure; main idea vs detail; para-jumble openers and linkers; common grammar errors

### Definition
- **Reading comprehension:** skim the first and last lines of each paragraph to map structure; read questions; return to the passage for evidence. Main-idea answers are broad but within scope; extreme words (always, never) are usually wrong.
- **Para-jumbles:** find the opening sentence (introduces the topic, no pronoun referring back), then pairs linked by pronouns, connectors (however, therefore) and chronology.
- **Grammar:** subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, parallelism, modifiers, pronoun reference, articles, prepositions.
- **Vocabulary:** business words appear in context (mitigate, leverage, attrition, ramp-up, headwinds).
- **Email/essay task:** structure (purpose → points → action), concise and polite ([[232 AI Productivity Toolkit for MBA Work - Excel, SQL, Decks & Research]], sub-topic 10).

### Example
1. Error spotting: "The list of approved suppliers **are** updated quarterly." → **is** (subject is "list").
2. Parallelism: "The role involves planning, coordination and **to monitor** vendors." → "planning, coordinating and monitoring vendors".
3. Para-jumble: (A) This raised costs sharply. (B) In 2024, the firm moved to single sourcing. (C) It has since added a second supplier. (D) Then a fire shut the supplier's plant. Order: **B-D-A-C**.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> OA verbal; writing quality also judged in essays, emails and GDs ([[177 Group Discussions & Extempore for MBA Placements]]).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Structure-first reading

## 11. Psychometric, Situational-Judgement and Game-Based Assessments
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Be consistent and honest; read the role's values; SJT = best/worst action; games measure cognition and risk-taking

### Definition
- **Personality questionnaires** (Likert scales, forced choice): measure traits such as conscientiousness, teamwork, resilience, openness. They include consistency checks; trying to "game" every item produces contradictions. Answer honestly while keeping the role's demands in mind.
- **Situational-judgement tests (SJTs):** workplace scenarios; choose the most and least effective actions. Effective answers usually: address the issue directly and early, involve the right stakeholders, follow policy and ethics, and keep the customer and team in mind; avoid escalating everything or ignoring problems. Read company values first ([[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]).
- **Game-based assessments:** short games measuring attention, memory, learning, risk appetite and planning. Practise once to know the format; then play naturally.
- **Video interviews** (recorded answers with timers): practise 60-90-second structured answers ([[048 Tell Me About Yourself]]).

### Example
SJT: "A key supplier tells you two days before dispatch that 30% of an order will be late. Your manager is travelling." Options ranked: **Most effective:** confirm the facts and new date, check alternative stock or suppliers, inform affected internal customers with options, and update the manager with a recommendation. **Least effective:** wait for the manager to return before acting.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> As screening tests; interviewers sometimes ask about your questionnaire results ("You rated yourself low on detail orientation; tell me more").

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Consistency and honesty
> - Ownership, stakeholder communication, ethics in SJTs

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: A 21-Day OA Plan and Mock Analysis
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**21-day plan** (about 60-75 minutes a day, alongside interview prep in [[Study Plan]]):
- Days 1-7: quant fundamentals (sub-topics 2-6), 20 timed questions a day; one DI set daily.
- Days 8-12: logical reasoning and critical reasoning; one arrangement set daily.
- Days 13-15: verbal and email writing; one RC daily.
- Days 16-21: full-length timed mocks every other day; analysis on alternate days.

**Mock analysis template:** for each wrong or skipped question log topic, error type (concept, calculation, misread, time) and time spent. Fix the top two error types each week. Track accuracy and attempts by section.

### Example
After mock 2: quant 14/25 attempts at 79% accuracy; errors: 3 misreads (units), 2 calculation slips, 1 concept (alligation). Actions: underline units, write the mixture cross every time, 10 minutes daily of mental arithmetic. Mock 4: 18 attempts at 89% accuracy.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Not asked; but a strong OA gets you into more interview rooms, which is the real constraint in placement season ([[238 Placement Season Playbook - Process, Calendar, Day-of Strategy & Mock Drills]]).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Timed practice and error logs
> - Two-pass section strategy
> - Weekly review of weakest areas

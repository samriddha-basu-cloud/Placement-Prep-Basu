## 1. How to Use a Sector Primer: The One-Page Template
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Eight boxes; 45 minutes per company; three numbers, two challenges, one question

### Definition
**Sector/company one-pager** (fill before every process):
1. **Structure:** market size and growth, top players and shares, regulation.
2. **Value chain and economics:** where margin is made; cost structure (materials, conversion, logistics, marketing).
3. **Operations model:** plants, network, make vs buy, channels, inventory profile.
4. **KPIs:** the five operating metrics the industry lives by.
5. **Current issues (last 12 months):** demand, costs, regulation, technology, disruptions; each dated.
6. **Company specifics:** segments, recent results, capex, strategy statements, leaders.
7. **Role fit:** what the role does in the first 2 years; why your background fits ([[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]).
8. **Questions to ask:** two or three informed questions.
Use AI to speed up the research, with sources checked ([[232 AI Productivity Toolkit for MBA Work - Excel, SQL, Decks & Research]], sub-topic 7).

### Example
"Three numbers, two challenges, one question" for a tyre company interview: numbers: FY26 vehicle wholesales +10.4% (OEM demand), natural rubber's share of raw-material cost (check the annual report), capacity utilisation; challenges: raw-material inflation with a weak rupee, EV-specific tyre demands (low rolling resistance, noise); question: "How is the replacement-market distribution strategy changing as e-commerce grows?"

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Why this industry?", "What do you know about us?", "What is our biggest challenge?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Specific, dated facts
> - Operations-relevant insight (network, cost, service)
> - A link to your experience

## 2. FMCG and Consumer Goods
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Brand + distribution + scale; high-volume, low-margin per unit; GT dominance with fast channel shift; working-capital light

### Definition
- **Structure:** multinationals (Hindustan Unilever, Nestlé, P&G, Colgate), Indian majors (ITC, Dabur, Marico, Britannia, Tata Consumer, Godrej Consumer), many regional and D2C brands; categories: foods, personal care, home care, beverages.
- **Economics:** gross margins typically high for personal care, lower for foods; advertising and distribution are big cost lines; negative or low working capital in leaders (distributors pay quickly).
- **Operations:** many SKUs, own plants plus contract manufacturers, C&FA depots, distributors, millions of retail outlets ([[130 FMCG & Retail Distribution - India Route-to-Market]]); promotions drive volatility ([[118 New-Product Forecasting, Demand Sensing & Demand Shaping]]).
- **KPIs:** case fill rate, OTIF to distributors, forecast accuracy/bias, inventory days, cost per case delivered, numeric/weighted distribution, out-of-stocks.
- **Roles:** supply chain planning, manufacturing, procurement, customer logistics, management trainee/GM programmes ([[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]] for HUL, P&G, ITC).

### Example
Talking point: "Rural FMCG volumes fell 5% in April-June 2026 while e-commerce volume grew 34.8%. For supply chain, that means smaller rural packs and tighter GT inventory, plus building quick-commerce-specific packs, dark-store replenishment and platform-level forecasting, with very different service levels from GT."

### In the news
See news box. GST rate rationalisation (5%, 18% and 40% slabs from 22 Sep 2025) required repricing and pack-price changes across categories ([[227 GST & Indirect Tax for Supply Chains]]).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How should our supply chain adapt to quick commerce?" / "Rural demand is weak; what would you do?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Channel economics and service differences
> - Pack, SKU and network implications
> - KPIs and a phased plan

## 3. Automotive and Auto Components
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ OEMs and tiered suppliers; JIT/JIS; cyclical; EV transition; GST 2.0 and rate cycle effects

### Definition
- **Structure:** passenger vehicle OEMs (Maruti Suzuki, Hyundai, Tata Motors, Mahindra, Toyota, Kia), two-wheelers (Hero, Honda, TVS, Bajaj, Royal Enfield, EV makers like Ather and Ola Electric), commercial vehicles (Tata, Ashok Leyland); component makers (Bosch, Motherson, Sona Comstar, Uno Minda, tyre makers).
- **Economics:** high fixed costs, cyclical demand, sensitive to interest rates and fuel prices; component makers squeezed between OEM price-downs and commodity costs.
- **Operations:** JIT and JIS supply, tiered networks, supplier quality (APQP, PPAP, 8D), long programme lifecycles ([[131 Automotive Supply Chain - JIT, Tiers & EVs]], [[121 Supplier Quality & Automotive Core Tools (APQP, PPAP, 8D)]]).
- **KPIs:** OEE, PPM defects, line stoppages caused by suppliers, inventory turns, delivery adherence, cost per vehicle, warranty claims.
- **Trends:** SUV mix shift, EVs (batteries, motors, semiconductors), localisation under PLI, exports to emerging markets.

### Example
Talking point: "FY26 was a record: 2.83 crore domestic wholesales, +10.4%, with utility vehicles up 11% but cars only 1.9%. The October 2026 rate hike, Brent near \$107 and the rupee near ₹96 could slow entry segments, so I'd watch dealer inventory days and flexible production planning."

### In the news
See news box. SIAM credited GST 2.0 reforms with lifting sentiment in FY26.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "A supplier fire stops our line tomorrow. What do you do?" or "How will EVs change our supply chain?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Immediate containment (safety stock, alternate sources, allocation) then root cause
> - Tier-2/3 visibility and dual sourcing
> - EV shifts: battery sourcing, semiconductors, new suppliers, fewer engine parts

## 4. E-commerce and Quick Commerce
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Marketplace vs inventory models; last-mile cost; dark stores; unit economics per order; speed vs assortment

### Definition
- **Structure:** marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho), vertical players (Nykaa, Myntra), quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, Flipkart Minutes, Amazon Now, BigBasket), D2C brands, B2B e-commerce for kiranas.
- **Economics:** contribution per order = AOV × take rate or margin + ad income + delivery fees − (last-mile + fulfilment + payment + discounts). Quick commerce depends on order density per dark store and AOV.
- **Operations:** fulfilment centres, sortation, dark stores (2,000-4,000 sq ft, 10-minute radius), returns, rider fleets ([[129 E-commerce & Quick-Commerce Fulfilment]], [[135 Reverse Logistics, Remanufacturing & EPR in India]]).
- **KPIs:** orders per day per store, delivery time, fill rate/availability, cost per order, picking time, returns rate, contribution margin per order.

### Example
Dark-store economics: 1,800 orders a day at AOV ₹520 and 18% blended margin plus ad income of ₹15 per order gives gross contribution ₹108.6 per order (520 × 0.18 + 15). Costs: last mile ₹45, store operations and rent ₹30, packaging and payment ₹12, discounts ₹15 → ₹102. Contribution ≈ **₹6.6 per order**, ≈ ₹11,900 a day. Store costs are mostly fixed (₹30 × 1,800 = ₹54,000 a day), so below about 1,475 orders a day (54,000 / (108.6 − 72)) contribution turns negative, so density is the key lever.

### In the news
See news box. Blinkit led with 3.4-3.6 million daily orders in August 2026; average order values (June quarter) were ₹518 at Blinkit, ₹691 at Instamart and ₹300-350 at Zepto.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Should a quick-commerce player expand to Tier-2 cities?" or "Reduce cost per order by 15%."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Unit economics per order with numbers
> - Density, AOV and basket levers
> - Operations levers: slotting, picking, batching, rider utilisation

## 5. Process Industries: Steel, Cement, Metals and Chemicals
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Capital-intensive; energy and logistics heavy; capacity utilisation drives profit; continuous processes; safety

### Definition
- **Structure:** steel (Tata Steel, JSW, SAIL, JSPL, AM/NS India), cement (UltraTech, Ambuja-ACC under Adani, Shree, Dalmia), aluminium and metals (Hindalco, Vedanta), chemicals (specialty and commodity).
- **Economics:** huge capex, high fixed costs, commodity pricing, energy (coal, power, pet coke) and freight as major costs; profit highly sensitive to utilisation and prices.
- **Operations:** continuous or batch processes, raw-material logistics (iron ore, coal, limestone), plant maintenance (TPM, [[022 Maintenance Management (TPM-RCM)]]), outbound logistics by rail and road, safety and sustainability (decarbonisation, CBAM for EU exports, [[014 Global SCM & Sustainability]]).
- **KPIs:** capacity utilisation, energy per tonne, yield, downtime, freight cost per tonne, lead distance, safety (LTIFR).

### Example
Cement freight: if outbound freight is about ₹1,000-1,200 per tonne and selling price about ₹5,500-6,000 per tonne (illustrative; verify), a 10% cut in lead distance through better plant-market allocation (an LP transportation model, [[147 Operations Research - Transportation, Assignment & Transshipment]]) is worth ₹100+ per tonne, a large share of EBITDA per tonne.

### In the news
See news box. Infrastructure capex (₹12.2 lakh crore in the FY27 Budget) underpins steel and cement demand ([[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]], sub-topic 9).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you reduce logistics cost per tonne?" or "How can a steel plant decarbonise?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Cost structure numbers per tonne
> - Network optimisation, mode shift to rail, utilisation
> - Energy efficiency, alternative fuels, carbon pricing

## 6. Pharma and Healthcare
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Regulated quality; generics exports; API dependence; cold chain; serialisation

### Definition
- **Structure:** Indian generics leaders (Sun, Dr Reddy's, Cipla, Lupin, Zydus, Aurobindo), MNC subsidiaries, CDMOs, distributors, hospital chains and diagnostics.
- **Economics:** domestic branded generics (field force-driven), export generics (US price erosion), API/KSM import dependence on China, R&D for complex generics and biosimilars.
- **Operations:** GMP compliance (USFDA, WHO), batch release, cold chain for vaccines and biologics, serialisation and track-and-trace, near-expiry management ([[132 Pharma & Healthcare Supply Chain]]).
- **KPIs:** right-first-time batches, OTIF, inventory days, expiry write-offs, deviations/CAPA closure, audit outcomes.

### Example
Talking point: "A USFDA warning letter at one plant can block US approvals and shipments from that site; supply chain teams must re-site products, which takes many months of tech transfer and validation. That is why dual-site strategies and quality culture matter as much as cost."

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you reduce expiry write-offs?" or "Reduce API dependence on China."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Quality and regulation first
> - Inventory and FEFO policies, demand sensing
> - Supplier development, PLI for APIs, dual sourcing

## 7. Consumer Durables and Electronics Manufacturing
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Seasonal demand; imported components; PLI-driven localisation; EMS model; after-sales

### Definition
- **Structure:** brands (LG, Samsung, Voltas, Havells, Blue Star, Whirlpool), electronics manufacturing services (EMS) firms (Dixon, Foxconn, Tata Electronics, Kaynes), component makers.
- **Economics:** thin margins in EMS, high import content (displays, chips, compressors), seasonal peaks (summer for ACs, festive season for TVs), currency sensitivity.
- **Operations:** build-to-forecast with long component lead times, CKD/SKD assembly, PLI-linked localisation, reverse logistics and service networks ([[134 Electronics & Semiconductor Supply Chain]], [[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]]).
- **KPIs:** forecast accuracy for peak season, component availability, yield, warranty cost, service turnaround.

### Example
AC demand peaks March-June. Compressors have long lead times, so the build plan locks in by December. If the summer is mild, finished goods pile up; if hot, stock-outs. Levers: postponement (common platforms, late configuration), capacity flexibility with contract manufacturers, and weather-linked demand sensing.

### In the news
See news box. Rupee weakness (₹96/\$) raises imported component costs ([[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]], sub-topic 10).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you plan for summer peak demand?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Lead-time-aware planning and scenario ranges
> - Postponement and flexible capacity
> - Localisation and currency risk

## 8. Logistics, 3PL, Ports and Express
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Fragmented trucking; asset-light vs asset-heavy; contract logistics; ports and multimodal; logistics cost about 8% of GDP

### Definition
- **Structure:** 3PL/contract logistics (Mahindra Logistics, TVS SCS, DHL Supply Chain), express and e-commerce logistics (Delhivery, Blue Dart, Shadowfax), freight forwarders, ports (Adani Ports, JNPA), rail (CONCOR, dedicated freight corridors), truck aggregators.
- **Economics:** low margins, utilisation-driven, fuel and driver costs, fragmented trucking.
- **Operations:** warehousing, transportation, cross-docking, network design, TMS/WMS, contract SLAs ([[009 Logistics & Distribution]], [[125 Transportation Management Deep Dive]], [[010 Warehouse Management]]).
- **KPIs:** cost per kg/tonne-km, on-time delivery, truck utilisation, empty miles, dwell time, damage rate, warehouse productivity.
- **Policy:** National Logistics Policy, PM Gati Shakti, logistics cost at 7.97% of GDP (FY24 study; [[180 Current Affairs & Economy Briefing for MBA Interviews (2025-26)]]).

### Example
A 3PL's warehouse contract earns ₹22 per pallet-day on 10,000 pallet positions at 85% occupancy: revenue ≈ 10,000 × 0.85 × 22 × 365 ≈ **₹6.8 crore** a year. Each 5-point fall in occupancy loses ≈ ₹40 lakh, which is why 3PLs push multi-client facilities and occupancy-linked contracts.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you cut a client's logistics cost by 10%?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Cost baseline by mode and lane
> - Network, mode shift, load consolidation, utilisation levers
> - SLA and service trade-offs

## 9. IT Services and Global Capability Centres
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Headcount-linked revenue under AI pressure; GCC growth; MBA roles in delivery, presales, transformation and supply-chain/finance operations

### Definition
- **IT services:** TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro, Tech Mahindra, LTIMindtree; revenue from application services, infrastructure, consulting, BPO; pricing by effort or outcome.
- **GCCs:** captive centres of multinationals (technology, analytics, finance, procurement, supply chain, R&D).
- **MBA roles:** business analysts, presales and solution consultants, delivery/project managers ([[037 PM Fundamentals & Lifecycle]]), SAP/ERP functional consultants ([[202 SAP Interview Questions, T-code Cheat Sheet & End-to-End Flows]]), procurement and supply-chain analytics in GCCs, AI transformation roles.
- **KPIs:** utilisation, revenue per employee, attrition, deal TCV, project margin, SLA adherence.

### Example
Talking point: "The top five IT firms' headcount fell by 7,389 in FY26 even as the wider tech sector added about 1.35 lakh people, and GCCs reached roughly 2.36 million employees. Work is shifting from effort-based delivery towards AI-led productivity and towards GCCs; for an MBA, value lies in domain plus technology: SAP, process redesign, analytics and AI adoption ([[231 AI Industry Landscape, Economics & India AI Ecosystem]], sub-topic 10)."

### In the news
See news box. TCS plans about 25,000 fresher hires in FY27, down from 40,000-42,000 earlier.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How will AI change our delivery model?" / "Why a GCC rather than a consulting firm?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Mechanism (effort per project falls, pricing shifts)
> - Where MBAs add value (domain, process, client management)
> - Your SAP/analytics skills linked to the role

## 10. Consulting and Big 4 Advisory
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Strategy vs operations vs implementation vs tech consulting; India practices; case and fit interviews

### Definition
- **Firm types:** strategy (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Kearney), Big 4 advisory (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC), technology and implementation (Accenture, Capgemini, IBM), boutique operations/procurement firms, and in-house strategy teams. Details in [[161 Consulting Firms & Recruitment Landscape in India]].
- **Operations practices:** supply chain strategy and network design, procurement transformation, manufacturing excellence, ERP/S&OP implementations, AI transformation ([[230 AI Strategy, Use-Case Prioritisation & ROI]]).
- **Interview process:** resume shortlist, case interviews ([[025 Case Interview — Profitability]], [[026 Case Interview — Operations Cases]]), guesstimates, fit/behavioural ([[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]).
- **KPIs for consultants:** utilisation, client impact, team feedback.

### Example
Answer to "Why operations consulting rather than industry?": "I want to solve operations problems across industries early in my career: in my internship I saw how a single MIS fix changed a plant's decisions, and consulting compresses that learning. Operations consulting also lets me use my SAP and analytics skills on network design and procurement problems, which are growing with AI and supply-chain resilience mandates."

### In the news

## 11. BFSI Operations and Fintech
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Banking and insurance operations; process excellence; digital lending; regulation; BFSI hiring up

### Definition
- **Structure:** banks (SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI, Axis, Kotak), NBFCs (Bajaj Finance), insurers, payments and fintech (PhonePe, Paytm, Razorpay), credit bureaus.
- **Operations roles for Ops MBAs:** process excellence (Lean Six Sigma in loan processing, claims), branch and service operations, collections strategy, vendor management, digital transformation, risk operations.
- **Economics:** net interest margin, cost-to-income ratio, credit costs, fee income; operations drive the cost-to-income ratio and turnaround time.
- **KPIs:** turnaround time (TAT) for loans/claims, first-time-right, cost per transaction, NPS, fraud losses, collection efficiency.
- **Regulation:** RBI (digital lending guidelines, KYC), IRDAI, DPDP for customer data.

### Example
A home-loan process takes 12 days on average from application to sanction. Value-stream mapping shows only 1.5 days of value-adding work; waiting for documents and credit queue time make up 8 days. Fixes: pre-filled data via account aggregator consent, parallel credit checks, a triage model for simple cases. Target TAT 5 days; each day less lowers drop-off (lost customers) measurably ([[017 Process Management & Optimization]], [[149 Queueing Theory & Waiting-Line Analysis]]).

### In the news
See news box. BFSI hiring at IIM Mumbai rose 33% in the 2026 placement season. The October 2026 rate hike affects lending growth and margins ([[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]]).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you reduce loan processing time?" / "Why BFSI for an operations MBA?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Process mapping, queueing and Lean tools applied to services
> - Customer and risk metrics together
> - Regulatory and data constraints

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: Conglomerate GM Programmes and Energy-Transition Businesses
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**Conglomerate general-management programmes** (Tata Administrative Services, Aditya Birla Group's leadership programmes, Mahindra, RPG, Reliance, ITC, Adani) rotate trainees across businesses and functions. They test breadth: business sense across sectors, leadership stories, values fit ([[176 Company-Specific Behavioural Rounds & Leadership Principles]]), current affairs and GDs ([[177 Group Discussions & Extempore for MBA Placements]]).

**Energy-transition businesses** recruit operations MBAs for project management (solar, wind, storage, green hydrogen, EV charging), supply chain (modules, cells, transformers) and commercial roles. Themes: India's non-fossil capacity (300.5 GW by July 2026 per [[180 Current Affairs & Economy Briefing for MBA Interviews (2025-26)]]), domestic manufacturing (ALMM, PLI for modules and cells), grid and storage bottlenecks, data-centre power demand ([[231 AI Industry Landscape, Economics & India AI Ecosystem]], sub-topic 7), carbon border taxes.

### Example
GM-programme question: "Which group business would you most like to work in, and what would you change there?" Strong structure: pick a business, state two facts about its position, name one operations or growth opportunity with a rough size, and your first 90-day approach. E.g., "The group's renewable-energy business: with transformer and module lead times as bottlenecks, I would want to work on project supply-chain planning, using critical-chain scheduling and supplier development to cut commissioning delays ([[039 Scheduling Tools (CPM-PERT-Gantt)]], [[168 Project Procurement, Contracts & EPC Delivery]])."

### In the news
See news box. Google's planned Visakhapatnam AI campus is to run on clean energy, one example of AI and energy transition demand reinforcing each other.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> GM programmes: "Where do you see yourself in the group?" Energy firms: "What is the biggest bottleneck in India's renewable build-out?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Knowledge of the group's businesses and values
> - A specific operations opportunity with numbers
> - Leadership and learning orientation

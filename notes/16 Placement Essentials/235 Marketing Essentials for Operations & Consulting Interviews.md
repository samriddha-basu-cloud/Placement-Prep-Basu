## 1. The Marketing Concept and the Marketing-Operations Interface
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Customer needs at the centre; value = benefits − costs; where marketing decisions hit the supply chain

### Definition
**Marketing** creates, communicates and delivers value to target customers profitably. Orientations evolved from production and selling to the **marketing concept** (start from customer needs) and the **holistic/societal** concept (relationships, sustainability). Customer **value** = perceived benefits − total cost (price, effort, risk).

**Marketing-operations interface** (where operations interviews probe):
- **Promotions and launches** → forecast error, bullwhip, capacity spikes ([[114 Bullwhip Effect, Beer Game & Information Sharing]], [[118 New-Product Forecasting, Demand Sensing & Demand Shaping]]).
- **SKU proliferation** (new packs, flavours) → complexity cost, lower forecast accuracy ([[139 Product Design & Supply Chain - DFSC, Postponement & Complexity]]).
- **Service promises** (10-minute delivery, same-day) → network and inventory design ([[129 E-commerce & Quick-Commerce Fulfilment]]).
- **Channel mix** → cost-to-serve differences ([[138 Order Management, Customer Service & Cost-to-Serve]]).
- **S&OP** is where both sides agree one plan ([[120 Integrated Business Planning (IBP) & S&OP Maturity]]).

### Example
Marketing plans a "buy 2 get 1" on a shampoo for Diwali, expecting +40% volume. Operations needs: promo-pack production (bundling line capacity), extra raw material (bottles have 8-week lead time), distributor stock-up timing, and a post-promo dip in the forecast. Without S&OP alignment, the plant runs overtime and distributors are overstocked in November.

### In the news
See news box. The shift to e-commerce and quick commerce changes pack sizes, service levels and promotion mechanics, which all land on supply chain teams.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "As an operations manager, how would you work with marketing?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Two or three concrete interface points (promotions, SKUs, service promises)
> - A process answer: S&OP, promo calendars, SKU rationalisation
> - Shared KPIs (fill rate, forecast bias, cost-to-serve)

## 2. STP: Segmentation, Targeting and Positioning
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Segment bases; attractive and reachable segments; positioning statement; perceptual maps

### Definition
- **Segmentation bases:** demographic (age, income, life stage), geographic (urban/rural, region, town class), psychographic (lifestyle, values), behavioural (usage rate, occasion, benefits sought, loyalty). B2B: industry, size, purchase process.
- **Good segments are MASDA:** measurable, accessible, substantial, differentiable, actionable.
- **Targeting:** undifferentiated, differentiated, concentrated (niche), micro/personalised; evaluate segment size, growth, competition, fit with capabilities.
- **Positioning:** the distinct place in the target customer's mind. Statement: "For [target], [brand] is the [frame of reference] that [point of difference] because [reason to believe]." Perceptual maps plot brands on two attributes customers care about.

### Example
Positioning for an electric scooter in Tier-2 India: "For first-time two-wheeler buyers in Tier-2 cities who ride under 40 km a day, [Brand] is the scooter that costs less to run than any petrol two-wheeler, because its swappable battery network removes charging worries." Target segment: daily commuters (behavioural) in Tier-2 towns (geographic) with ₹40,000-80,000 monthly household income (demographic). Operations implication: battery-swap station density and service network matter as much as the vehicle ([[131 Automotive Supply Chain - JIT, Tiers & EVs]]).

### In the news
See news box. Rural and urban demand diverged in 2026, a live example of why segment-level tracking matters.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you segment the market for X?" in market-entry cases ([[027 Case Interview — Market Entry]]).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Two or three relevant bases, not all of them
> - Criteria for choosing the target
> - A crisp positioning statement

## 3. The Marketing Mix: 4Ps and 7Ps
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Product, Price, Place, Promotion; plus People, Process, Physical evidence for services; 4Cs customer view

### Definition
- **Product:** features, quality, design, brand, packaging, sizes, services, warranties; product line breadth and depth.
- **Price:** list price, discounts, credit terms, price architecture across packs (sub-topic 6).
- **Place:** channels, coverage, logistics, inventory, outlet types (sub-topic 7).
- **Promotion:** advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, PR, digital, trade marketing (IMC: integrated marketing communication).
- **Services add 3Ps:** **People** (staff), **Process** (service delivery, [[151 Service Operations Management]]), **Physical evidence** (store, app, uniforms).
- **4Cs (customer view):** customer solution, cost, convenience, communication.

### Example
Mix for a ₹10 sachet shampoo vs a ₹450 premium bottle from the same company: product (basic vs premium formulation), price (₹10 price point vs value-based), place (rural general trade and kirana vs modern trade, e-commerce and salons), promotion (mass TV and wall paintings vs influencer and digital). Operations differences: sachet lines run at high speed with low unit cost; the premium SKU has lower volume, more variants and higher service expectations.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Design the marketing mix for launching X in India."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Mix consistent with the chosen segment and positioning
> - Specific Indian channel and price-point choices
> - Operational feasibility (cost, distribution reach)

## 4. Consumer Behaviour, the Funnel and the Customer Journey
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Decision process; involvement; AIDA/funnel metrics; diagnosing the leak

### Definition
- **Decision process:** need recognition → information search → evaluation of alternatives → purchase → post-purchase behaviour.
- **Involvement:** high (car, laptop: extensive search) vs low (biscuits: habit, availability, impulse).
- **Influences:** cultural, social (family, reference groups), personal, psychological (motivation, perception); in India also language, regional taste and value-consciousness.
- **Funnel:** awareness → consideration → trial → repeat → loyalty/advocacy (AIDA: attention, interest, desire, action). Diagnose where customers drop out.
- **Customer journey map:** touchpoints and pain points across stages, often used in PM and service design ([[033 Design Thinking & UX]]).

### Example
Funnel for a new ready-to-cook brand among 1,000 target households: awareness 60% (600), consideration 30% (180), trial 15% (90), repeat 4% (40). Trial-to-repeat is only 44% (40/90), so the problem is product or availability after trial, not advertising. Operations check: was the product in stock on repeat visits? Was shelf life an issue ([[133 Food, Agri & Perishables Supply Chain - India]])?

### In the news
See news box. Quick commerce compresses the funnel for low-involvement goods: availability on the app at the moment of need decides the sale.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Sales of our new product are below plan. Diagnose."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Funnel numbers to locate the leak
> - Hypotheses per stage (awareness, distribution, product, price)
> - Data to confirm and targeted fixes

## 5. Brands and Brand Equity
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Brand as promise; Keller's CBBE pyramid; Aaker's dimensions; architecture; extensions

### Definition
- **Brand equity:** the added value a brand gives a product (price premium, loyalty, distribution leverage).
- **Keller's CBBE pyramid:** salience (awareness) → performance and imagery → judgements and feelings → resonance (loyalty, community).
- **Aaker:** brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associations, brand loyalty (plus other proprietary assets).
- **Brand architecture:** branded house (one master brand, e.g., Tata across businesses), house of brands (many separate brands, e.g., HUL's portfolio), endorsed and hybrid models.
- **Extensions:** leverage equity into new categories; risk dilution if the fit is poor.
- **Private labels:** retailer and platform brands compete on price; growing in modern trade and e-commerce.

### Example
Price premium as an equity measure: if a branded atta sells at ₹62/kg and comparable private label at ₹52/kg with similar quality, the brand commands a ₹10 (19%) premium. If quick-commerce platforms push private labels, the brand must defend through availability (being on the shelf/app), innovation and promotions; the premium depends on salience at the moment of purchase.

### In the news
See news box. Commerce media's 24.2% projected growth means brands increasingly pay platforms for visibility at the point of sale.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Should brand X extend into category Y?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Fit (associations, quality perception, channel overlap)
> - Market attractiveness and competition
> - Risk to the core brand and operational capability to deliver

## 6. Pricing: Strategies, Elasticity and Promotion Break-even
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Cost-plus, competition-based, value-based; skimming vs penetration; price elasticity; volume needed to justify a discount

### Definition
- **Methods:** cost-plus (cost × (1 + markup)), competition-based, **value-based** (price to customer's perceived value; best for differentiated products).
- **New-product strategies:** **skimming** (high price, then lower; innovative products, low competition) vs **penetration** (low price for share; scale economies, network effects).
- **Tactics:** price points (₹10, ₹99), pack-price architecture (grammage changes, shrinkflation), bundling, dynamic pricing, psychological pricing.
- **Price elasticity of demand** $E = \%\Delta Q / \%\Delta P$; |E| > 1 elastic (revenue falls with price rise) ([[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]]).
- **Promotion break-even:** with contribution margin $m$ (% of price) and discount $d$ (% of price), volume must rise by $d/(m - d)$ to keep contribution constant.
More pricing cases in [[160 Case Interview - Cost Reduction, Turnaround & Pricing]].

### Example
1. Price ₹100, contribution margin 40%. A 10% discount cuts unit contribution from ₹40 to ₹30, so volume must rise **33.3%** (10/30) just to break even.
2. A 5% price increase causes an 8% volume fall: E = −1.6 (elastic); revenue changes by 1.05 × 0.92 − 1 = **−3.4%**. But if contribution margin is 30%, profit may still rise: old contribution 30 per unit × 100 units = 3,000; new (35 per unit) × 92 = 3,220, **+7.3%**. Price decisions should be judged on profit, not revenue.

### In the news
See news box. GST rate cuts from 22 Sep 2025 (5%, 18% and 40% slabs) forced FMCG and auto firms to reprice and change pack-price points ([[227 GST & Indirect Tax for Supply Chains]]).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Should we cut prices by 10% to regain share?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Break-even volume calculation
> - Elasticity and competitor reaction
> - Profit (contribution) focus and capacity check

## 7. Distribution and Channels in India: GT, MT, E-commerce, Quick Commerce
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Channel structure; numeric vs weighted distribution; channel economics; conflict

### Definition
- **General trade (GT):** company → (C&FA) → distributor/stockist → retailer (kirana, chemist) → consumer; still the majority of FMCG sales, especially rural ([[130 FMCG & Retail Distribution - India Route-to-Market]]).
- **Modern trade (MT):** supermarkets and hypermarkets, bought centrally.
- **E-commerce** (marketplaces, D2C sites) and **quick commerce** (10-30 minute delivery from dark stores).
- **B2B e-commerce** platforms supplying kiranas.
- **Distribution metrics:** **numeric distribution** = % of outlets stocking the brand; **weighted distribution** = % of category sales through outlets stocking it; out-of-stock rate; lines per bill; drop size.
- **Channel conflict:** price differences across channels, deep discounting online, distributor margins squeezed.

### Example
A brand is in 400 of 1,000 audited stores (numeric distribution **40%**), but those stores sell 65% of the category (weighted distribution **65%**): it is in the big stores but missing many small ones. To grow, add the high-potential missing outlets via distributor salesmen beat plans, not more advertising. Separately, if quick-commerce sells at 15% below GT prices, kirana owners complain and reduce stocking, a channel-conflict problem solved through pack differentiation (channel-specific SKUs) and pricing discipline.

### In the news
See news box. E-commerce volume grew 34.8% while rural traditional trade shrank in April-June 2026, accelerating these channel tensions.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> FMCG and consulting: "How would you increase distribution of brand X in Tier-3 towns?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Channel structure and economics (margins at each level)
> - Numeric vs weighted distribution logic
> - Cost-to-serve and route-to-market levers

## 8. Digital Marketing and Unit Metrics: CAC, LTV, ROAS
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Paid/owned/earned; performance metrics; LTV/CAC > 3 rule of thumb; break-even ROAS = 1 / gross margin

### Definition
- **Channels:** paid (search, social, marketplace ads, commerce media), owned (website, app, CRM, WhatsApp), earned (reviews, PR, influencers).
- **Metrics:** CTR (clicks/impressions), CPC, CPM, conversion rate, CAC (customer acquisition cost = acquisition spend / new customers), AOV, repeat rate, churn, **LTV** (lifetime value), **ROAS** (revenue / ad spend).
- **Simple LTV** = monthly contribution per customer × expected lifetime (1 / monthly churn).
- **Break-even ROAS** = 1 / gross margin %.
Metrics overlap with product analytics ([[031 Product Metrics & Analytics]]).

### Example
1. D2C snack brand: AOV ₹600, gross margin 25%, 2 orders a month, monthly retention 80%. Monthly contribution = 600 × 0.25 × 2 = ₹300; expected lifetime = 1/0.2 = 5 months; **LTV ≈ ₹1,500**. CAC ₹450 → LTV/CAC = **3.3** (healthy). If CAC rises to ₹700, LTV/CAC falls to 2.1: fix retention or cut paid acquisition.
2. Campaign spend ₹10 lakh generates ₹45 lakh revenue: ROAS = **4.5**. At 30% gross margin, gross profit = ₹13.5 lakh, net of spend **₹3.5 lakh**. Break-even ROAS = 1/0.30 = **3.33**.

### In the news
See news box. Digital is now 68% of India's ad market, so these metrics are standard in brand and category roles.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> PM, D2C and e-commerce interviews: "Is this marketing channel worth it?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - LTV and CAC with the arithmetic
> - Contribution, not revenue
> - Cohort retention as the hidden driver

## 9. Market Research: Surveys, Conjoint, NPS and Experiments
> 🟡 Tier 3 · _Key points:_ Qualitative vs quantitative; sampling and bias; conjoint for trade-offs; NPS; A/B tests

### Definition
- **Qualitative:** interviews, focus groups, ethnography (discover needs; [[164 Product Discovery & User Research]]).
- **Quantitative:** surveys with proper sampling ([[092 Sampling & Experimental Design]]), retail audits, panel data (NielsenIQ, Kantar).
- **Conjoint analysis:** respondents choose between product profiles; estimates the value of each attribute level (useful for pricing and feature trade-offs).
- **NPS (Net Promoter Score)** = % promoters (9-10) − % detractors (0-6) on "how likely are you to recommend?".
- **Experiments:** A/B tests, test markets ([[214 Causal Inference & Experimentation Beyond A-B Tests]]).
- **Biases:** leading questions, non-response, social desirability, "say vs do" gap.

### Example
NPS survey of 500 B2B customers: 210 promoters (42%), 190 passives (38%), 100 detractors (20%). NPS = 42 − 20 = **+22**. Drill-down: detractors cite late deliveries (55%) and invoice errors (25%), so the top NPS levers are operational ([[009 Logistics & Distribution]]).

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How would you find out whether customers will pay more for faster delivery?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Method matched to the question (conjoint/price test for willingness to pay)
> - Sampling and bias awareness
> - A decision rule from the results

## 10. B2B Marketing and Key Account Management
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Buying centre; rational and relationship-based; value selling; KAM; tenders

### Definition
B2B (industrial) marketing differs: few large customers, derived demand, **buying centre** (users, influencers, buyers, deciders, gatekeepers), formal procurement and tenders, long cycles, technical specifications, total cost of ownership. **Value selling** quantifies customer savings. **Key account management (KAM):** dedicated teams, joint business plans, service-level agreements, vendor-managed inventory, collaborative forecasting (CPFR). Many operations roles (sales and operations, customer supply chain, key account logistics) sit here ([[124 Outsourcing, Supplier Partnerships & Kraljic Strategies]], seen from the seller's side).

### Example
Selling a premium industrial lubricant at ₹480/litre vs a competitor's ₹350 to a cement plant: value-selling shows that drain intervals double (2 changes a year instead of 4) and unplanned gearbox downtime falls by 10 hours a year at ₹1.5 lakh per hour. Annual savings of ₹15 lakh in downtime plus lower consumption easily exceed the price premium on 4,000 litres (₹5.2 lakh). Pitch to the maintenance head and plant head, not only purchase.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Industrial and conglomerate interviews: "How would you win a key account from a cheaper competitor?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Buying-centre map
> - TCO / value calculation with numbers
> - Service and supply reliability as differentiators

## 11. Strategy Tools: Product Life Cycle, Ansoff and BCG
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ PLC stages and supply chain needs; growth vectors; portfolio roles

### Definition
- **Product life cycle (PLC):** introduction, growth, maturity, decline. Supply chain fit changes: responsive in introduction/growth, efficient in maturity, rationalisation in decline ([[112 Supply Chain Strategy - Fit, Segmentation & Maturity]]).
- **Ansoff matrix:** market penetration, market development, product development, diversification (risk rises in that order).
- **BCG matrix:** stars, cash cows, question marks, dogs by market growth and relative share ([[023 Business Fundamentals & Strategy]]).
- **Porter's generic strategies:** cost leadership, differentiation, focus.

### Example
A tea company: core packaged tea is a cash cow in maturity (efficient supply chain, cost focus); premium green tea is a question mark in growth (responsive, smaller batches); entering ready-to-drink beverages is product development on Ansoff with new cold-chain needs; selling packaged tea in the Middle East is market development (export documentation, [[126 International Trade Documentation, Customs & Trade Finance]]).

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How should company X grow?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Ansoff options with risk and capability fit
> - Portfolio roles and resource allocation
> - Operational implications of each path

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: Marketing Cases Operations Candidates Get
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
Three recurring case types for operations and general-management candidates, with skeletons:
1. **Declining sales:** market (is the category shrinking?) → share (are we losing to competitors?) → decompose sales = outlets × penetration × frequency × volume per purchase × price → channel and region cuts → root cause → fixes.
2. **Product launch:** market and segment → positioning and mix → go-to-market plan → **supply readiness** (capacity, packaging, distribution pipeline fill) → KPIs ([[036 Go-To-Market Strategy]]).
3. **Channel strategy:** should we go D2C or quick commerce? → channel economics, cannibalisation, channel conflict, operational requirements, capabilities.

### Example
"Sales of a biscuit brand fell 8% this year in Maharashtra." Decompose: category fell 3% (rural slowdown); brand share fell from 18% to 17% (a 5.6% relative fall; 0.97 × 17/18 ≈ 0.92, which accounts for the 8% drop); within the brand, GT down 15%, MT flat, e-commerce/q-com up 25%. GT drop concentrated in Tier-3 towns where a distributor exited and numeric distribution fell from 70% to 55%. Root cause: route-to-market gap, not product or advertising. Fix: appoint a new distributor, temporary van sales, and restore beats; monitor numeric distribution weekly.

### In the news
See news box. The April-June 2026 rural decline and online surge make this exact decomposition a likely 2026-27 interview case.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> FMCG GM programmes and consulting first rounds.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A sales decomposition tree before hypotheses
> - Market vs share vs channel separation
> - An operational root cause where data points there

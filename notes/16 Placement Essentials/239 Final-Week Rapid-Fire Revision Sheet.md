## 2. Supply Chain Management: 15 Rapid-Fire Answers
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Definitions with one-line operations meaning ([[143 SCM Interview Question Bank]])

### Definition
1. **Supply chain management:** planning and coordinating flows of material, information and money from suppliers to customers to meet service at lowest total cost. [[001 SCM Introduction & Fundamentals]]
2. **SCOR processes:** classic SCOR: Plan, Source, Make, Deliver, Return, Enable; the SCOR Digital Standard uses Orchestrate, Plan, Order, Source, Transform, Fulfill, Return. [[111 SCOR Model & Supply Chain Process Frameworks]]
3. **Efficient vs responsive supply chain (Fisher):** functional products → efficient; innovative products → responsive. [[112 Supply Chain Strategy - Fit, Segmentation & Maturity]]
4. **Bullwhip effect:** variability amplifies upstream; causes: forecast updating, batching, price promotions, shortage gaming; fixes: share POS data, smaller batches, EDLP, VMI. [[114 Bullwhip Effect, Beer Game & Information Sharing]]
5. **Kraljic matrix:** profit impact vs supply risk → leverage, strategic, bottleneck, non-critical. [[124 Outsourcing, Supplier Partnerships & Kraljic Strategies]]
6. **Incoterms 2020:** 11 terms; EXW (buyer bears most), FOB (risk passes when loaded on vessel), CIF (seller pays freight and insurance to destination port; risk passes at loading), DDP (seller bears most). [[009 Logistics & Distribution]]
7. **S&OP:** monthly cross-functional process to agree one demand-supply-finance plan; IBP extends it to financial and strategic integration. [[120 Integrated Business Planning (IBP) & S&OP Maturity]]
8. **MRP inputs:** MPS, BOM, inventory records; outputs: planned orders. [[005 Production & Operations Planning]]
9. **DDMRP:** decoupling points with buffers (red/yellow/green) sized by ADU, lead time and variability. [[117 Demand-Driven MRP (DDMRP) & Buffer Management]]
10. **Cross-docking:** goods move from inbound to outbound with little or no storage. [[010 Warehouse Management]]
11. **3PL vs 4PL:** 3PL executes logistics; 4PL integrates and manages multiple 3PLs. [[009 Logistics & Distribution]]
12. **Postponement:** delay differentiation until demand is known (e.g., late labelling). [[139 Product Design & Supply Chain - DFSC, Postponement & Complexity]]
13. **Cost-to-serve:** total cost of serving a customer/channel; reveals unprofitable customers. [[138 Order Management, Customer Service & Cost-to-Serve]]
14. **Supply chain resilience:** ability to absorb and recover from disruption: redundancy, flexibility, visibility, collaboration. [[015 Supply Chain Risk & Resilience]]
15. **PM Gati Shakti and National Logistics Policy:** integrated infrastructure planning platform and policy to cut logistics cost. [[145 India Manufacturing & Supply Chain Policy - PLI, Gati Shakti & NLP]]

### Example
Rapid chain: "Cash-to-cash cycle?" DIO + DSO − DPO. With DIO 45, DSO 30, DPO 40 → **35 days**; cutting DIO by 10 days on ₹2,000 crore COGS frees about 2,000 × 10/365 ≈ **₹55 crore** of working capital ([[136 Supply Chain Finance & Working Capital]]).

See news box. Disruption cases worth one line each are in [[141 Supply Chain Disruption Case Library (2011-2026)]].

## 3. Inventory and Planning Numericals
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ EOQ, safety stock, ROP, ABC, forecast error; know the drivers

### Definition
- EOQ = √(2DS/H); safety stock = Z × σ_d × √LT (demand uncertainty only); ROP = d̄ × LT + SS.
- Service level Z values: 90% = 1.28, 95% = 1.65, 98% = 2.05, 99% = 2.33.
- ABC: A ≈ 20% of items, 80% of value (Pareto).
- Forecast metrics: MAD, MAPE, WAPE, bias, tracking signal = cumulative error / MAD (alarm beyond ±4).
Notes: [[003 Inventory Management]], [[004 Demand Forecasting & Planning]], [[115 Advanced Inventory Policies - EPQ, Discounts & (s,S) Systems]].

### Example
D = 12,000 units/yr, S = ₹500/order, H = ₹30/unit/yr → EOQ = √(2 × 12,000 × 500 / 30) = √4,00,000 ≈ **632 units** (about 19 orders a year). Daily demand 300 (σ 40), lead time 9 days, 95% service: SS = 1.65 × 40 × 3 = **198**; ROP = 300 × 9 + 198 = **2,898 units**. Halving lead-time variability or lead time is usually cheaper than adding stock.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Compute EOQ and ROP", then "What happens to safety stock if lead time doubles?" (rises by √2, about 41%).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Clean arithmetic with units
> - Driver intuition (square-root effects)

## 4. Lean, Six Sigma and Quality
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Wastes, tools, DMAIC, capability ([[007 Lean Manufacturing]], [[008 Six Sigma & Quality Tools]], [[011 Quality Management (TQM)]])

### Definition
1. **8 wastes (TIMWOODS):** transport, inventory, motion, waiting, over-production, over-processing, defects, skills unused.
2. **5S:** sort, set in order, shine, standardise, sustain.
3. **Kaizen vs kaikaku:** continuous small improvements vs radical change.
4. **VSM:** map material and information flows; compute value-added ratio.
5. **Kanban:** pull signals limiting WIP; number of kanbans = D × LT × (1 + safety)/container size.
6. **SMED:** convert internal set-up steps to external to cut changeover time.
7. **Poka-yoke:** mistake-proofing.
8. **DMAIC:** Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control.
9. **Six Sigma:** 3.4 DPMO (with 1.5σ shift).
10. **Cp, Cpk:** spread vs spec; Cpk also checks centring; ≥ 1.33 capable.
11. **Control chart:** common vs special cause; X̄-R for variables, p/np/c/u for attributes. [[091 Statistical Quality Control (SQC)]]
12. **8D and 5 Whys:** structured problem solving for customer complaints. [[121 Supplier Quality & Automotive Core Tools (APQP, PPAP, 8D)]]

### Example
USL 10.5 mm, LSL 9.5 mm, mean 10.1, σ 0.1: Cp = 1.0/0.6 = **1.67**; Cpk = min(0.4, 0.6)/0.3 = **1.33**. Capable, but centring the process at 10.0 would raise Cpk to 1.67.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Difference between Lean and Six Sigma?" (waste/flow vs variation/defects; combined as Lean Six Sigma).

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - One-line distinctions and a shop-floor example
> - Numbers for capability or DPMO

## 5. Operations Management and Operations Research
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Capacity, OEE, Little's law, takt, queueing, LP ([[158 Operations Management Interview Question Bank & Numericals]])

### Definition
1. **Little's law:** WIP = throughput × flow time.
2. **Takt time** = available time / demand.
3. **Bottleneck:** the resource with the highest utilisation; it sets system output (TOC: identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, repeat).
4. **OEE** = availability × performance × quality; world-class ≈ 85%. [[018 Capacity Management & OEE]]
5. **Scheduling rules:** SPT minimises average flow time; EDD minimises maximum lateness; Johnson's rule for two machines. [[021 Scheduling & Sequencing]]
6. **M/M/1 queue:** ρ = λ/μ; Lq = ρ²/(1 − ρ); Wq = Lq/λ. [[149 Queueing Theory & Waiting-Line Analysis]]
7. **LP:** objective, decision variables, constraints; shadow price = value of one more unit of a constrained resource. [[146 Operations Research - Linear Programming]]
8. **Transportation problem:** minimise shipping cost from supply to demand nodes. [[147 Operations Research - Transportation, Assignment & Transshipment]]
9. **TPM pillars and MTBF/MTTR:** availability = MTBF/(MTBF + MTTR). [[022 Maintenance Management (TPM-RCM)]]
10. **Aggregate planning strategies:** chase, level, mixed. [[153 Aggregate Planning Models & Workforce Strategy]]

### Example
OEE: availability 90%, performance 95%, quality 98% → **83.8%**. Queue: trucks arrive at a weighbridge at 8/hour, service 10/hour: ρ = 0.8; Lq = 0.64/0.2 = **3.2 trucks**; Wq = 3.2/8 h = **24 minutes**. Raising service to 12/hour cuts ρ to 0.67 and Lq to about 1.3: small capacity gains near high utilisation give big waiting-time reductions.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Calculate OEE" or "Why does waiting time explode near 100% utilisation?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Formula, numbers, then the managerial insight

## 6. Project Management and EVM
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Triple constraint, CPM/PERT, EVM, agile ([[171 Project Management Interview Questions & Numericals]])

### Definition
1. **Project vs operations:** temporary and unique vs ongoing and repetitive. [[037 PM Fundamentals & Lifecycle]]
2. **PMBOK knowledge areas / performance domains.** [[038 PMBOK Knowledge Areas]]
3. **Critical path:** longest path; zero total float. [[039 Scheduling Tools (CPM-PERT-Gantt)]]
4. **PERT:** te = (O + 4M + P)/6; variance = ((P − O)/6)².
5. **Crashing:** shorten critical activities with the lowest cost per day first.
6. **EVM:** CV = EV − AC; SV = EV − PV; CPI = EV/AC; SPI = EV/PV; EAC = BAC/CPI.
7. **RACI:** Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed. [[040 Risk & Stakeholder Management]]
8. **Risk responses:** avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept (threats); exploit, enhance, share, accept (opportunities).
9. **Agile vs waterfall:** iterative delivery with feedback vs sequential phases; Scrum roles: PO, Scrum Master, team. [[041 Agile Project Management]]
10. **Contracts:** fixed price, cost-plus, time and materials; EPC/turnkey. [[168 Project Procurement, Contracts & EPC Delivery]]

### Example
BAC ₹100 lakh; PV ₹40 lakh, EV ₹35 lakh, AC ₹42 lakh. CPI = 35/42 = **0.83**; SPI = 35/40 = **0.875**; EAC = 100/0.833 = **₹120 lakh**; the project is over budget and behind schedule. PERT activity O = 4, M = 6, P = 14 days: te = **7 days**, variance **2.78**.

See news box.

### Interview angle

## 10. Statistics, ML and AI
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ The 12 most-asked items ([[215 Statistics Interview Question Bank & Numericals]], [[233 AI & ML Interview Question Bank for MBA Roles]])

### Definition
1. **Mean vs median:** median is robust to outliers (salaries, lead times).
2. **Standard deviation and coefficient of variation** (σ/μ) for comparing variability. [[086 Descriptive Statistics]]
3. **Normal distribution:** 68-95-99.7 rule. [[088 Probability Distributions]]
4. **p-value:** probability of data at least this extreme if H0 is true; not the probability H0 is true. [[089 Hypothesis Testing]]
5. **Type I vs II errors:** false positive vs false negative; power = 1 − β.
6. **Correlation ≠ causation;** use experiments or causal methods. [[214 Causal Inference & Experimentation Beyond A-B Tests]]
7. **R²:** share of variance explained; adjusted R² penalises extra variables. [[090 Regression Analysis]]
8. **Overfitting and validation;** time-aware splits for forecasting. [[094 ML Fundamentals & Workflow]]
9. **Precision vs recall;** threshold by cost of errors. [[096 Classification Algorithms]]
10. **LLM basics:** next-token prediction; hallucination; RAG. [[228 Generative AI & LLM Foundations for Managers]]
11. **Agents:** goal, tools, actions; reliability compounds (0.95¹⁰ ≈ 0.60). [[229 Agentic AI & Intelligent Automation in Operations]]
12. **Responsible AI:** bias, explainability, privacy (DPDP), human oversight. [[220 Responsible AI, Explainability & Model Governance]]

### Example
A model flags 80 of 1,000 machine-weeks; 40 of the 50 real failures are among them: precision 0.50, recall 0.80, accuracy 95% (same as "never fails"), so judge by recall and cost, not accuracy.

See news box.

### Interview angle

## 11. Finance and the Economy
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Statements, ratios, valuation, working capital, macro ([[108 Financial Statements & Ratios]], [[236 Economics Essentials - Micro, Macro & Policy for Interviews]])

### Definition
1. **Three statements:** P&L (performance), balance sheet (position), cash flow (cash movements); linked through retained earnings and cash.
2. **EBITDA:** operating profit before depreciation and amortisation; proxy for operating cash generation.
3. **ROCE** = EBIT / capital employed; key for asset-heavy operations.
4. **Working capital** = current assets − current liabilities; C2C cycle measures it in days.
5. **NPV, IRR, payback;** NPV is the decision rule. [[109 Valuation Basics (NPV, IRR, DCF)]]
6. **WACC:** weighted cost of debt (after tax) and equity. [[226 Corporate Finance Essentials - Capital Structure & Cost of Capital]]
7. **Contribution margin and break-even.** [[110 Cost Accounting for Operations]]
8. **Variance analysis:** price vs usage (materials), rate vs efficiency (labour). [[225 Budgeting, Variance Analysis & Balanced Scorecard]]
9. **GST input tax credit:** tax paid on inputs offsets output tax. [[227 GST & Indirect Tax for Supply Chains]]
10. **Repo rate effects:** borrowing costs, demand for credit-financed goods, rupee. 
11. **Rupee depreciation:** helps exporters, hurts importers; inflationary.
12. **Fiscal deficit and capex:** FY27 target 4.3% of GDP; public capex ₹12.2 lakh crore.

### Example
A plant's EBIT is ₹180 crore on capital employed of ₹1,200 crore: ROCE = **15%**. If WACC is 12%, it creates value (EVA ≈ (15% − 12%) × 1,200 = ₹36 crore).

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Read this P&L", "How does inventory reduction affect ROCE?", "Impact of the rate hike on us?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Formula, direction of impact, a number
> - Link between operations decisions and financial metrics

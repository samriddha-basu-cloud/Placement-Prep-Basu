## 1. How Placements Work at Indian B-Schools
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Summers → PPO/PPI → laterals → finals; placement committee; clusters/days; shortlists; policies

### Definition
Typical cycle for a two-year MBA (details vary):
1. **Summer internship placements** (first year): 8-10 week internships; performance can lead to a **PPO** (pre-placement offer) or **PPI** (pre-placement interview).
2. **Lateral placements** (for experienced candidates) in some schools.
3. **Final placements** (second year, often November-February): companies come in **clusters/days/slots** ranked by role and compensation; students apply through the placement portal; companies shortlist on CV, grades and tests; processes include online tests, GDs, case interviews, technical/functional interviews and HR rounds.
4. **Policies:** once placed, students may be barred from further processes or allowed only "dream" applications above a compensation threshold; PPO holders may have restrictions; withdrawing after accepting is usually penalised.
5. **Roles of the placement committee (PlaceCom):** coordinate companies, schedules, slotting of students across parallel processes, and enforce conduct.

### Example
A Day-1 morning might have 6 companies with processes running in parallel rooms: a consulting firm with case rounds, an FMCG firm with a GD then interviews, an e-commerce firm with a case plus HR round, and two GCC/IT firms with functional interviews. A student shortlisted by three must be slotted by PlaceCom across rooms, and companies may make "holds" or same-day offers.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Not asked; knowing the system lets you plan applications, slots and offer decisions without panic.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Reading the placement policy document carefully
> - Understanding dream/one-offer rules before applying
> - Knowing how slotting and holds work at your institute

## 2. The Recruiting Funnel: Why Shortlists Decide Outcomes
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Applications → shortlists → final rounds → offers; probability of at least one offer; improve the weakest conversion

### Definition
Treat placements like a sales funnel ([[031 Product Metrics & Analytics]]): applications × shortlist rate × conversion to final rounds × offer rate. If each final round independently converts with probability $p$, the chance of at least one offer from $n$ final rounds is $1 - (1 - p)^n$. Implication: **getting into enough final rounds** matters as much as interview polish, so CV quality, OA scores and the breadth of the target list are levers.

### Example
- 8 final rounds at 25% each: P(at least one offer) = 1 − 0.75⁸ = **90%**.
- 4 final rounds at 25%: 1 − 0.75⁴ = **68%**.
- 8 final rounds at 35% (better interviewing): 1 − 0.65⁸ = **97%**.
Diagnosis by stage: low shortlist rate → CV, grades, OA ([[179 Resume, LinkedIn & Cold Outreach for Placements]], [[234 Aptitude & Online Assessment Prep - Quant, Logical, Verbal & DI]]); low final-round conversion → cases, technical depth, fit answers.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Not asked; but this logic should decide where your preparation hours go.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Track your own funnel numbers weekly
> - Fix the weakest stage first
> - Apply broadly enough within policy limits

## 3. Building and Tiering Your Target List
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Role × sector × company; fit score; dream/target/safe; avoid over-concentration

### Definition
Steps:
1. **Roles:** operations/SCM, consulting, product, project management, analytics, GM programmes ([[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]).
2. **Sectors:** from [[237 Recruiter Sector Primers - FMCG, Auto, E-commerce, Manufacturing, IT-GCC & Consulting]].
3. **Companies:** past recruiters at your campus (placement reports), alumni presence, new recruiters.
4. **Fit score** (1-5 each): role interest, skills match (SAP, analytics, domain), story fit (internship/work experience), shortlist likelihood, location/lifestyle.
5. **Tier:** dream (low probability, high desire), target (good fit), safe (high probability, acceptable).
6. **Balance:** do not prepare only for one sector; at least two role tracks with overlapping prep (e.g., operations + consulting; PM + analytics).

### Example
For a profile with a tyre-company internship, SAP MM exposure and an MIS project ([[049 STAR Stories — Leadership & Conflict]]): target track = operations/SCM in auto, FMCG and e-commerce; second track = operations consulting and Big 4 supply-chain advisory; dream = strategy consulting; safe = GCC procurement/supply-chain analytics and SAP functional roles. Prep overlaps: cases and guesstimates serve consulting and operations; SAP and Excel serve GCC and operations.

### In the news
See news box. Shifts in hiring (BFSI and FMCG up, IT services cautious) argue for a balanced list.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Which other companies/roles are you applying for?" (answer honestly but show coherence)

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A coherent theme across applications (e.g., operations problem-solving)
> - Why this company is high on your list
> - No disparagement of other options

## 4. The 90-Day Preparation Calendar
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Phases: foundations → practice → simulation → taper; daily blocks; weekly review

### Definition
Use the vault's [[Study Plan]] for content; this calendar layers practice and execution on top.

| Phase | Days to Day-1 | Focus | Daily blocks (typical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundations | T-90 to T-61 | core concepts (SCM, ops, PM, stats), CV final, story bank | 2 h concepts, 30 min OA, 30 min news |
| Practice | T-60 to T-31 | cases and guesstimates, technical question banks, mocks 3/week | 1.5 h cases, 1 h question banks, 30 min OA, 30 min news |
| Simulation | T-30 to T-8 | full mocks 5/week, company prep packs, GD practice | 2 h mocks, 1 h company packs, 30 min revision |
| Taper | T-7 to T-0 | rapid revision ([[239 Final-Week Rapid-Fire Revision Sheet]]), sleep, logistics | 1 h revision, light mocks, rest |

**Weekly review (Sunday, 30 minutes):** funnel numbers, mock scores by dimension, error log, next week's priorities.

### Example
Week T-45 plan: Mon-Fri 2 cases a day with a partner (profitability, operations), 30 SCM questions from [[143 SCM Interview Question Bank]], 1 OA mock on Wednesday, 2 company packs (FMCG, e-commerce); Saturday full mock with a senior; Sunday review: structure scores improving (3.2 → 3.7), maths errors persist → add 15 minutes of mental maths daily.

See news box. Reserve 20 minutes a day for news; macro facts change fast (the RBI moved rates in October 2026).

### Interview angle

> [!tip] Strong answer includes

## 5. Running a Mock-Interview System
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Prep groups of 3-4; rubric; rotate roles; record and review; seniors and alumni for realism

### Definition
- **Group:** 3-4 people with overlapping targets; rotate interviewer, candidate, observer.
- **Rubric (1-5):** structure, content/technical depth, numerical accuracy, communication and presence, business judgement, fit stories. Observer notes specific moments.
- **Formats:** case mocks, technical rapid-fire (SCM, SAP, Excel, SQL), behavioural/HR, GD (5-8 people, [[177 Group Discussions & Extempore for MBA Placements]]), stress interviews.
- **Tracker:** date, type, interviewer, scores, top feedback, action; review weekly.
- **AI sparring** for volume, humans for realism ([[232 AI Productivity Toolkit for MBA Work - Excel, SQL, Decks & Research]], sub-topic 8).

### Example
Tracker extract:

| Date | Type | Structure | Maths | Comms | Judgement | Key feedback | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 02 Nov | Ops case | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 | OEE formula slip; rushed synthesis | revise [[018 Capacity Management & OEE]]; 30-sec synthesis drill |
| 06 Nov | HR | – | – | 4 | 4 | TMAY 2.5 min too long | cut TMAY to 90 s ([[048 Tell Me About Yourself]]) |
| 09 Nov | Ops case | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | good; add a risk at the end | add "risks & next steps" to closing |

> [!tip] Strong answer includes

## 6. Company Prep Packs and Pre-Placement Talks
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ One-pager per company; PPT notes; alumni calls; role-specific question bank

### Definition
**Prep pack contents** (group members split companies, then share): the one-pager template from [[237 Recruiter Sector Primers - FMCG, Auto, E-commerce, Manufacturing, IT-GCC & Consulting]] (sub-topic 1); interview experiences from seniors (rounds, question types, case themes); role description; alumni contacts; 3 questions to ask; "Why this company" answer ([[050 Why Consulting - PM - Operations-]]).

**Pre-placement talks (PPTs):** attend; note the role structure, priorities named by speakers, values language; ask one specific question (it is often noticed); use these points in interviews ("In your PPT, Mr X mentioned the quick-commerce supply chain build-out…").

### Example
FMCG prep pack bullets: "Rounds last year: OA (quant + logical), GD on rural demand, functional interview (inventory, forecasting, S&OP), HR. Cases: reduce stock-outs in modern trade; plan for a festive promo. PPT highlights: new plant commissioning in 2027; quick-commerce channel growth; digital twin in planning. My angle: CEAT-type planning experience; MIS automation; SAP MM/PP."

See news box.

### Interview angle

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Specific references (PPT, annual report, alumni conversations)

> - Personal connection to the company's priorities

## 7. Applications and Shortlisting Strategy
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Role-tailored CV versions where allowed; cover notes; OA readiness; meeting deadlines; policy limits

### Definition
- **CV versions:** some schools allow role-specific CV variants (operations, consulting, product); tailor the top third (summary, key achievements order) ([[179 Resume, LinkedIn & Cold Outreach for Placements]], sub-topic 4).
- **Application forms:** some ask short essays ("why this role" in 150 words); prepare reusable but specific drafts.
- **OA readiness:** keep a calm setup (stable internet, quiet room, permitted calculator) ([[234 Aptitude & Online Assessment Prep - Quant, Logical, Verbal & DI]]).
- **Deadlines:** a missed portal deadline is a lost funnel entry; set calendar alerts.
- **Policy limits:** application caps or dream-only rules after an offer; plan the order.

### Example
Application tracker columns: company, role, cluster/day, deadline, CV version, essay done, OA date, shortlist (Y/N), rounds, outcome, notes. Reviewing it weekly reveals, for instance, that consulting shortlists are low (2 of 9) while operations shortlists are high (7 of 10), so you rebalance prep time towards operations depth while keeping case practice.

See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Indirectly through shortlists and CV-based questions.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Consistency between CV, essays and interview answers
> - Every CV line defensible in depth

## 8. Day-of Execution: Slots, Parallel Processes and Holds
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Communicate with PlaceCom; prioritise by preference and stage; energy management; professional conduct

### Definition
- **Before the day:** know your preference order among companies where you are shortlisted; share it with PlaceCom if your institute uses it for slotting.
- **During the day:** stay reachable; inform the PlaceCom coordinator before leaving a room; if two final rounds clash, ask for slot adjustment early, never walk out without informing.
- **Holds and offers:** a company may put you "on hold" or make an offer with a short acceptance window; understand your institute's rules on accepting, and whether accepting ends your participation.
- **Energy:** eat, hydrate, keep a short warm-up routine between rounds (sub-topic 12).
- **Conduct:** be courteous to everyone (coordinators, other candidates, support staff); recruiters notice.

### Example
At 11 a.m. you are in round 2 of company A (your second preference) and shortlisted for the final round of company B (first preference) at 11:30. Action: inform the PlaceCom coordinator immediately; they negotiate with B for a later slot or with A to finish your round. Do not skip either process unilaterally.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Sometimes: "Do you have other offers or processes today?" Answer honestly and professionally, aligned with your institute's guidance.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Honesty about your situation
> - Clear statement of interest in the company if genuine
> - No bluffing about offers

## 9. Virtual, Recorded and Assessment-Centre Formats
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Camera, audio, lighting; recorded one-way video; group exercises; presentation tasks

### Definition
- **Live video interviews:** camera at eye level, face lit from the front, quiet room, wired or stable internet, headset, plain background, notes not visible; look at the camera when making key points; pause slightly to allow for lag.
- **Recorded one-way interviews:** fixed preparation and answer time (e.g., 30 s prep, 90 s answer); practise to time; structure (STAR, point-first).
- **Assessment centres:** group exercise (case discussion with role assignments), in-tray/email prioritisation exercise, presentation of a mini-case, sometimes role plays (difficult customer or supplier).
- **Case presentations:** 20-40 minutes to prepare 2-4 slides; use SCQA and action titles ([[162 Structured Communication - SCQA, Storylines & Case Delivery]]).

### Example
In-tray exercise: 12 emails in 30 minutes. Priority logic: safety/compliance first (a quality hold on a batch), then customer-impacting issues (key account shortage), then time-bound commitments (supplier contract expiring today), then routine items delegated or scheduled. Write brief, polite replies with clear actions and owners ([[040 Risk & Stakeholder Management]]).

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> As formats rather than questions.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Technical setup tested a day before
> - Timed practice for recorded answers
> - Clear prioritisation logic in in-tray tasks

## 10. Handling Rejection, Stress and Momentum
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Key points:_ Rejection is probabilistic; debrief, don't ruminate; routines; support; perspective

### Definition
With 25-35% conversion even for strong candidates, rejections are normal (sub-topic 2). Practices:
- **Debrief within an hour** (sub-topic 12), extract one lesson, then let it go.
- **Routines:** sleep, food, short exercise, fixed prep blocks; avoid all-night cramming before interview days.
- **Comparison traps:** peers' offers are not your timeline; focus on your funnel.
- **Support:** prep group, mentors, family, campus counsellors; ask for help early if stress affects sleep or eating.
- **Perspective:** the first job is a start, not a destiny; many careers change direction within three years.

### Example
After two final-round rejections on Day 1, a student debriefs: both interviews went wrong on the "Why this company?" answer (generic). Fix that evening: rewrite the answer for the next three companies using PPT notes and annual-report facts. Next day: two offers. The lesson was specific and fixable.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Tell me about a failure" or "How do you handle setbacks?" ([[052 Strengths, Weaknesses, Failures]])

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - A real setback with a specific lesson
> - The action you took and the result
> - Calm, reflective tone

## 11. Offer Decisions and Placement Policies
> 🟠 Tier 2 · _Key points:_ Decision criteria beyond CTC; fixed vs variable; role and learning; location; policies on acceptance and reneging

### Definition
Evaluate offers on: role content and learning, manager and team, company trajectory, fixed vs variable pay and in-hand salary, location and cost of living, bond/clawback clauses, growth path and exit options. Use a weighted decision matrix ([[150 Decision Analysis & Simulation]]). Details on CTC structure and negotiation in [[178 HR Round, Offers & Salary Negotiation in India]]. Respect institute policies: accepting and then reneging damages the institute's relationship with the recruiter and future batches, and is often penalised.

### Example
Offers: A (FMCG supply chain, ₹22 lakh CTC with ₹18 lakh fixed, Mumbai) vs B (GCC procurement analytics, ₹24 lakh CTC with ₹21 lakh fixed, Bengaluru). Weights: role fit 35%, learning 25%, fixed pay 20%, location 10%, brand 10%. Scores (1-5): A = 5, 5, 3, 3, 5 → 0.35×5 + 0.25×5 + 0.2×3 + 0.1×3 + 0.1×5 = **4.40**; B = 3, 3, 4, 4, 3 → 1.05 + 0.75 + 0.8 + 0.4 + 0.3 = **3.30**. A wins for an operations career despite lower CTC.

### In the news
See news box.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> HR rounds: "If you get offers from us and X, which will you choose?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Genuine reasons this role fits your goals
> - Professional honesty without playing companies against each other on campus

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: Night-Before Checklist, 30-Minute Warm-up and Post-Interview Debrief
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
**Night before:** documents (CV copies, ID, mark sheets as required), formal clothes ready, company one-pagers printed or on phone, charge devices, check slot schedule, 30 minutes of light revision only ([[239 Final-Week Rapid-Fire Revision Sheet]]), sleep 7+ hours.

**30-minute warm-up before an interview:**
1. 5 min: read the company one-pager (three numbers, two challenges, one question).
2. 10 min: say TMAY and "Why this company" aloud once each.
3. 10 min: one quick guesstimate or case opening aloud; 10 mental-maths questions.
4. 5 min: breathing, posture, water.

**Post-interview debrief (10 minutes, same day):** questions asked; what went well; what went wrong; one fix; share with your prep group (helps peers interviewing later with the same company, within institute norms).

### Example
Debrief log: "Company: X (e-commerce ops). R1: guesstimate (daily orders of a dark store) – OK; case: reduce delivery time – missed batching lever. R2: SQL question on window functions – solved; HR: relocation – fine. Fix: add order batching and rider utilisation to the last-mile lever list ([[129 E-commerce & Quick-Commerce Fulfilment]])."

See news box.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes

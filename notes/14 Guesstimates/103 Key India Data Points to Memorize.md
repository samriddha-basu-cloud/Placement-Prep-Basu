## 1. Demographics
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ Pop: 1.4B; Urban: 500M; Rural: 900M; Households: ~300M; Avg family: 4.5 persons

### Definition
Core anchors for any guesstimate:

| Item | Memorise | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Population | 1.4B (UNFPA 2025: 1.46B) | |
| Urban / rural | ~500M / ~900M (35% / 65%) | 500 + 900 = 1,400 |
| Households | ~300M | 1.4B / 4.5 = 311M |
| Avg household size | ~4.5 | |
| Sex ratio | roughly 940–1,000 women per 1,000 men (varies by source) | state as approximate |

Use: households as the base for products bought per household (TV, LPG, fridge); persons for personal products (phones, apparel); a city of 1 crore ≈ 22 lakh households. Say "about 1.4B" in the interview; mention that UNFPA estimates 1.46B if the interviewer wants the latest.

Caveat: India's last full Census was 2011 (the next was delayed), so all current figures are estimates or projections.

### Example
How many households in a 1 crore city? 1 crore / 4.5 = **22 lakh**. If 60% own a fridge: 13.2 lakh fridges.

### In the news
See news box. UNFPA 2025 puts the population at 146.39 crore; the commonly used 1.4B anchor is within about 4%.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "What is India's population and how many households?" as the opening step of a market-size case.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Rounded anchors with household size logic
> - Urban/rural split
> - Consistency check (people = households × size)
> - Honest note that figures are estimates

## 2. Age Distribution
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ 0-14: 26% (364M); 15-64: 67% (938M); 65+: 7% (98M); Median age: 28 years

### Definition
Age buckets drive product demand: children (schools, toys, baby food), working age (jobs, loans, mobiles), seniors (healthcare, pensions).

| Bucket | Tracker | UNFPA 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| 0–14 | 26% (364M) | **24%** |
| 15–64 | 67% (938M) | **68%** |
| 65+ | 7% (98M) | **7%** |
| Median age | 28 | **28.2** |

Arithmetic: 26% × 1.4B = 364M; 67% = 938M; 7% = 98M (sums to 1,400M). With 24/68/7 (sums to 99% because of rounding): 0–14 ≈ 336M, 15–64 ≈ 952M, 65+ ≈ 98M. Use the newer split and say "about a quarter, two-thirds, one-fifteenth".

**Demographic dividend:** a large working-age share lowers the dependency ratio $=\frac{(0\text{–}14)+(65+)}{15\text{–}64}$ ≈ 31/68 ≈ 0.46. The dividend narrows as fertility falls.

### Example
How many children are in elementary school (ages 6–14)? The 0–14 group is 24% ≈ 336M, so each single year of age holds about 336/15 ≈ **22M** children; ages 6–14 are 9 years, so 9 × 22M ≈ **~200M**.

### In the news
See news box. The UNFPA figures (24/68/7, median 28.2) show a youth bulge slowly maturing; the ageing 65+ share will rise, so healthcare and pension products grow.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many school-age children are there in India?" or "What age group would you target for this product?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Buckets and approximate percentages
> - Per-year-of-age shortcut (about 20–22M per cohort)
> - Link between age mix and demand
> - Mention of dependency ratio and ageing

## 3. Income Segments
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ Rich (>₹10L): 5%; Middle class (₹3-10L): 25%; Lower middle (₹1-3L): 35%; Poor: 35%

### Definition
A simple 4-tier household income ladder (annual household income, rough shares; treat as an interview heuristic, not official data):

| Segment | Household income | Share | Households (of ~300M) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affluent | above ₹10 lakh | 5% | ~15M |
| Middle class | ₹3–10 lakh | 25% | ~75M |
| Lower middle | ₹1–3 lakh | 35% | ~105M |
| Low income | below ₹1 lakh | 35% | ~105M |

The shares sum to 100%. Always state **household vs individual** income. Official classification differs across sources (e.g., consumer-research bodies define "middle class" and "affluent" differently), so quote your own definition explicitly. Use the ladder to compute **addressable market**: a ₹50,000 phone targets the top two segments (about 90M households), a ₹500 packet of detergent targets almost everyone.

Per capita income is lower than household income divided by size would suggest in rich households; use segments for affordability tests (e.g., product price < 5–10% of household annual income for durable goods).

### Example
A ₹1.5 lakh product with the rule "price ≤ 10% of annual household income" needs income ≥ ₹15 lakh, so addressable households ≈ a subset of the 5% affluent: about 15M × share above ₹15 lakh (say 50%) = **~7.5M households**.

### In the news
See news box. Income is not in the three sourced items; use UPI's ₹1,300 average ticket and 886M internet users to judge how far down the ladder digital products already reach (internet is ahead of income in India).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many Indians can afford a ₹X product?" or "Who is your target segment?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Household income definition and ladder
> - Affordability rule of thumb
> - Segment sizes in households
> - Caveat that shares are approximate

## 4. Smartphone Users
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ ~700M smartphone users; ~400M active internet users; ~200M active app users

### Definition
Funnel from population to digital action:

| Stage | Tracker figure | Updated source |
|---|---|---|
| Internet users | ~400M "active" (old) | **886M in 2024** (IAMAI-Kantar), 900M+ in 2025 |
| Smartphone users | ~700M | roughly 700M+ is in the plausible range; treat as approximate and verify |
| Heavy app/transaction users | ~200M | UPI alone had 66 crore daily transactions in 2025 |

So the tracker's 400M "active internet" is out of date; use **about 900M internet users, of which 55% are rural**. Use the funnel: population 1.4B → smartphone ~700M+ → internet ~900M (includes shared devices and feature phones) → paying users. The smartphone and internet figures overlap but are not identical because of definitions (any internet use vs own smartphone), so state the definition.

Use for: digital product market sizes (apps, ads, UPI, OTT).

### Example
App for working professionals: 900M internet users × 35% urban = ~315M; × 45% aged 18–40 in this group ≈ 140M; × 20% interested = **~28M** target users.

### In the news
See news box. IAMAI: 886M users, 488M rural, women 47%, 98% use regional-language content; the product lesson is vernacular and rural-first design.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many people could use our app?" or "How many UPI users are there?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Funnel with clear definitions
> - Use updated 886M internet figure and note rural share
> - Distinguish ownership, usage and paying
> - Regional language and low-end device implications

## 5. Urban Cities
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ Tier 1 (Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune): ~100M people

### Definition
City tiers are a heuristic segmentation. The tracker's "Tier 1 ≈ 100M people" covers six metros (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune); also commonly added Kolkata and Ahmedabad in the "top 8" list. Rough interview anchors (urban-agglomeration populations, approximate and varying by source):

| Segment | Examples | People (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Mega metros | Delhi, Mumbai | ~2 crore each |
| Other metros | Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Pune, Ahmedabad | ~0.7–1.3 crore each |
| Tier 2 | Jaipur, Lucknow, Indore, Nagpur... | ~20–40 lakh each |
| Tier 3 and below | district towns | below ~10 lakh |

Check: 6 metros × ~1.0–1.7 crore average ≈ 60–100M, so "~100M" is an upper-end round figure; use "about 7–10 crore" and say it is approximate. Urban India total ≈ 500M, so Tier 1 is roughly one-fifth of it, and much of the premium/consumption market.

Why it matters: premium goods, quick commerce and ride-hailing launch in Tier 1 first; Tier 2+ drive volume growth.

### Example
Quick-commerce dark-store sizing: a Tier 1 city of 1.2 crore people ≈ 27 lakh households; one dark store serves about 2–3 km radius (~15,000–25,000 households assumption) → **~110–180 stores** needed for full coverage.

### In the news
See news box. IAMAI: rural India holds 55% of internet users, so Tier 2 and rural markets are no longer an afterthought for digital firms.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "In which cities would you launch first?" or "How many people live in Tier 1 cities?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - City-tier definition and approximate populations
> - Criteria for launch order (density, income, logistics)
> - Expansion path to Tier 2/3
> - State the 100M figure as approximate

## 6. Vehicle Ownership
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ ~250M two-wheelers; ~35M cars; ~8M commercial vehicles; ~3M tractors

### Definition
Registered-vehicle stock anchors (rounded and unverified, from the tracker): two-wheelers ~250M, cars ~35M, commercial vehicles ~8M, tractors ~3M. Total ≈ 296M, consistent with the "300M vehicles" used in the petrol-pump guesstimate in [[104 Classic Guesstimate Types & Templates]].

Sanity: 250M two-wheelers / 300M households ≈ 0.83 per household; 35M cars / 300M households ≈ 12% of households have a car.

Registered stock includes scrapped or inactive vehicles, so "on-road" is lower. Use **stock ÷ life** to get annual sales; e.g., 250M two-wheelers over ~12 years of life ≈ 21M replacement demand (annual sales are lower because stock includes older vehicles still running; verify against SIAM data before quoting).

Applications: fuel demand, petrol pumps, EV adoption, insurance, tyres, parking.

### Example
Two-wheeler fuel demand (illustrative assumptions): 250M × 25 km/day = 6,250M km/day; at 45 km per litre = ~139M litres/day if all ride daily. At 50% daily usage = **~70M litres/day**.

### In the news
See news box. No specific 2024–26 vehicle statistic was verified for this note; use UPI-scale digital payments at fuel stations as the typical context only if you have a source.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many cars are there in India?" or as the base of petrol pump, EV charger or tyre-demand questions.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Segment mix: two-wheelers dominate
> - Stock vs annual sales logic
> - Per-household ownership sanity check
> - Admit approximate and offer a range

## 7. Education
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ Literacy rate 77%; graduates ~300M; college enrollment ~35M annually

### Definition
Anchors from the tracker (rounded, unverified here):
- **Literacy rate ~77%** (older Census 2011: 74%; newer survey-based estimates are higher). Quote as "about three-quarters to 80%".
- **Graduates** ~300M is high; use it carefully because the figure depends on definition (see sanity check below).
- **College enrolment** ~35M students at any time (tracker says "annually").

Sanity check on the graduate figure: the 15–64 population is ~950M, so 300M graduates would be 31% of it, which seems high for India; treat it as an upper-end/unverified number, and prefer **"about 35–40M in higher education at a time"** (that matches the 35M figure when read as total enrolment, not a yearly intake).

Annual intake rough logic: 35M enrolment / 3–4 year average course = **~9–12M new entrants a year**.

Use: edtech sizing, skilling, graduate hiring, coaching.

### Example
Students in Class 1–12: school age 6–17 ≈ 12 cohorts × ~22M = **~265M**, consistent with the "250M K-12" used in the edtech guesstimate.

### In the news
See news box. UNFPA's age structure (24% aged 0–14, 68% aged 15–64) frames the education pipeline; no specific verified education statistic was fetched for this note.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many college students are there in India?" or "Size the market for exam coaching."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Cohort approach (about 20–22M per age year)
> - Distinguishing enrolment (stock) from intake (flow)
> - Realistic caveats on graduates
> - Link to willingness to pay by income segment

## 8. Consumption Data
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ ₹200-300 daily food spend (urban avg); ₹50-100 (rural); FMCG ₹2L crore market

### Definition
Interview anchors (rounded; the tracker's own figures, not verified here):
- Urban daily food spend about ₹200–300 per person; rural about ₹50–100.
- FMCG market about ₹2 lakh crore (tracker), but published estimates vary by definition and are generally larger; present it as "a few lakh crore".

Cross-checks:
- Food spend (1 lakh crore = 10¹² rupees): 500M urban × ₹250 × 365 = 4.56 × 10¹³ = **₹45.6 lakh crore**; 900M rural × ₹75 × 365 = 2.46 × 10¹³ = **₹24.6 lakh crore**; total ≈ ₹70 lakh crore. That is far above the tracker's ₹2 lakh crore "FMCG" figure because total food spend includes fresh, unbranded and restaurant food; **branded FMCG is a subset**, and the ₹2 lakh crore figure looks low against published market estimates, so say "a few lakh crore" and flag that you would verify it.
- Per-capita check: ₹250/day × 365 = ₹91,000 per urban person per year, about 45% of the ~₹2 lakh per-capita GDP anchor. That is high, so treat ₹200–300 as an upper-end, perhaps restaurant-inclusive, urban figure and flag it.

Use shares: a household spends a larger share on food at low income (Engel's law).

### Example
Sizing milk spend (illustrative assumptions): 300M households × 1 litre/day × ₹55 = ₹16,500M = **₹1,650 crore/day**, about **₹6 lakh crore/year**.

### In the news
See news box. UPI average ticket of about ₹1,300 and 66 crore daily transactions show how much everyday spending has digitised; consumption values in this note are not from those sources.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How big is the packaged-food market in India?" or "How much does an urban family spend on groceries?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Urban/rural split with per-person or per-household spend
> - Cross-check against per-capita GDP and income shares
> - FMCG as a subset of total consumption
> - Candour about uncertainty

## 9. Digital Payments
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ UPI transactions: 10B+/month; ~700M UPI users; avg transaction ₹2,000

### Definition
UPI is the dominant retail digital payment rail. **Updated sourced numbers (PIB, FY2025-26):**
- **24,161.69 crore transactions = about 241 billion a year** (the tracker's "10B+/month" is outdated; the month of March 2026 hit **22.64 billion**, and the 2025 daily average was **66 crore = 660M**).
- **Value ₹314 lakh crore** a year; peak month ₹29.53 lakh crore.
- **Average ticket ≈ ₹1,300** (tracker's ₹2,000 is high).
- 703 banks live; UPI = ~49% of global real-time payment volume.

Arithmetic: 24,161.69 crore = 2.416 × 10¹¹ transactions; ₹314 lakh crore = 3.14 × 10¹⁴ rupees; ratio = ₹1,300. Monthly average: 24,161.69/12 ≈ 2,013 crore = **~20 billion**.

The "~700M UPI users" figure is unsourced here; the number of unique users differs by source, so say "several hundred million".

### Example
Check: 66 crore/day × ₹1,300 = ₹85,800 crore/day = ₹0.86 lakh crore/day; × 365 = **₹313 lakh crore**, matching PIB's ₹314 lakh crore.

### In the news
See news box. UPI went from 2 crore transactions in FY2016-17 to 24,161.69 crore in FY2025-26 (nearly 12,000×), PIB says.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "How many UPI transactions happen daily?" or "Size the UPI merchant-acquiring revenue pool."

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Updated figures: ~66 crore a day, ~₹1,300 average, ~20 billion a month
> - Per-day and per-year conversion without slips
> - MDR is zero for most UPI (revenue pools come from other sources); say you would check current policy
> - Link to fintech cases (lending, payments)

## 10. E-commerce
> 🔴 Tier 1 · _Tracker hint:_ ~250M online shoppers; $60-70B market; Flipkart + Amazon ~60% share

### Definition
Tracker anchors: ~250M online shoppers, a $60–70B market (GMV), and Flipkart plus Amazon at around 60% share. These are **not verified in this note**; treat them as approximate and label them so.

Sanity arithmetic:
- $65B at ₹85/$ ≈ ₹5.5 lakh crore; per shopper = ₹5.5 lakh crore / 250M = **₹22,000 per year** (~₹1,800 per month), plausible.
- Share check: 60% of ₹5.5 lakh crore ≈ ₹3.3 lakh crore combined GMV for the two leaders.
- Penetration: 250M shoppers / ~900M internet users (IAMAI 886M) ≈ **28%**, so large headroom.
- Orders per shopper, say 10 a year, gives 2.5B orders a year (~7M a day).

Include segments beyond marketplaces: quick commerce, food delivery, travel, grocery. Be careful to say whether market size is **GMV (gross merchandise value)** or revenue; platform revenue is a small fraction (take rate).

### Example
Marketplace revenue sizing: GMV ₹5.5 lakh crore × effective take rate 8% (assumption) = **₹44,000 crore** of platform revenue.

### In the news
See news box. With 886M internet users and 488M of them rural, the next e-commerce wave is likely to come from Tier 2+ and vernacular users (analytical reading of the IAMAI figures).

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> "Estimate the size of Indian e-commerce" or "How many parcels does India ship per day?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - GMV vs revenue distinction
> - Shoppers × orders × AOV method
> - Penetration vs internet users
> - Share and competition caveat

## 11. ⭐ Advanced: Building and Maintaining Your Own Data Sheet
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
Prep sheets age quickly (the tracker's UPI and internet numbers were already stale). Build a one-page **anchor sheet** with columns: *Metric, Value, Year, Source, Confidence (H/M/L)*. Refresh from:
- **Official:** Census/UNFPA, NITI Aayog, RBI, NPCI/PIB, MoSPI, SIAM, TRAI.
- **Industry:** IAMAI, company annual reports, quarterly results.

Rules: store the **round number you will speak** next to the exact source number; record the **ratio checks** (households × size ≈ population); and review monthly before interviews. Use ranges for uncertain items and keep a "what would I verify" flag.

### Example
| Metric | Spoken | Source number | Year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population | 1.4B | 146.39 crore (UNFPA) | 2025 |
| Internet users | ~900M | 886M (IAMAI-Kantar) | 2024 |
| UPI per day | ~65 crore | 66 crore (PIB) | 2025 |
| UPI avg ticket | ~₹1,300 | 314 lakh cr / 24,162 cr | FY26 |

### In the news
See news box. All four rows above come from the sourced news items.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Implicit: "Do you know current numbers?"

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Mention source and year when quoting
> - Round for speed, cite exact if asked
> - Flag low-confidence figures
> - Update habitually

## 12. ⭐ Advanced: Ratios and Conversions That Save Time
> ⭐ Advanced · _Added beyond the tracker_

### Definition
Memorise a few conversion shortcuts:
- **Per-capita:** 1% of 1.4B = 14M; 1 in 10 = 140M.
- **Households:** persons / 4.5; 1 crore people ≈ 22 lakh households.
- **Per-cohort:** ~20–22M people per year of age (about 1.4B / 65 years).
- **Time:** 365 days ≈ 52 weeks ≈ 12 months; working days ≈ 250; hours per year ≈ 8,760.
- **Money:** ₹1 lakh crore = ₹1 trillion = ~$12B at about ₹85/$ (state your FX assumption); 1 crore = 10M.
- **Usage:** 15% of 300M = 45M.

Dependency ratio, penetration and market-share identities help: $\text{Market}=\text{Users}\times\text{ARPU}$, $\text{ARPU}=\text{Revenue}/\text{Users}$.

### Example
UPI daily value ₹0.86 lakh crore ≈ ₹0.86 trillion ≈ **$10B per day** at ₹85/$. Per person per day (1.4B): ₹0.86 trillion / 1.4B ≈ ₹614.

### In the news
See news box. These conversions are how you move between PIB's "lakh crore" figures and global "billion dollar" language.

### Interview angle
> [!question] How it is asked
> Implicit in every numeric case.

> [!tip] Strong answer includes
> - Quick, error-free conversions
> - State the FX assumption
> - Clear units on every line
> - Sanity check after converting
